# Panel-harvest criteria

Failure modes harvested from full-panel persona reads of a complete manuscript —
twelve personas, one per seat, every flag verified at its cited line. These are the
findings that survived verification and that per-chapter linting cannot see. Check each
against the chapter under review.

## Narrator absolutes

Unfalsifiable universals doing characterization or worldbuilding shorthand: "no soldier
ever forgets the first time," "everyone in the city knew," "she would never..." —
asserted with no scene behind them, in the confident cadence of established fact. A
universal is a claim the book must be able to pay for; either anchor it (a scene, a
named instance) or soften it to what this narrator could actually know. Highest-risk
location: the load-bearing sentence of an emotional beat, where the absolute is doing
the work.

## Authority without scars (asserted, never shown)

Expertise, history, or institutional knowledge claimed without a single dramatized
instance: an institution named but never entered, a skill referenced but never
demonstrated, a backstory doing load-bearing work that exists only as assertion. The
reader cannot check it, and the story never makes them feel it. If a claim matters to
the chapter's turn, it needs one on-the-page instance; if it cannot get one, it is
texture and should be weighted like texture.

## The structural problem solved by individual conscience

A problem the setting has established as institutional — a law, a shortage, a chain of
command — resolved by one character's courage, conscience, or heartfelt speech to
someone who holds power the scene never verified. Readers with an eye for power find
this the most false thing in a book. Either the character has real leverage (shown
earlier), or the institution behaves the way institutions in this world actually
behave, or the resolution is partial and costs something.

## The quotable line is the risk spot

Errors concentrate in the chapter's most polished sentences — the stinger, the closing
button, the line built to be quoted. Fluency bypasses scrutiny: the smoother the line,
the less anyone checks it. Verify the chapter's best lines hardest: does the aphorism
actually hold against what happened on the page? Is the antithesis earned by the scene,
or does it snap shut over a hole?

## Gloss discipline fails at distance and under compression

Terms, coined phrases, and relationship shorthand read fine where they are defined and
cold four chapters later — and the failure concentrates in compressed passages:
asides, dialogue, action, one-line transitions, where the gloss habit blinks. Check
invented terms and author-coinages at each use after distance: is there a re-anchor, or
is the reader assumed to still be carrying the definition? A definition shipped once
and never re-anchored reads, chapters later, as never shipped.

## The idealized baseline

Before-times sentimentalized: the past era, the dead mentor, the lost home rendered
simpler, warmer, and more honest than any lived place could be, so the present feels
like a fall. Nostalgia is a legitimate effect when the narration knows it is
nostalgia; the tell is the baseline presented as fact. If a memory sequence contains no
friction, no cost, and no flaw, it is advertising, not memory.

## The sting and its repair

When the narration mocks a type of person, check who else is standing in the blast
radius — a POV character, an ally, or the reader themselves is often inside the
targeted type. The repair grammar works and is cheap: name the fence within a sentence
or two of the sting (the sympathetic instance, the aimed-away clause). Unrepaired
stings are where readers quit a book that has otherwise earned their trust. Flag any
mockery whose butt is also someone the book asked the reader to love.

## The parable arc

Stories-within-the-story (anecdotes, memories, war stories, parables a character
tells) that all resolve the same way — same moral shape, frictionless arc, thesis
delivered on schedule. One is a device; every one resolving identically is a template,
and readers stop trusting all of them. Vary the resolution: the lesson that fails, the
parable with no moral, the story that turns on the teller.
