## Jargon Density Criteria

Check every line in your assigned chunk against ALL of the following categories. Jargon is a clarity failure only when the chapter's reader (per the plan's audience assumptions) would not know the term. Terms of art for an expert audience are fine; the same term in a generalist chapter is a flag.

### Undefined Terms of Art
- Domain-specific term used before it is defined, doing argumentative work.
- Acronym used on first mention without expansion.
- Coined term or framework name used with no in-line definition at point of use.
- **Test:** Would a smart generalist reader know this term? If no, and it is not defined in the sentence or immediately adjacent, flag it.
- **Exception:** the term is defined at first use (in the sentence, parenthetical, or immediately preceding sentence).

### Jargon Stacking
- More than 2 domain-specific terms in one sentence.
- Two specialized frameworks named in the same paragraph without a bridging explanation.
- **Test:** Count the terms a generalist must pause on. 3+ in a sentence = flag.

### Banned Words (unless strictly technical)
Flag these unless the term is the chapter's defined technical vocabulary:
- utilize (use)
- facilitate (help, enable)
- leverage (use, apply)
- impact as a verb (affect)
- paradigm (framework, model — unless Kuhnian)
- holistic (comprehensive, integrated)
- granular (detailed, fine-grained)
- significant without a statistical referent (substantial, meaningful)
- robust (strong, reliable — specify what is robust about it)
- "it is important to note" constructions (state the point instead)

### Nominalized Jargon
- Verb phrases wearing noun clothing: "conduct an analysis of" (analyze), "make a determination" (determine), "give consideration to" (consider).
- **Test:** Is there a plain verb for this noun phrase? If yes, flag.

### Register Mixing
- One sentence contains both casual idiom and technical terminology, confusing the reader's footing.
- **Fix:** keep the technical term and drop the idiom — or vice versa; never both in one sentence.
