## Cognitive Load Criteria

Check every line in your assigned chunk against ALL of the following categories. The reader holds a small working memory: every sentence must be processable in one pass, every paragraph in one breath of attention.

### Overloaded Sentences
- Sentences over 25 words (count them; flag at 30+ unless the rhythm demands it).
- Sentences where the subject and main verb are separated by more than 6 words.
- Sentences requiring a second read: the re-read test — if the editor must re-read, flag.
- **Exception:** deliberate rhythmic length for emphasis; one long sentence in a paragraph of short ones.

### Nested Clause Pileups
- More than 2 levels of embedding: "The study [that the team [which Smith founded] ran] showed..."
- Parentheticals inside parentheticals.
- Interruptions that abandon the main clause for a full sentence before returning.
- **Fix:** split into two sentences; promote the buried clause to its own sentence.

### One-Idea Violations
- A sentence with two "and"s joining independent claims.
- Evidence and interpretation crammed into one sentence such that both are lost.
- **Exception:** deliberate parallelism for rhetorical effect.

### Paragraph Load
- Paragraphs over 150 words in argument prose.
- Paragraphs holding 2+ argument moves (sub-claim + new evidence + turn all at once).
- Walls of text: 3+ consecutive paragraphs with no signpost, break, or concrete anchor.
- **Fix:** one argument move per paragraph; split at the move boundary.

### Memory Burden
- Pronoun or reference chains longer than 2 hops before the referent is named again ("it... they... this... that" chain).
- Forward references: "as we will see", "which we return to below" — used more than once per chapter, or pointing further than the next section.
- **Fix:** name the noun again; cut the forward reference or shorten the distance.

### Unpacking Order
- The abstract stated before the concrete when the concrete is shorter (invert: concrete first).
- Jargon before definition (definition belongs at point of use, or a concrete instance comes first).
- **Test:** Can a first-time reader process the sentence left-to-right without backtracking?
