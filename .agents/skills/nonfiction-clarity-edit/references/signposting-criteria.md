## Signposting Criteria

Check every line in your assigned chunk against ALL of the following categories. Non-fiction readers must see the argument's architecture: every move needs a marker. Missing signposts force readers to reverse-engineer the logic.

### Missing Transitions
- Section or paragraph boundary with no logical connector: the new section opens with a topic, not a relationship to what came before.
- Transitions that mark sequence only ("Next", "Additionally", "Furthermore") without encoding the logical relation (causation, contrast, concession, synthesis, escalation).
- **Fix:** replace with the relation: "Because [prev], [next] follows." / "But [prev] assumes [X]." / "Together, [A] and [B] imply [C]."

### Topic Announcements
- Topic sentence that announces rather than argues: "This section discusses X" instead of "X fails because..."
- "In this chapter we will..." / "The next section shows..." constructions.
- **Test:** Does the first sentence state a claim the paragraph proves? If it only names a topic, flag.

### Author-Centered Framing
- "I will argue", "we discuss", "as mentioned above", "as discussed previously".
- Backward-looking references without a forward payoff.
- **Fix:** re-center on the argument: "Having established [X], the question becomes [Y]."

### Hidden Argument Moves
- A turn (reversal, complication, synthesis) executed with no marker: the reader cannot tell that belief should change.
- Counter-argument introduced without a steel-man signpost ("The strongest objection is...") or rebuttal without a pivot.
- Evidence presented with no immediate interpretation in the same paragraph.
- **Test:** At every point where the reader's understanding should change, is there a signpost saying so?

### Over-Signposting
- Signposting every sentence; mechanical "First... Second... Third..." scaffolding for 3+ items where the logic is already clear.
- Redundant roadmap restated in every section opening.
- **Fix:** signpost the real moves; cut the decorative ones. A signpost's cost must be less than the confusion it prevents.

### Roadmap Integrity
- A promised structure ("three moves: A, B, C") must match the actual structure. A roadmap that names 3 moves where 4 follow is a defect.
- **Test:** Count the promised moves; count the delivered moves; flag mismatches.
