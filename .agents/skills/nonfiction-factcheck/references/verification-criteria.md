## Verification Criteria

Check every assertion in your assigned range against ALL of the following categories. The draft's own text is never evidence. Only the research corpus and the sources it cites count.

### Statistics
- The number in the chapter matches the source number exactly (value, unit, direction, sign).
- The denominator is stated or recoverable: "50%" means 50% of what, of what total?
- The population and context match: a study of adults does not support a claim about children.
- Dates and time windows match: a 2019 study is not "recent" in a 2026 book; a 6-month effect is not a permanent one.
- Precision matches the source: "about 30%" must not become "31.4%".
- Effect size and uncertainty are represented: a significant p-value does not license "large effect" without the effect size.
- **Test:** Could a reader reproduce the source's number from the chapter's sentence alone?

### Quotes
- The wording is verbatim, including articles, prepositions, and tense. One altered word is a misquote.
- The attribution names the right person, in the right role, at the right time.
- The quote's source and location are real and correctly cited (work, year, page if the corpus records it).
- The surrounding context preserves the source's meaning: a real quote severed from its qualifier is a misquote of substance.
- The quote does the argumentative work claimed for it: if the chapter says the quote "proves" X, the quote must actually say X.
- **Test:** Read the source passage whole. Would its author recognize the chapter's use?

### Claims
- Every causal claim is supported by causal evidence in the corpus; a correlational source supports only a correlational claim.
- Every comparative claim ("more than", "faster", "higher") has both sides verified.
- Every superlative ("first", "only", "largest", "never", "all") is checked against the whole corpus, not one convenient case.
- Hedging matches evidence strength (see the calibrated-certainty table in the taxonomy).
- The claim does not outrun its citation: the source must say what the chapter claims, not merely sit adjacent to it.
- **Test:** Underline the claim. Does the cited passage entail it, or merely not contradict it?

### Proper Nouns
- Names of people, institutions, studies, places, and works match the corpus spelling, capitalization, and form exactly.
- Study names, dataset names, and model names are the official labels, not paraphrases.
- Titles of works are complete and correctly attributed.
- **Test:** Search the corpus for the exact string. If the corpus spells it differently, the corpus wins.

### Attributions
- "X found" requires X to be the author, or the finding to be X's own words; citing a reviewer summarizing X is misattribution.
- "According to X" requires the corpus to contain X saying it.
- Positions attributed to named people or schools ("Smith argues that...") must trace to that person's actual work.
- **Test:** Who is the original source? If the corpus only has a secondary citation, the chapter must say so or the finding stands.

### Cross-Chapter Consistency
- The same fact asserted in an earlier chapter must match here: same number, same name, same date, same defined term.
- A re-cited study must be cited the same way both times (author, year, finding).
- **Test:** If the chapter plan or continuity table records the fact, compare against it.
