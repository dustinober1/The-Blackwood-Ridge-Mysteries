# Anti-Flat Chapters: Diagnosing and Fixing Argument-Free Nonfiction

## The Flat Chapter Patterns

### 1. The Survey Chapter
**Symptom:** "In this chapter we'll look at X, Y, and Z."
**Diagnosis:** Topics listed, not argued. No claim drives the structure.
**Fix:** Pick the strongest insight across X, Y, Z. Make it the claim. Reorganize evidence to prove it.

### 2. The Literature Review Chapter
**Symptom:** "Smith (2019) argues... Jones (2020) counters... Lee (2021) adds..."
**Diagnosis:** Summarizing others' arguments instead of making your own.
**Fix:** Use sources as evidence for YOUR claim. Each citation must serve your argument.

### 3. The Background/Context Chapter
**Symptom:** "Before we understand X, we need the history of..."
**Diagnosis:** Context treated as prerequisite rather than evidence.
**Fix:** Cut context that doesn't serve the claim. Weave necessary context into evidence blocks.

### 4. The Definition Chapter
**Symptom:** "Let's define our terms: A means... B means... C means..."
**Diagnosis:** Definitions floating free of argumentative work.
**Fix:** Define terms at the moment they're needed for a specific claim.

### 5. The Process/How-To Chapter (in argument-driven books)
**Symptom:** "Step 1: ... Step 2: ... Step 3: ..."
**Diagnosis:** Procedure without stakes or principle.
**Fix:** Each step must demonstrate a principle or overcome an obstacle. The procedure IS the argument.

### 6. The "And Another Thing" Chapter
**Symptom:** Evidence blocks don't build. Each could be reordered without loss.
**Diagnosis:** Additive structure, not cumulative argument.
**Fix:** Each block must change what the reader believes, enabling the next block.

## Diagnostic Questions

For each evidence block, ask:
1. **What sub-claim does this prove?** (If none, cut or repurpose)
2. **What would the reader believe BEFORE this block?** (Must be specific)
3. **What does the reader believe AFTER?** (Must differ from before)
4. **What evidence TYPE is this?** (Must vary across blocks)
5. **What's the TURN?** (Insight, reversal, complication, synthesis)
6. **What does this enable NEXT?** (Forward pull)

## The Argument Spine Test

A chapter passes if you can answer all five in one sentence each:
1. **Claim:** _______________
2. **Stakes:** _______________
3. **Evidence strategy:** _______________
4. **Synthesis:** _______________
5. **Necessity:** _______________

If any answer is "it covers..." or "it explains..." — the chapter is flat.

## Rehabilitation Protocol

1. **Extract the buried claim** — Often hiding in the conclusion or introduction
2. **Audit evidence blocks** — Tag each with sub-claim, evidence type, turn
3. **Reorder for accumulation** — Each block must enable the next
4. **Add missing turns** — Where evidence just sits, insert insight/reversal
5. **Write the synthesis** — Not summary: new understanding created
6. **Cut orphan evidence** — If it doesn't serve the spine, it belongs elsewhere or nowhere
