# Chapter Hooks: Forward Pull for Nonfiction

## Hook Types

### 1. The Open Question
**Pattern:** "But this raises a problem: [specific tension]. Chapter [N+1] resolves it by [preview]."
**Works when:** Current chapter creates a genuine puzzle the next chapter solves.

### 2. The Escalating Stakes
**Pattern:** "We've seen [claim] at [scale]. But at [larger scale], the implications are [stark consequence]."
**Works when:** Argument naturally expands in scope.

### 3. The Counter-Intuitive Reversal
**Pattern:** "Everything so far suggests [X]. Yet [evidence type] reveals [not-X]."
**Works when:** Next chapter delivers a genuine surprise backed by evidence.

### 4. The Missing Piece
**Pattern:** "[Claim] explains [phenomenon A]. But it cannot explain [phenomenon B]. That requires [next chapter's framework]."
**Works when:** Current framework has a known, acknowledged boundary.

### 5. The Practical Imperative
**Pattern:** "Understanding [claim] changes how we [act/decide/design]. Chapter [N+1] shows exactly how."
**Works when:** Book has applied/practical dimension.

### 6. The Narrative Thread
**Pattern:** "[Case/person/historical figure] thought [X]. But in [next setting], they encounter [Y]."
**Works when:** Book uses sustained narrative/case study.

## Anti-Patterns (Don't Use)

- **Generic teaser:** "In the next chapter, we'll explore..." (No tension)
- **False cliffhanger:** "The answer may surprise you!" (Reader knows it won't)
- **Topic list:** "Next we'll look at X, Y, and Z" (Survey, not argument)
- **Author promise:** "I'll show you..." (Focus on reader's need, not author's plan)
- **Repetition:** Same hook type every chapter (Vary the mechanism)

## Hook Placement

Place the hook in the **Reader Takeaway** section of the chapter plan:
- **One-sentence takeaway:** The synthesized insight
- **Actionable implication:** What the reader can do/think differently
- **Forward hook:** The specific mechanism pulling to next chapter

## Hook Verification

Before finalizing a plan, test the hook:
1. Does it name a SPECIFIC tension/question/stakes?
2. Does the next chapter's plan actually resolve/address it?
3. Is it the READER'S pull, not the author's outline?
4. Does it vary from the previous chapter's hook type?
