## AIism Detection Criteria

Check every line in your assigned chunk against ALL of the following categories.

### Forbidden Words
Flag these words unless the exception applies:
- tapestry / tapestries
- testament
- sentinel
- verdant
- cerulean
- rhythm (ALLOWED for literal music only)
- cacophony
- symphony (ALLOWED for literal music only)
- whisper / whispers / whispering / whispered (ALLOWED for actual quiet speech only)
- palpable
- unmistakable
- undeniable
- visceral
- primal
- delve
- seamlessly
- unlock / unleash / elevate (metaphorical use only)
- journey (metaphorical use only)
- beacon (metaphorical use only - literal signal lights are fine)

Also check the review guide for any project-specific forbidden words.

### Forbidden Phrases
- "practiced precision" / "practiced ease" / "practiced [anything]"
- "a mix of [X] and [Y]" / "a mixture of" / "a combination of"
- "couldn't help but [verb]"
- "found himself [verbing]" / "found herself [verbing]"
- "something akin to"
- "a sense of"
- "the kind of [X] that [verb] more than [Y]"
- "the [noun] of someone who [clause]" (e.g., "with the composure of someone who had spent decades...")
- "all [adjective noun] and [adjective noun]" (e.g., "all sharp angles and quiet authority")
- "In today's ..." openers (e.g., "in today's rapidly evolving landscape")
- "delve into" / "navigate the [abstract noun] of"

### Paradiastole Patterns
These rhetorical inversions are an AI tell:
- "not [X] but [Y]" (e.g., "not anger but something colder")
- "it wasn't [X], it was [Y]"
- "less [X] than [Y]" used for emotional descriptions

### Over-Qualified Adverbs
Adverbs that add nothing:
- "entirely involuntary"
- "completely unnecessary"
- "absolutely certain"
- "perfectly still"
- "utterly silent"

Flag any adverb + adjective pair where the adverb just intensifies without adding meaning.

### Vague Abstract Descriptors
- "that brightness" / "that darkness" / "that [abstract noun]"
- "something like [emotion]"
- "a kind of [emotion]"
- "there was something about [person/thing] that..."
- "what could only be described as"
- Abstract emotional hedging: "almost like," "bordering on," "verging on"

### Forbidden Personification
Inanimate things (cities, buildings, rooms, weather, the world) must NOT be given human agency:
- "[Place] felt [emotion]"
- "[City/room] [verb implying speech or opinion]"
- "[Non-person] knew / understood / agreed / watched / decided"
- "[Place] seemed to [human action]"

**Test:** Replace the subject with a literal human. If the sentence only makes sense with a person, it's personification. Flag it.

**Exception:** POV-character ironic humor framing an inanimate thing (e.g., "Physics could file a complaint later") is borderline — flag it and note the exception.

### AI Construction Patterns
- Colon-announcement constructions: "One thing was clear:" / "The answer was simple:"
- Stacked abstract nouns: "grief and gratitude and something he couldn't name"
- Mirroring constructions: "Not the [X] of [Y], but the [X] of [Z]"
- Emotional math: "equal parts [X] and [Y]"
- The "beat of silence" / "loaded silence" / "heavy silence" cliche cluster

### Self-Announcing Narration (the docent)
The narration pointing at its own construction — telling the reader how well told the
telling is. Flag:
- "Little did she know" and its cousins (the narrator winking at withheld information)
- "It was, in retrospect, the moment everything changed" / "That was the last quiet morning" (when used as announcement rather than as the character's own retrospective thought)
- "To understand what happened next, you need to know..." (tour-guide staging)
- Lines that narrate the narration's craft: "The sentence landed like a verdict" when nothing in-scene lands anything
Rare legitimate use: an established intrusive narrator whose voice does this on every
page by contract. Check the voice rules before flagging a deliberate storyteller voice.

### Noun-Swapped Dies (within this chapter)
The same sentence template re-fired with fresh nouns — individually fine sentences
whose repetition is the tell: "The forge does not rest. The hammer does not forgive."
two pages after "The road does not end. The map does not lie." Flag the *second and
later* instances of a construction whose frame (small function words) matches an
earlier one while the content words were swapped. One instance is style; the refill is
a die. (Book-level repetition across chapters is `drift-check`'s job — this category is
within the chunk you were assigned.)

### Math / Analytical Language (AI Tell)
AI defaults to quantitative framing when it can't render the actual feeling. Flag whenever mathematical or analytical vocabulary is used metaphorically or to describe emotional/situational judgment.

**Forbidden words (metaphorical use only — literal school/work contexts are fine):**
- math / the math
- arithmetic
- geometry / geometric
- calculus (as in "the calculus of risk")
- equation / equations
- algorithm / algorithmic
- formula / formulaic
- variable / variables
- metric / metrics
- odds (when used as abstract shorthand: "the odds were against them")
- probability / probabilistically
- statistic / statistically
- exponentially (almost always wrong: "exponentially worse" is rarely meant literally)
- trajectory (when used metaphorically: "the trajectory of his life")
- optimize / optimized / optimization
- quantify / quantifiable
- ratio / ratios
- compound (metaphorical: "compound the problem")
- factor / factored in / factors into

**Forbidden phrases:**
- "ran the numbers" / "run the numbers"
- "did the math" / "do the math" / "looked at the math"
- "the math doesn't add up" / "the math checks out"
- "the geometry was off" / "the geometry of [situation]"
- "calculated the odds" / "calculated the risk" / "calculated the cost"
- "the equation was simple" / "changed the equation" / "the equation changes"
- "factor in [abstract thing]" / "factor out"
- "by any metric" / "on any metric"
- "the calculus of [noun]" (e.g., "the calculus of survival")
- "the formula for [noun]" (e.g., "the formula for success")
- "exponentially [adjective]" when not literally exponential
- "on a trajectory toward" / "trajectory of [abstract thing]"
- "optimize for [abstract thing]" (e.g., "optimizing for survival")

**Why:** These phrases are how AI describes situations it doesn't want to render concretely. "The math worked out" means nothing. What actually happened? Write that instead.
