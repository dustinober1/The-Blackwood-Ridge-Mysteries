# Big Idea Frameworks

## What Is a Big Idea?

A big idea is **not** a topic. It's a **proposition** — a specific claim about how the world works that, if accepted, changes the reader's behavior or understanding.

Formula: **[Unique Mechanism/Insight] + [Reader Outcome] + [Why It Works]**

| Not a Big Idea (Topic) | Big Idea (Proposition) |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| "How to negotiate" | "The best negotiators don't compromise — they expand the pie by discovering hidden variables the other side values but doesn't know they value" |
| "Productivity systems" | "Your brain is for having ideas, not holding them — externalize everything into a trusted system so your attention stays on the work" |
| "Leadership skills" | "Authority is granted by followers, not titles — you lead by making others more capable, not by telling them what to do" |

## Five Frameworks for Finding Your Big Idea

### 1. The Contrarian Insight
**Pattern:** "Everyone thinks X. Actually Y. Here's why."

- Find the conventional wisdom in your domain
- Identify where it fails (edge cases, new context, hidden assumption)
- Articulate the correction with evidence
- Example: "Everyone thinks willpower builds habits. Actually, environment design builds habits. Willpower is a finite resource; environment is always on."

### 2. The Missing Step
**Pattern:** "Books teach you A and Z. They skip B through Y. This book is B through Y."

- Map the reader's journey from problem to solution
- Identify where existing books jump (the "just do it" gap)
- Build the missing bridge
- Example: "Books tell you to 'find product-market fit.' They don't tell you how to run the 20 customer interviews that actually reveal it. This book is the interview playbook."

### 3. The Synthesis
**Pattern:** "Field A has insight X. Field B has insight Y. Nobody has combined them. Together they solve Z."

- Identify two (or more) disconnected literatures/practices
- Find the complementary mechanisms
- Show the emergent property
- Example: "Behavioral economics explains *why* we decide. Design thinking explains *how* to prototype solutions. Together they give you a method for designing choices that work *with* human psychology."

### 4. The Unique Mechanism
**Pattern:** "I discovered/developed [specific tool/framework/process] that produces [result] because [mechanism]."

- You have a proprietary or rare method
- Name it, define it, show the mechanism
- Prove it with your evidence
- Example: "The 'Decision Ledger' — a 5-minute daily practice that surfaces your implicit decision criteria so you can audit and improve them. Works because it makes the invisible visible without adding cognitive load."

### 5. The Counter-Intuitive Data
**Pattern:** "The data says X. Conventional wisdom says Y. The data wins. Here's what to do instead."

- You have access to unique data (your research, proprietary dataset, longitudinal study)
- The data contradicts common practice
- Build the new practice from the data
- Example: "Analysis of 10,000 startup pitches: the ones that raised the most money *didn't* have the best traction — they had the clearest 'why now' narrative. Traction matters less than timing story."

## Testing Your Big Idea

| Test | Question | Pass |
|------|----------|------|
| **One-Sentence** | Can you say it in one sentence without commas? | Yes |
| **Stranger Test** | Would a stranger at a party say "Tell me more"? | Yes |
| **Competitive** | Does it differentiate from top 5 competing books? | Yes |
| **Evidence-Backed** | Do you have the evidence for each clause? | Yes |
| **Actionable** | Does it imply a clear reader action? | Yes |

## Big Idea → Thesis → Book Structure

```
Big Idea (marketplace hook)
    ↓
Thesis (central argument — the "why it works" expanded)
    ↓
Evidence Architecture (what proof you need)
    ↓
Structure (how the argument unfolds chapter by chapter)
    ↓
Reader Transformation (where the reader ends up)
```

**The big idea sells the book. The thesis proves it. The structure delivers it. The transformation keeps the promise.**
