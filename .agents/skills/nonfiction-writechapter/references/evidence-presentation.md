# Evidence Presentation: Variety and Precision

## The Variety Rule

Never present two evidence blocks of the same type the same way. Vary:
- **Narrative frame** (experiment as story / expert as character / data as revelation)
- **Sentence structure** (lead with finding / lead with method / lead with implication)
- **Authority stance** (direct quote / paraphrase with citation / "the data show")
- **Visualization language** (describe a figure / walk through a table / narrate a trend)

## Evidence Type Templates

### Empirical Study (Narrated)
> "In a 2023 randomized trial (N = 2,400), researchers assigned [population] to [intervention] vs [control]. After [duration], the [outcome] difference was [effect size, CI, p-value]. Crucially, [moderator/subgroup finding] — this matters because [theoretical implication]."

### Empirical Study (Data-First)
> "[Effect size] — that's the magnitude of [outcome] improvement when [intervention] replaces [control] across [N] participants in [context]. The confidence interval [range] rules out trivial effects."

### Expert Quote (Integrated)
> "[Expert name], who [credential/context], puts it precisely: '[exact quote].' This isn't opinion — it's [summary of the evidence base the quote represents]."

### Expert Quote (Counterpoint)
> "Skeptics cite [expert]: '[quote].' But [expert]'s own data show [contradiction/limitation], and [newer study] updates the picture."

### Historical Case (Scene-Set)
> "[Date/place]. [Key figure] faced [decision point with stakes]. The choice — [specific action] — resulted in [outcome]. What [figure] couldn't know: [hindsight insight that serves your argument]."

### Historical Case (Pattern-First)
> "Across [time period], [pattern] repeated in [N] independent cases: [specifics]. The invariance suggests [structural cause], not [contingent explanation]."

### Theoretical Derivation (Step-by-Step)
> "If [premise 1] and [premise 2] hold, then [conclusion] follows necessarily. [Premise 1] is established by [evidence]. [Premise 2] is [axiom / definition / empirical regularity]. Therefore [claim]."

### Counter-Argument (Steel-Manned)
> "The objection: [strongest form]. Its evidence: [specific study/logic]. Its logic: [explicit syllogism]. If this objection succeeds, [your claim] collapses because [exact dependency]."

### Counter-Argument Rebuttal
> "But [objection] assumes [hidden premise]. [New evidence / logical analysis] shows [premise is false / boundary condition violated]. Therefore [objection] does not refute [claim]; it only limits it to [refined scope]."

## Precision Checklist (Every Evidence Presentation)

- [ ] **Specificity:** Named study, author, year, N, design, key statistic
- [ ] **Accuracy:** Numbers match source exactly; quotes are verbatim
- [ ] **Context:** Reader knows population, setting, limitations
- [ ] **Relevance:** Explicit link to the sub-claim being proved
- [ ] **Turn:** The evidence does something — reverses, complicates, synthesizes, extends
- [ ] **Variety:** Different presentation mode from previous block
- [ ] **Certainty calibration:** Language matches evidence strength

## Common Precision Failures

| Failure | Example | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Vague citation | "Studies show..." | "A 2022 meta-analysis of 47 RCTs (N = 12,000) found..." |
| Quote without context | "As Smith says, 'X.'" | "Smith (2021), analyzing the same dataset, concludes: 'X.'" |
| Statistic without denominator | "50% improved" | "50% of the 200 treatment-group participants improved (vs 30% control)" |
| Effect without uncertainty | "The treatment works" | "The treatment reduced symptoms by 12 points (95% CI: 8–16, p < .001)" |
| Correlation as causation | "X leads to Y" | "X predicts Y (r = .42); the experimental evidence for causation is [cite]" |
| Cherry-picked case | "Company X succeeded by doing Y" | "Of 50 companies attempting Y, 3 succeeded (6%); Company X illustrates the success pattern" |
