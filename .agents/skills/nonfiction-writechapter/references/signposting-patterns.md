# Signposting Patterns for Nonfiction Drafting

## Opening Signposts (First 2-3 paragraphs)

### Claim + Stakes
> "The central claim of this chapter is [claim]. This matters because [stakes — what fails for the book's thesis if the reader doesn't accept it]."

### Roadmap (use sparingly, only for complex chapters)
> "We establish this in three moves: first, [evidence type] shows [sub-claim]; second, [evidence type] reveals [turn]; third, [synthesis] yields [new understanding]."

## Evidence Block Transitions

### From Claim to First Evidence
> "To prove [claim], we start with [evidence type]: [specific study/case/data]."

### Between Evidence Blocks (The Turn Transition)
> "But [finding from previous block] only holds if [assumption]. [Next evidence type] tests this by [specific approach]."

### Complication/Reversal Transition
> "[Previous evidence] suggests [X]. Yet [new evidence] shows [not-X / X only under conditions / X with critical caveat]."

### Synthesis Transition
> "Together, [evidence 1] and [evidence 2] imply [synthesis] — a pattern neither reveals alone."

## Within-Block Signposts

### Introducing Concrete Evidence
> "Consider [specific study: author, year, design, n, key finding]."

### Quoting Authority
> "As [exact quote with citation], [author] demonstrates [point]."

### Presenting Data
> "The data show [specific numbers with context]. This means [interpretation]."

### Moving from Concrete to General
> "This [case/study/finding] illustrates a broader principle: [generalization]."

### Flagging Evidence Strength
> "This correlation is robust (p < .001, replicated across [contexts])."
> "This finding is preliminary: [limitation]. We treat it as suggestive, not definitive."

## Counter-Argument Signposts

### Steel-Manning
> "The strongest objection: [counter-claim in its best form]. Proponents argue [specific reasoning with evidence]."

### Rebuttal
> "This objection fails because [specific flaw / boundary condition / contrary evidence]. [Evidence] shows [rebuttal point]."

### Concession + Pivot
> "Granted, [valid limitation]. But this does not undermine [core claim] because [reason]."

## Closing Signposts

### Synthesis (not summary)
> "We began with [claim]. The evidence forces us to [revise/refine/deepen] it: [synthesis — the new understanding]."

### Takeaway
> "The practical upshot: [actionable implication — what the reader can do/think differently]."

### Forward Hook
> "But [synthesis] raises a new question: [specific hook]. Chapter [N+1] answers it by [preview of next chapter's argument]."

## Anti-Patterns (Remove These)

| Pattern | Problem | Replace With |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| "In this chapter we will..." | Author-centered, passive | "We argue [claim] because [stakes]." |
| "As mentioned above..." | Backward-looking | Forward signpost: "Having established [X], we now..." |
| "The next section discusses..." | Topic list, no argument | "To prove [sub-claim], we examine [evidence]." |
| "It is important to note..." | Filler, hedges | Direct statement of what's important and why. |
| "Interestingly,..." | Empty intensifier | State what's interesting and why it matters. |
| "Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally" | Additive, not cumulative | Causal transition: "Because [X], [Y] follows." |
