# Decision-Maker Audience Profile

## Who They Are

**Title/Role:** C-suite, VPs, Directors, Investors, Board members, Policy makers, Buyers, Procurement leads.

**Daily Context:** Decision-dense, time-starved. They don't read books — they *scan* books for the decision framework. 15 minutes max. They delegate deep reading to staff.

**Pain Points:**
- Proposals with no clear decision criteria
- Vendors/teams pushing solutions without risk analysis
- "Strategic" initiatives with no measurable outcomes
- Technical detail that obscures business logic
- Surprise dependencies / hidden costs / vendor lock-in

**Goals:** Approve the right things, kill the wrong things, allocate capital, reduce risk, align stakeholders, sleep at night.

**Information Consumption:** Executive summary → Decision framework → Risk appendix → (maybe) deep dive. Skims for: decision criteria, ROI model, risk matrix, comparable cases, exit criteria. Trusts: peer decisions, independent analysis, quantified outcomes.

## Knowledge Assumptions

**Baseline (Some exposure → Practitioner):**
- Understands the business domain (P&L, strategy, org design)
- Knows the *vocabulary* of the technical domain (not the implementation)
- Has seen 10+ proposals in this space
- Has been burned by: overpromise, underdeliver, scope creep, talent gaps

**Gaps They Need Filled:**
- How to *evaluate* this domain without being a practitioner
- What questions to ask (and what answers smell like BS)
- Decision criteria that separate signal from noise
- Risk factors practitioners forget to mention
- How to structure the engagement (pilot, phase, kill criteria)

## Objection Patterns

| Objection | Root Cause | Book Response |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| "Where's the ROI model?" | Needs quantified business case | Chapter 2: Decision Framework with ROI calculator template |
| "What are the risks you're not telling me?" | Trust deficit from past burns | "Risk Register" per approach; pre-mortem templates; failure mode library |
| "How do I compare vendors/approaches?" | No evaluation framework | Decision matrix with weighted criteria; "ask these 5 questions" scripts |
| "This feels like a solution looking for a problem" | Solution-first framing | Problem-first diagnosis: "Don't buy X until you've confirmed Y" |
| "What's the pilot look like?" | Wants reversible commitment | Pilot design template: scope, success metrics, timeline, kill criteria |

## Prerequisite Norms

| Category | Typical Requirements | Tolerance for Missing |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Knowledge | Business fluency; domain vocabulary (not expertise) | Low — write for the intelligent non-specialist |
| Tools | Spreadsheet, BI dashboard, project tracker | Low — assume standard exec toolkit |
| Access | Budget authority; stakeholder alignment; pilot capacity | Medium — address org readiness explicitly |
| Mindset | Outcome-oriented; risk-aware; delegates well | Low — this is the audience DNA |
| Time | 2-4 hours total engagement | High — structure for 15-min scan + 90-min deep dive |

## Credibility Signals They Respect

1. **"Here's the decision framework [Company X] used to approve $5M"** — Named, quantified, replicable
2. **"Here's what failed and why — and how we'd decide differently"** — Failure transparency
3. **"Independent analysis by [firm] confirms..."** — Third-party validation
4. **"Here are the 3 questions that kill 80% of bad proposals"** — Practitioner-tested filters
5. **"Appendix: full methodology, data, and limitations"** — Audit-ready rigor

## Structural Expectations

- **Depth:** Executive summary + Decision framework + Reference appendices
- **Format:** Framework / Reference (narrative only as case study context)
- **Chapter Length:** 2,500–4,000 words (but: executive summary = 1 page)
- **Total Length:** 40k–60k words (lean; appendices can be long)
- **Visual Density:** High — decision matrices, risk heatmaps, ROI models, process flows
- **Actionability Ratio:** 50% decision tools / 50% context
- **Must-Have:** One-page executive summary; decision checklist; pilot template; risk register
- **Navigation:** "Read this if you have 15 min" guide; decision flowchart; appendix index

## Writing for Decision-Makers

**Voice:** Trusted advisor. "Here's how to think about this decision" not "Here's what to do."
**Tone:** Respectful of their intelligence and time. No fluff. No jargon without definition.
**Structure:** Decision Question → Evaluation Criteria → Options Analysis → Risk Assessment → Recommendation Framework → Pilot Design
**Pacing:** Front-load the decision tool. Context is reference material.
**Evidence:** Decision evidence. Show the framework working in real decisions.
