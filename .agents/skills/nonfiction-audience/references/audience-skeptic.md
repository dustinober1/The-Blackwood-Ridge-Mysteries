# Skeptic/Critic Audience Profile

## Who They Are

**Title/Role:** Academics, researchers, peer reviewers, subject-matter experts, regulators, auditors, competitive intelligence analysts, fact-checkers, journalists covering your beat.

**Daily Context:** Evaluates claims for a living. Reads with a red pen. Knows the literature better than you do. Has seen your methodology's cousins fail. Time is not the constraint — *rigor* is.

**Pain Points:**
- Cherry-picked evidence / survivor bias / publication bias
- Causal claims from correlational data
- "Study shows" without: n, p, effect size, confidence interval, replication status
- Ignoring the null results / boundary conditions / failed replications
- Advocacy disguised as analysis

**Goals:** Verify the claims. Find the limits. Assess the methodology. Determine if this advances the field or just adds noise. Decide: cite, ignore, or debunk.

**Information Consumption:** Systematic. Reads: abstract → methods → results → discussion → references → appendix. Checks: citations, data availability, preregistration, conflict of interest. Trusts: transparency, methodological rigor, intellectual honesty, steel-manning.

## Knowledge Assumptions

**Baseline (Expert):**
- Knows the canonical papers, key debates, replication crises in the field
- Fluent in: research design, statistics, measurement theory, validity threats
- Has *strong opinions* on the open questions
- Will check your citations. Will notice what you didn't cite.

**Gaps They Need Filled:**
- *Your* specific contribution (what's new vs. what's synthesis)
- *Your* methodological choices (and why alternatives were rejected)
- *Your* boundary conditions (where this doesn't apply)
- *Your* failure modes (what you tried that didn't work)
- *Your* data (or clear statement of why not available)

## Objection Patterns

| Objection | Root Cause | Book Response |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| "This causal claim isn't warranted by the design" | Methodology overreach | Explicit causal language discipline: "associated with" vs "causes"; design limitations section |
| "You're ignoring [key paper / contrary evidence]" | Literature gap | Comprehensive literature map; "why we weight X over Y" rationale; steel-man section |
| "The effect size is trivial / CI crosses zero" | Statistical literacy | Report: effect sizes, CIs, power, Bayes factors; practical significance vs statistical |
| "This only works in [WEIRD / specific] populations" | Generalizability claim | Explicit scope conditions; heterogeneity analysis; "does not apply to..." list |
| "Where's the preregistration / data / code?" | Transparency norm | Open science badges; OSF links; "available on request" with timeline; explain constraints |

## Prerequisite Norms

| Category | Typical Requirements | Tolerance for Missing |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Knowledge | Expert-level domain fluency; research methods | None — *write for peers* |
| Tools | Statistical software; reference manager; replication toolkit | N/A |
| Access | Journal access; data repositories; author contact | Medium — provide supplements |
| Mindset | Critical; constructive; values truth over win | Low — this is the audience |
| Time | Unbounded (will spend 10+ hours if worthwhile) | N/A — depth rewarded |

## Credibility Signals They Respect

1. **Preregistered studies with open data/code** — Gold standard
2. **Explicit limitations section *before* the discussion** — Intellectual honesty
3. **Steel-manned counter-arguments with citations** — Good faith engagement
4. **Effect sizes, confidence intervals, power analyses — always** — Statistical maturity
5. **"We were wrong about X in the first edition; here's the update"** — Self-correction

## Structural Expectations

- **Depth:** Reference-grade (no hand-waving; every claim sourced)
- **Format:** Reference / Framework (narrative only as context for methods)
- **Chapter Length:** 4,000–6,000 words (dense; no fluff)
- **Total Length:** 80k–120k words (including appendices, supplements)
- **Visual Density:** High — forest plots, path diagrams, measurement models, data viz
- **Actionability Ratio:** 40% evidence / 60% claims (evidence *is* the content)
- **Must-Have:** Methods appendix; data availability statement; preregistration links; replication guide; errata process
- **Navigation:** Detailed methodological TOC; claim-evidence map; citation index; supplement cross-ref

## Writing for Skeptics

**Voice:** Collaborative peer. "Here's what we found, here's how we found it, here's where it might break."
**Tone:** Precise. Hedged appropriately. No rhetorical flourishes that obscure meaning.
**Structure:** Research Question → Literature Gap → Methodological Choice → Results (with uncertainty) → Boundary Conditions → Limitations → Implications → Open Questions
**Pacing:** Dense. Every sentence earns its keep. Signposting for skimmability *and* depth.
**Evidence:** Primary evidence. Your data. Your methods. Your pre-commitments. Others' data as comparison.
