# Curious Generalist Audience Profile

## Who They Are

**Title/Role:** Educated professionals in other fields, lifelong learners, interdisciplinary thinkers, "T-shaped" people, readers of *The New Yorker*, *Atlantic*, *Harvard Business Review*, popular science/business/history.

**Daily Context:** Reads for pleasure and perspective. 30-60 minutes at a stretch. Buys books based on: reviews, author reputation, "sounds fascinating," gift recommendations. Not trying to *do* anything with this — trying to *understand*.

**Pain Points:**
- Jargon walls that exclude rather than illuminate
- Books that assume you care about the field's internal debates
- 400 pages for one insight that could be an essay
- Authors who mistake comprehensiveness for clarity
- No "so what?" for the non-specialist

**Goals:** Dinner-party fluency. Mental model expansion. "Aha" moments that reframe something they see in the world. Intellectual satisfaction.

**Information Consumption:** Narrative-driven. Reads cover to cover if the story holds. Skips: methodology chapters, technical appendices, tool tutorials. Trusts: narrative authority, synthesis quality, "I never thought of it that way."

## Knowledge Assumptions

**Baseline (Novice):**
- College-educated; comfortable with abstract concepts
- Zero domain-specific knowledge assumed
- Knows: scientific method, basic statistics, critical thinking
- Does NOT know: your field's history, key figures, canonical debates, terminology

**Gaps They Need Filled:**
- The *human story* behind the ideas (who, why, what was at stake)
- The *big picture* — how this connects to things they already know
- The *so what* — why this matters beyond the field
- The *edges* — where the ideas break, what's still unknown
- A *mental model* they can carry away, not facts to memorize

## Objection Patterns

| Objection | Root Cause | Book Response |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| "I got lost in the terminology" | Jargon without scaffolding | Glossary as narrative companion; "in plain English" sidebars; analogies from their world |
| "Why should I care?" | No bridge to their interests | Chapter openings: "This matters because..." connecting to universal themes |
| "This feels like a textbook" | Information density > narrative pull | Narrative spine: characters, conflict, mystery, resolution — ideas as characters |
| "I'll never use this" | No transferability | "Takeaway" mental models applicable beyond the domain |
| "It's too long" | Respect for their time | Ruthless editing; "skip to..." signposts; essay-length chapters |

## Prerequisite Norms

| Category | Typical Requirements | Tolerance for Missing |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Knowledge | General education; curiosity | None — *zero* domain prerequisites |
| Tools | None | N/A |
| Access | None | N/A |
| Mindset | Open; enjoys complexity; tolerates ambiguity | Low — this is the audience |
| Time | 6-10 hours total (2-3 sittings) | Medium — structure for 30-60 min chapters |

## Credibility Signals They Respect

1. **"I spent 5 years embedded with [group] and here's what I saw"** — Narrative authority
2. **"This idea changed how I see [everyday phenomenon]"** — Personal transformation
3. **"The leading expert, [Name], told me..."** — Access to insiders as characters
4. **"Here's the experiment that settled a 50-year debate"** — Story of discovery
5. **"We still don't know X — and that's the exciting part"** — Intellectual honesty

## Structural Expectations

- **Depth:** Conceptual (not technical); synthesis over comprehensiveness
- **Format:** Narrative / Hybrid (ideas as story; frameworks as takeaways)
- **Chapter Length:** 3,000–4,500 words (essay length)
- **Total Length:** 60k–85k words
- **Visual Density:** Medium — conceptual diagrams, timelines, maps, portraits
- **Actionability Ratio:** 30% insight / 70% application (application = mental model transfer)
- **Must-Have:** "One idea per chapter" clarity; narrative hooks; "why this matters" refrains
- **Navigation:** Thematic TOC; "start anywhere" chapter independence; further reading as narrative trail

## Writing for Generalists

**Voice:** Storyteller-guide. "Come with me — I found something strange."
**Tone:** Intellectual companionship. Not dumbing down — *opening up*.
**Structure:** Hook → Mystery → Investigation → Revelation → Implication → New Mystery
**Pacing:** Novelistic. Cliffhangers. Characters (scientists, practitioners, ideas themselves).
**Evidence:** Story evidence. Scenes. Dialogue. Moments of discovery. Data as character.
