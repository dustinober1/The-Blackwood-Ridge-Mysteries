# Sheriff Dalton Cross

## Core profile

Mid-fifties, tall, broad-shouldered, pressed uniform, permanent hat line. A conventional small-town lawman whose best trait and deepest limitation are both procedural seriousness.

## External drive

Build cases that survive scrutiny, protect innocent people from intuition masquerading as proof, and keep official records accurate enough to outlast the people holding office.

## Internal need

Trust specialized context without abandoning responsibility. Cross must distinguish procedure from institutional comfort and use boundaries to admit expertise rather than exclude it.

## Wound and fear

His father taught him: “Evidence first. Gut feelings get innocent men hanged.” Cross fears being out of depth and compensates through control. Book 4 shows him that bad procedure can be perfectly neat and that loyalty to an office may require naming its failures.

## Voice and body

- Clipped, direct, minimal.
- Dry wit used rarely.
- Removes hat indoors as genuine respect; withholding it signals deliberate resistance.
- Rubs back of neck when out of depth.
- Sets jaw when friendship, mentorship, or institutional loyalty conflicts with duty.
- Speech shortens under pressure.

## Current method

Cross owns scenes, warrants, searches, interviews, evidence handling, subpoenas, arrests, and official wording. He can log Callie's observations without pretending they are legal conclusions. He requires source, custody, corroboration, and portable phrasing.

## Current relationships

- **Callie:** Records consultant, case by case, boundaries written. He trusts her documentary analysis enough to act on it after corroboration. Their warmth is controlled and non-romantic.
- **Mae:** Old family/community connection. He listens to her but no longer needs Mae to unlock every difficult decision.
- **Eli:** Helpful apprentice, never official investigator. Cross rewards narrow work and enforces its limits.
- **Office history:** Roland Tavis's Book 4 failure is now part of the official record, not a private inheritance.

## Appearance history

### Book 1

Nearly fixed on Eli as the obvious suspect; accepted Callie's evidence; arrested Gus; shook Callie's hand.

### Book 2

Read Callie's full file but resisted acting against his friend Wells. Ultimately arrested Wells. Jaw-set became the friendship-versus-duty tell.

### Book 3

Began consulting Callie informally and called her work useful, but did not call it partnership.

### Book 4

Invited Callie into a controlled record-reconstruction role. Corrected the Lila Mae Crowe file, confronted predecessor Roland Tavis, arrested Benedict Calder, and authorized a formal case-by-case records-consultant arrangement.

### Book 5 — entering state

Must prove the arrangement functions: give Callie access only within written scope, challenge interpretive overreach, translate handwriting observations into investigative steps, and establish Nora Mercer Shaw's arrest grounds independently.
