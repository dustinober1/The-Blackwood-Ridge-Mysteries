# Mae Hartwell

## Core profile

About sixty at Book 1 (text-derived: "nine years old" + "fifty years," Book 1 ch4 — exact arithmetic; ~62 by Book 5), silver-haired, sharp-eyed, warm without being soft. Musical Southern cadence, gardening/cooking metaphors used precisely, lavender and practical layers. Her hands are usually occupied; stillness signals stress. *(Age corrected 2026-10-12 from "early seventies" — the published text wins; see reverse/book-01/reconciliation.md.)*

## External drive

Protect people she loves and keep Blackwood Ridge human when truth turns public life brittle.

## Internal need

Offer care without using warmth, food, family authority, or silence to control what another person is allowed to know or do.

## Wound and flaw

Mae carried partial knowledge of the founding scandal for decades and chose silence to protect living descendants. Her warmth was real and also a way to keep rooms from asking what she knew. Books 1–2 break that strategy without granting easy absolution.

## Voice and body

- Warm, unhurried cadence; no caricature.
- Gardening and cooking images carry sharp observation.
- Calls people honey/darlin' sparingly and naturally.
- Hands stop when truly afraid.
- Lavender remains a sensory marker, thinner after the fracture.

## Current relationships

- **Callie:** Surrogate-family bond changed by truth. Mae now knocks and asks permission. The Book 4 thaw is practical: towels, labels, bread outside evidence space, memory offered as context. No complete apology or erased harm.
- **Cross:** Long familiarity — she has known him since he was a deputy (text-derived; the old "changed his diapers" framing is void, age-impossible). Can reach him but must not become his moral switch.
- **Eli:** Treats him as someone who belongs at the worktable without making belonging ceremonial.
- **Eleanor:** Promised to look after Callie. Book 5 tests whether looking after can mean setting a limit rather than taking control.

## Appearance history

### Book 1

Begged Callie to stop exposing the founding secret, then remained at the door after the truth broke the town.

### Book 2

Confessed Marguerite Dabney's visit and moved Cross to act. Spoke late but “earlier than Marguerite.” Began the three-knock permission habit.

### Book 3

Slow thaw continued through practical support and restrained context.

### Book 4

Entered Eleanor's back room by permission, helped preserve damaged records, humanized Lila Crowe without turning memory into proof, and established shared work as the new relationship language.

### Book 5 — entering state

Provides Mercer civic/family context, finds a genuine repair ticket during authorized work, notices the cost of Callie's public usefulness, and places `NOT TODAY` on part of the request stack instead of delivering a moral speech.
