# Packaging — Murder Most Annotated

## Bestseller research

### Cover style hierarchy (2025–2026 market)

1. **Illustrated watercolor / stylized art** — strongly outperforms photographic covers. Photography signals thriller or amateur self-publishing to cozy readers.
2. **Settings-as-storytelling** — bookshops, cafes, village streets with rich detail.
3. **Abstract / minimalist objects** — post-Richard Osman trend. Bold color blocks, symbolic objects (teacups, keys), type-forward. Growing in Amateur Sleuth.
4. **Dogs** — now the #1 animal on cozy covers (cats declining outside paranormal).
5. **People** — female sleuth against setting; less dominant.

### Standard cozy palette
- Mint green, lavender, soft rose/pink, butter yellow, warm cream/ivory backgrounds.
- Seasonal variants: autumn (orange, burgundy, gold); coastal (aqua, coral).
- Atmospheric/gothic-tinged cozies: deep purple/black, desaturated blues and grays, sepia and aged tones, muted metallics (tarnished gold, aged silver).

### Typography conventions
- Playful, rounded fonts; script accents for handcrafted feel.
- Serif for traditional/atmospheric; sans-serif for contemporary.
- Must be legible at 150 px thumbnail width.
- Series title and author name given equal prominence.

### Composition patterns
- Central object/setting as focal point.
- Cute elements framing scene (teacups, flowers, books, baked goods).
- Subtle mystery hints: magnifying glass, faint weapon outline.
- Series branding via consistent visual template across books.

### Tired tropes / cover clichés to avoid
- Generic pastel-illustration busy scenes (oversaturated market).
- Cookie-cutter pun-based titles on covers.
- Photo-realistic covers (signal amateur to cozy readers in 2026).
- Cat-helps-sleuth imagery in non-paranormal cozies.
- Cluttered compositions that fail at thumbnail.

### Growing trends
- Atmospheric/gothic-tinged cozies bridging traditional cozy and literary mystery.
- Bookish cozies (bookshop/library subgenre) — marked "Growing" by Automateed.
- Minimalist "single bold symbol" covers (Richard Osman effect).
- Deeper emotional stakes — grief, family secrets, found family.

This book bridges:
- **Core cozy mystery readers** (amateur sleuth, small-town setting).
- **Literary fiction crossovers** (readers who discover cozies through Richard Osman).
- **Bookish mystery fans** (cross-pollination with library/archival subgenres).

### Differentiators

This novella occupies the "atmospheric bookish cozy" niche — a space with growing demand but limited supply. Its key differentiators from standard cozies:

1. **Literary emotional weight** — the moral-ambiguity stakes and grief-driven protagonist set it apart from lighter culinary cozies.
2. **The marginalia hook** — unique intellectual appeal; no other cozy series is built around decoding historical margin notes.
3. **Gothic-tinged atmosphere** — warmth of the cozy with a chill underneath; bridges cozy and literary mystery.

## Cover guidance

### Visual concepts

**Concept A: The Annotated Page (recommended)**
A single antique book lies open on a dark wooden surface, its yellowed pages covered in faded handwritten marginalia. A brass magnifying glass rests partially on the page, its glass catching a warm amber light from a nearby unseen source. The marginalia text is illegible at thumbnail but clearly handwritten, suggesting secrets. A faint tea stain (bergamot-brown) marks the corner of one page. The mood is warm but haunted — inviting and atmospheric simultaneously. Generous negative space above and below for typography.

**Concept B: The Bookshop at Dusk**
An exterior view of a small brick bookshop on a rain-slicked village square at twilight. Warm golden light glows from the windows, illuminating stacks visible inside. A single gas lamp casts a halo on the wet cobblestones. A figure (silhouette only) is faintly visible through the frosted glass — someone reading. The tone is nostalgic, mysterious, and deeply inviting. More traditional cozy-scene approach but with the atmospheric darkness that distinguishes this book.

**Concept C: The Magnifying Glass**
Minimalist composition. A brass magnifying glass hangs from a dark leather cord against a background of aged, foxed paper (the color of old book pages). Through the glass, a portion of handwritten text is magnified and legible — the fragment of a cipher. Below the glass, a faint ring left by a tea cup. Extremely restrained. The object itself is the mystery. Works at every size.

### Typography

- **Title font:** Elegant serif (e.g., Cormorant Garamond, Playfair Display). Refined, literary feel — not playful or rounded. The title "Murder Most Annotated" is itself literary (allusion to Shakespeare), so the type should match.
- **Author name:** Slightly smaller than title, same serif family or a clean sans-serif (e.g., Lato, Source Sans). Placed below title with generous spacing.
- **Series label:** Small, unobtrusive. "The Blackwood Ridge Mysteries · Book 1" in caps or small caps, placed between title and author or at the bottom.
- **Hierarchy (top to bottom):** Series label (smallest) → Title (largest, most prominent) → Author name (second largest).
- **All type must be legible at 150 px thumbnail width.**

### Composition

- **Upper third:** Series label (small) and title (large, centered or left-aligned with elegant tracking).
- **Middle third:** Focal imagery (Concept A, B, or C).
- **Lower third:** Author name, with optional tagline or series marker.
- **Negative space:** Leave generous margins around imagery for clean type placement. The image should breathe.

### Series branding

This is Book 1 of a planned series. Design the cover as a template future books can inherit:
- **Consistent typography treatment** across all series covers (same fonts, same hierarchy, same placement).
- **Shared visual motif:** the aged-paper/leather texture or the brass glass element — something that can recur in variation.
- **Color progression:** Book 1 aged jewel tones; future books could shift the accent color while maintaining the base palette.
- **Consistent frame or border** (optional): a thin ornamental rule or leather-binding edge that appears on every cover.
- **Series label placement** locked to the same position on every cover.

### Avoid

- Pastel watercolor busy scenes — this book's tone is too literary and atmospheric.
- Playful/rounded fonts or pun-based title treatments — the Shakespeare allusion sets a more elevated register.
- Generic magnifying-glass-and-top-hat stock imagery — a cozy cliché.
- Any imagery that reads as thriller, horror, or true crime (no blood, no weapons, no threatening shadows).
- Photographic covers — illustrated or painterly only.
- Cats on the cover — this is not a paranormal cozy.
- Overly cheerful color palettes — the book has real grief and moral weight.
