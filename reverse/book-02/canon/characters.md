### Callie Thorne

- **Role**: Protagonist; sole POV. ~36 (see Book 1 canon: 35 at Book 1).
- **Appearance**: Unchanged — cardigans, glasses on a chain, ink-stained fingers; the glass "hers by choice and not by ghost."
- **Speech**: Book 1's spare, level register, now with the cost carried openly ("Both of those things are real").
- **Wants**: To honor a dead stranger's aim without breaking the town twice — and to keep Doris from learning it "from the town, not from her."
- **Wound**: Book 1's lesson internalized: uncovering the past wounds the town; the tea's "almost right" register tracks the unfinished grief.
- **Arc**: keeper of an unopened mystery → the woman who says what a careful woman could not → "useful, not welcome," with the vow *not like this* minted for the series.
- **Relationships**: Mae (fracture held, three-knock grammar); Cross (respect, no handshake); Eli (teacher, formally, on Saturdays); Doris (the broken promise, receipted); Marguerite (posthumous trust).
- **Consistency notes**: WALKS everywhere (no car — series canon; ch7's driving is a flagged must-fix); the bergamot made Eleanor's way (two-finger count); knocks answered by sitting with people.

### Marguerite Dabney

- **Role**: Victim-author (died of cancer ~1 week before ch1); the cipher's maker.
- **Age**: never stated `[documented blank]` — taught Wells junior year (~1988–89), then colleague; plausibly 70s `[inferred]`
- **Appearance**: known by hand (small, steady, blue-black; final plates hurrying) and reputation (garden-club woman; "sharp, exact, respected more than she was warmed to")
- **Speech**: quoted twice — "You read what other people leave in the margins, don't you." (naming, ch1) and the final-plate message (ch5)
- **Wants**: remembrance, and the seeing said by someone who could survive saying it
- **Wound**: thirty-two years of having seen and said nothing — "the careful thing, which was nothing"
- **Arc**: posthumous — the estate aimed at The Foxed Page; the cipher a map, not an accusation; "Say it."
- **Relationships**: Doris (fellow keeper of the silence); Mae (the October visit); Wells (student, colleague, the man she saw); the garden club (her institution)
- **Consistency notes**: the botanical is [bible-named] *Flora's Lexicon of the Virginias* (1873) — title never printed in text; visited Mae last October; directed her books to the shop ("the annotated one to be priced by you personally")

### Doris Coalter

- **Role**: Lauren's mother; the living stake.
- **Age**: seventy or near it `[text ch2]`
- **Appearance**: narrow, upright, steel-grey hair cut short; the knitter of a perpetually unfinished scarf
- **Speech**: flat, dry, rationed; short declaratives laid down like stakes; the flatness "the loudest thing in the garden"
- **Wants**: Lauren back first; the truth only if it comes to HER first
- **Wound**: thirty-two years of "runaway" — the file, the gate, the waiting
- **Arc**: the demand → the closed kitchen → the reckoning: the photo turned to the wall and back; "the grief could move now"
- **Relationships**: Lauren (daughter); deceased husband (unnamed); Wells (thirty years of Thursdays — "penance and cover and cruelty and care"); Mae (casseroles)
- **Consistency notes**: hands flat, palms down, when holding herself in place; the groceries bag she cannot unpack; "Mae told me" — the receipt of the broken promise

### Wells Ambler

- **Role**: Antagonist; killer. Principal, Blackwood Ridge High.
- **Age**: ~51 (nineteen in summer 1994 — "the summer we graduated") `[text-derived]`
- **Appearance**: tall, pressed shirt, sleeves rolled, greying temples; the baritone that never has to rise
- **Speech**: warm, anecdotal, tuned to the room's weather; names people deliberately; the deniable confession-shape ("That's not — it's not a confession. It's just the shape of it.")
- **Wants**: for the man to be real — "Tell me I'm not just the boy on the dock."
- **Wound**: the boy who walked home wet and "kept being alive. Every morning. Thirty-two years of mornings."
- **Arc**: beloved principal → the half-beat stop → the shaking palm → the confession → arrested; "You built a good life, Wells. You built it over her. Both of those things are real."
- **Relationships**: Lauren; Marguerite (teacher, colleague); Cross (friend — "He sat with me through the winter"); Doris (the Thursdays); wife and children (unnamed `[documented blank]`)
- **Consistency notes**: the OPEN PALM — practiced, deliberate; fails into "not a fist but a closing" (ch7); the fine tendon-shaking "not a tremor"; the two stories (party+home to Callie; Harlow barn to the Carmodys); went to the council ("crusade") rather than to Callie
