## Sample: The reclaimed morning

**Tags**: open, interiority
**Source**: ch1
**Why it's here**: The series' ritual-gauge in its Book 2 state — the set bone, the missing register, carried without complaint.

> She held her tea in both hands. Bergamot. She had reclaimed it in the weeks since — reclaimed the shop, the back room, the glass, the ritual of the morning cup — and the reclamation had held, more or less, the way a set bone holds. You could walk on it. You did not quite trust it. The tea was almost right. There was a register it hit when she was a girl, Eleanor at the long table, the steam rising between them, that she could not seem to find again, and she had stopped trying to name what was missing and settled for the fact of the warmth in her hands.

## Sample: The garden read

**Tags**: description
**Source**: ch2
**Why it's here**: The floriography mode — meanings italicized inline, the em-dash correction, the reading-as-walking.

> Here was a bed of rosemary and myrtle — *remembrance* and *love* — and at its head a small stone Marguerite had set the year Lauren went, not a grave — there was no grave — but a place to put the date, chiseled rough: 1994. Here was a patch of narcissus gone late and leggy — *self-love*, or *the return of affection* — and here, against the kitchen wall where the rain came off the eaves, a row of maidenhair fern, and fern in the old language meant *sincerity* and also *secret* and also *magic*, and Marguerite had planted it beneath the window where she must have washed her dishes for thirty years.

## Sample: Doris's testimony

**Tags**: dialogue
**Source**: ch2
**Why it's here**: Doris's rationed register — the flatness as the loudest thing; the list laid down like stakes.

> "They said she run. She did not run." Doris's voice did not rise. It went flatter, and the flatness was the loudest thing in the garden. "They dragged the lake. They did not find her. They wrote it down 'runaway' and they closed the file and they let me stand at this gate for thirty-two years waiting for a girl who was never coming back down that road."

**Tags**: interiority
**Source**: ch4
**Why it's here**: The cipher putting the seeing in Callie's head — the fragment close on the careful thing.

> The wet hemline.
>
> Callie could see it. That was the thing the cipher did that no secondhand account could do — it put the seeing in her own head. The kitchen window. The light on. The boy in the middle of the road, walking to keep the dust off his shoes because his trousers were soaked below the knees. He had been at the lake. He had come back alone. Lauren had not come home. ## Sample: The open palm shakes

**Tags**: dialogue
**Source**: ch7
**Why it's here**: Wells's tell failing — the definition-pair, the collapse measured in tendons.

> His hand was on the table. It was open. The palm was shown. And the palm was shaking, the fine, invisible tremor, and the openness was no longer practiced or deliberate or held a half-second too long. It was just a hand. A man's hand, shaking, on a table, in a room where his life had ended thirty-two years after it should have.

## Sample: The almost-right

**Tags**: close, interiority
**Source**: ch8
**Why it's here**: The closing register — the reclamation tested and held; the series' standing measure left deliberately unresolved.

> She made tea. One cup. She stood at the counter and she drank it, and the bergamot was — almost right. Not quite. The register she could not find, the one that tasted like Eleanor's kitchen and the world before grief — it was still missing. She did not think it would come back. But the tea was warm, and she had made it herself, and the almost-right was its own answer: the reclamation held. It had been tested and it had held, and the holding was not belonging — belonging was not hers to claim yet — but it was no longer just survival. It was a life. A small one. A useful one.
