## The narration

Book 1's spine, unbroken: third-person limited past tense inside Callie
Thorne; the cataloguing eye; emotion carried by objects and ritual, almost
never named. Book 2's additions: the flowers' Victorian meanings italicized
inline as the cipher's running gloss; the definition-pair as a beat ("Not a
confession. Not an accusation. A map."; "Not a tremor. A fine, invisible
shaking"); and hands everywhere — Callie reads people by their hands, and
the book's two great tells are hands (Wells's open palm, Doris's flat palms).

Rhythm: the long anaphoric accumulation closed by the verdict fragment
("The tumbler turned." / "She never ran." / "It was a map."). The bergamot's
"almost right" recurs as a gauge, never resolving. Lists run asyndetic
("penance and cover and cruelty and care"); the serial comma is effectively
absent (one confirming instance in the series, Book 1 ch6 — config keeps the
descriptive `true`). Numbers and times spelled out ("thirty-two years," "eight-fourteen").

Dialogue attribution: untagged exchanges and action beats; plain "said" with
rare measured adverbs; attribution by prop business — the trowel, the dish
cloth, the gate latch, the collar, the hat not removed.

## The register pair

The two-register system is intact and colder than Book 1: the cozy blanket
(peonies, the garden, casseroles, cuttings) laid over the gothic chill (the
cove's silent water, the dying hand hurrying, the groceries as the unbearable
knot). "The chill here is colder because the town is already wounded and
flinches from Callie on contact." `[bible premise]`

## Per-character speech

- **Callie** (dialogue): Book 1's spare, level register; now capable of the
  plain verdict ("Both of those things are real"); asks almost nothing,
  answers carefully.
- **Doris Coalter**: flat, dry, rationed; declaratives laid down like stakes
  ("They said she run. She did not run."); the flatness is the loudest
  register; grief held "so deep it had become structural."
- **Wells Ambler**: warm baritone tuned to the room's weather; names people
  deliberately; the deniable shape ("It's just the shape of it. You
  understand."); post-collapse, stripped and repetitive ("I walked home. I
  walked home and the legs of my trousers were wet").
- **Mae Hartwell**: the cadence thinner; "honey" placed as welcome-and-wall;
  the knock; "Late." / "It's enough." / "Lord, Callie."
- **Dalton Cross**: clipped; hat withheld as signal; the friendship spoken as
  refusal ("I won't do it, Callie"); "wear boots."
- **Eli Townsend**: apologies receding; craft questions arriving; "he was
  past the *thank you*, into the place where a person just nods."
- **Harriet Vailes**: careful, formal, knuckles whitening; the message
  delivered without becoming its face.
- **Marguerite** (written voice only): the rationed seeing; one imperative in
  the whole book ("Say it.").

## Motifs (deliberate repetition — never flag as crutch)

- **Floriography** — the cipher's vocabulary; book and garden one text.
- **The open palm** — Wells's practiced innocence; its collapse, not a fist,
  is the confession.
- **Hands flat, palms down** — Doris's grip on the earth.
- **The almost-right tea** — the gauge of unfinished grief (carried from
  Book 1; still unresolved by design).
- **The three-knock permission** — Mae's new grammar of approach.
- **Margins as voices** — the series mechanism, now aimed at a living man by
  a dead woman.
- **The groceries on Thursdays** — the knot of penance, cover, cruelty, care.

## Boundary notes for future books

- Same as Book 1: single POV, documents-only access to the past, no
  supernatural, no romance-forward Cross, no vague-handwriting solutions.
- The vow *not like this* (ch8) is series canon: Callie will not preserve
  truth through a silence that consumes a life.
- Eli's competence and closeness are the long-arc vulnerability (per
  series-bible): nothing in later books may shortcut the trust built here.
