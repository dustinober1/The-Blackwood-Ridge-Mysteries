## Voice & Style Profile

- **POV/tense (mechanical)**: Third-person limited, past tense, Callie Thorne only — the Book 1 spine holds exactly. Offstage action (Wells's interview lead-in, the legal process) arrives as reported sound and secondhand fact.
- **Serial comma**: Effectively absent — the voice is asyndetic ("penance and cover and cruelty and care"; "the wet hemline and the Harlow barn lie and the old dock and the deep"). One confirming comma-list exists in the whole series (Book 1 ch6, "motive, means, and access"). Descriptive config keeps `serial_comma: true`; the linter will not fight the voice because lists rarely close with "and."
- **Dialogue tag habits**: Identical grammar to Book 1 — untagged exchanges and action beats; plain "said" when tagged, with measured adverbs; attribution by prop business (the trowel, the dish cloth, the collar, the gate latch, the hat not removed).
- **Sentence rhythm / paragraph shape**: Book 1's engine unchanged — long anaphoric accumulations closed by verdict fragments ("The tumbler turned." / "She never ran." / "It was a map."). New Book 2 signatures: the definition-pair ("Not a confession. Not an accusation. A map."; "Not a tremor. A fine, invisible shaking"), the flowers' meanings italicized inline, and the recurring beat of hands (flat palms, the open palm, still knitting needles, shaking needles).
- **Per-character speech patterns**: Doris — flat, dry, rationed; short declaratives laid down like stakes ("They said she run. She did not run."); flatness as the loudest register. Wells — warm baritone tuned to the room; names people deliberately; the deniable sentence ("That's not — it's not a confession. It's just the shape of it."); post-collapse, stripped and repeated ("I walked home. I walked home and the legs of my trousers were wet"). Mae — the cadence thinner; "honey" placed as welcome-and-wall; the knock; "Late." / "It's enough." / "Lord, Callie." Cross — clipped, hat withheld, "wear boots"; the friendship spoken as refusal ("I won't do it, Callie"). Eli — apology receding, craft questions arriving ("Could you teach me?"). Harriet — careful, formal, knuckles whitening.
- **Tone and emotional register**: The two-register system intact and colder — "the chill here is colder because the town is already wounded and flinches from Callie on contact" (bible premise). New motifs: the open palm, hands flat palms-down, the unfinished scarf, groceries as the unbearable knot, the three-knock permission, the bergamot's missing register tracked chapter by chapter.
