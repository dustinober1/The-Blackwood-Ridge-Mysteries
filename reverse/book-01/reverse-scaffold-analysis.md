## Voice & Style Profile

- **POV/tense (mechanical)**: Third-person limited, past tense, single POV — Callie Thorne owns all 8 chapters; no head-hopping. Offstage action (the arrest, the search of Ralston Hall) arrives only as reported speech to Callie (ch7).
- **Serial comma**: Yes — "motive, means, and access to his habits" (ch6). The manuscript's list habit otherwise leans asyndetic for rhythm ("Not misplaced. Not misfiled."), but where a list closes with "and" the comma is consistently present.
- **Dialogue tag habits**: Predominantly untagged exchanges and action beats; when a tag appears it is plain "said," occasionally carrying a measured adverb ("Cross said it mildly, almost kindly, which was worse than if he'd said it sharp," ch2; "'I don't,' she said," ch3). No expressive tags (no snapped/exclaimed); attribution is done with prop business instead — hats set on counters, glasses cleaned, cuff links adjusted.
- **Sentence rhythm / paragraph shape**: Long, layered declaratives that accumulate by anaphora and triad ("She saw it in... She saw it in... She saw it in...," ch7), punctuated by short verdict fragments that close a beat ("Killed." ch4; "They did not fit." ch6; "The pages were gone." ch2). Motif-repetition is structural: "She filed it," "the room narrowed," "the pressure of the hand," "stepped over the floorboard." Em-dash asides are frequent. Forensic/book-conservation metaphor dominates narration (foxed, binding, watermark, meniscus, catalogue). Number style: years in numerals (1840, 1962), quantities and clock times spelled out ("a hundred and forty acres," "seven-twelve," "a quarter to nine").
- **Per-character speech patterns**: Callie — spare, level, declarative; deflects into professional register; almost never asks questions. Gus Ralston — warm patron's baritone, diminutives ("sweetheart," "Good girl"), word-doubling ("Wonderful. Wonderful."), menace dressed as concern; never gives a direct order. Dalton Cross — clipped procedural fragments, deposition cadence ("We clear?"), plain nouns (baggies, chain of custody). Eli Townsend — fragmented, self-interrupting, apology-stacked, accelerating under stress ("I didn't — they moved the display table, I was just—"). Mae Hartwell — unhurried domestic cadence, "honey," garden/kitchen metaphor in comfort mode; goes flat and metaphor-free when grave (the kitchen scene, ch4). Wendell Pike — academic self-correction and hedge ("well, not hoping, that's strong, but"), enthusiasm breaking through dashes, talks in the language of documents.
- **Tone and emotional register**: Elegiac restraint — grief and dread carried by objects and ritual (tea, glass, rain, lavender) rather than named feeling; moral seriousness about truth versus communal peace; the narration itself "catalogues," reading people the way Callie reads documents.
