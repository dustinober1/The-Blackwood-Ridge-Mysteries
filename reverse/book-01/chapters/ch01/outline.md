# Chapter 1: The Auction

**Act**: 1 — The Death and the Tear
**Timeline anchor**: Day 1, ~6:15 pm – late evening (late 2025; see canon/timeline.md)
**POV**: Callie Thorne
**Word target**: 2890 (actual span: 3208)

## Chapter Goal

*Retrofitted from finished prose — this outline describes what the chapter does, not a plan.* Open the series: establish Callie's inherited rituals and wound, kill Wendell Pike in Eleanor's image, and seed every thread the book will pull.

## Dramatic Question

Did Wendell Pike die of natural causes? — Answered in the reader's bones (no), confirmed by the ME in ch3; the deeper question, *who and why*, is carried forward.

## Scene Breakdown

### Scene 1: Closing rituals
- **Setting**: The Foxed Page back room, 6:15 pm, rain starting; leather and bergamot and cold stone.
- **Characters**: Callie.
- **Purpose**: Install the entire ritual system — bergamot, the brass glass, the floorboard, Eleanor's two-years-gone presence, the reading glasses on their chain.
- **Value shift**: `guarded routine → summoned` (she goes to the auction "less for the buying than for the being-there")
- **Key Beats**:
  1. The tea ritual and Eleanor's mug; "Habits didn't require an explanation she couldn't give."
  2. The glass on its felt rest; Eleanor's lesson quoted; the oldest binding's marginal hand.
  3. Locking up; the square in rain; the town hall and the archives named as her second workshop.
- **Exit state**: Callie crosses the square to the Ridgeway Auction House.

### Scene 2: The auction floor
- **Setting**: Converted tobacco warehouse; old-money crowd; lots under glass.
- **Characters**: Callie, Augustus Ralston, Eli Townsend, Wendell Pike, the auctioneer.
- **Purpose**: Assemble the cast around warmth and wrongness — Ralston's patronage ("little project"), Eli's flinch and the pulled-forward Lot Fourteen, Pike's deferred annotations ("There's a great deal to — well. After."), the shared bergamot.
- **Value shift**: `collegial warmth → catalogued unease`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. Ralston holds court by lowering his voice; finds her without scanning; the cuff-link tic.
  2. Eli's overshoot apology; eyes sliding to the back of the room.
  3. Pike and the annotations he won't show yet; the two bergamot drinkers; "Will you sit with me?"
- **Exit state**: Lots begin; Callie settles beside Pike.

### Scene 3: The back office
- **Setting**: The corridor and back office, ~7:21 pm; cold tea spreading across concrete.
- **Characters**: Callie, staff, the room of bidders.
- **Purpose**: The death — and the recognition scene: the cold cup, the meniscus, Eleanor two years ago. The catalogue runs before feeling does.
- **Value shift**: `ordinary evening → grief reawakened, suspicion seeded`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. Ralston exits toward the back corridor between lots 3–4 (tracked "without meaning to").
  2. Pike goes to refresh his tea during lot 4; the side door bangs open.
  3. The body; the bergamot "which was exactly the same as the bergamot... where her mother had died."
- **Exit state**: Callie stands at the edge of a crime scene no one has called one.

### Scene 4: Statements
- **Setting**: The auction hall, brittle quiet; Cross arrives twenty minutes later.
- **Characters**: Callie, Dalton Cross, Eli, the room.
- **Purpose**: Establish Cross's procedural grammar and the town's trained calm; the word "murder" stays unsaid; Eli reads as guilty to everyone who doesn't know him.
- **Value shift**: `trust in procedure → first doubt of it`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. Cross's level questions; "this is a natural death"; raising his voice so the room hears.
  2. Eli's overflow to a deputy; Callie "did not yet know he needed help."
  3. Ralston's absence noticed and filed without meaning.
- **Exit state**: Callie stays in the emptied room, cataloguing.
