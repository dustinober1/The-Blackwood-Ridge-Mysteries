## Chapter Goal

*Retrofitted from finished prose.* Close the wound without erasing it — the town thaws by increments, Callie reclaims the glass as a chosen tool, and a new margin-mystery opens the series door.

## Dramatic Question

Who annotated the botanical, and who is knocking? — Carried forward: this is Book 2's engine.

## Scene Breakdown

### Scene 1: The thaw
- **Setting**: The shop, open on purpose, across a week.
- **Characters**: Callie; the town in increments (the shopkeeper, the browser, the bench men, Mrs. Tucker).
- **Purpose**: Exile softening by degrees — a specific book sold on day three; the bench's nod on day six ("a verdict... she was still a person they recognized"); Mrs. Tucker's "Thank you" on day seven; eleven books by week's end. Mae's washed dish waits on the shelf: "The distance was not a wall. It was a doorway."
- **Value shift**: `pariah → neighbor`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. "She had not closed it" — the first decision, the morning after.
  2. The bench's nod; Mrs. Tucker's red-but-steady eyes.
  3. "The normalcy of it was the first thing that had not hurt."
- **Exit state**: The shop is a shop again; Callie is in it.

### Scene 2: The eighth day
- **Setting**: The back room, rearranged — scanner moved, lamp at the morning corner; rain returning.
- **Characters**: Callie.
- **Purpose**: The reclaiming — the floorboard stepped ON deliberately ("done being quiet for a ghost"); the bergamot tastes like tea again; the glass picked up and found lighter; Eleanor's line "only what it was... a true thing her mother said, once, about looking."
- **Value shift**: `mourning → dwelling`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. The step-on; the creak "in the small unnecessary noise of it."
  2. The tea that tastes right; "the grief was still in it... but she drank it."
  3. The glass on the felt rest — "the setting-down was not a letting-go. It was a putting-away."
- **Exit state**: She catalogs an estate lot by hand and habit.

### Scene 3: The botanical and the knock
- **Setting**: The back room table, nearly noon; then the front door.
- **Characters**: Callie; the unknown annotator (present only as a hand); the visitor (present only as two knuckles).
- **Purpose**: The hook — a nineteenth-century botanical with every margin full: small, precise, steady, modern blue-black ink, plate-by-plate names and dates, dense passages "not quite English and not quite anything else," patient, almost playful, written to be found. Callie reaches for the glass; the knock — unhurried, patient, "the knock of someone who had decided they would wait." She sets the glass down "to come back to," steps on the floorboard, and opens the door.
- **Value shift**: `ended → begun`
- **Key Beats**:
  1. The hand that is "not Eleanor's... not Wren's... not Pike's" — a hand she has never seen.
  2. Curiosity — "the first thing she had felt, since the auction, that was not a subtraction."
  3. The knock and the open door — the book ends mid-gesture.
- **Exit state**: The series door stands open (Book 2: the annotator is Marguerite Dabney; the cipher is botanical).
