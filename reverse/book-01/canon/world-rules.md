# World Rules — LOCKED REFERENCE

Setting and social rules for The Annotated Murder, retrofitted from the
finished manuscript and reconciled with the world bible (2026-10-11).
Grounded contemporary cozy mystery — no magic, no supernatural. Source tags
as in `characters.md`.

## Time and place

- **Story-present**: late 2025; the present thread runs seven days (Day 1 = auction night). `[bible: series timeline]`
- **Blackwood Ridge, Virginia** — foothills of the Blue Ridge; population ~3,500; founded 1780s; preserved colonial/antebellum architecture; brick storefronts, gas-lamp-style lighting on the square (deliberate historic character, not an anachronism), ivy on stone walls, a creek through town, willows along it. `[bible world.md]`
- The modern world presses at the edges (smartphones, digital scanners, online retail) but inside town "time moves slower"; founding families still hold social and economic power. `[bible]`
- County seat and finer geography unnamed — documented blank, inventible by later books. `[decision]`

## Social rules

- **Family names carry weight**: a Ralston or Hartwell name opens doors and silences questions; a drifter like Eli is invisible by default. `[text+bible]`
- **History is currency**: knowing who owned what is social capital; Callie's archival expertise makes her quietly powerful and quietly dangerous. `[bible]`
- **The town protects its own**: scandals handled internally; the sheriff answers to the town council, which bends to old money. `[bible]`
- **Politeness is armor**: Southern indirection — implications, silences, strategic kindness; direct confrontation is rare and therefore powerful. `[bible]`
- **The Ralston patronage web**: the library wing, the archives' scanner ($8,000) and climate control, the Historical Society and its annual lecture, council goodwill. "The Ralston name... opened doors and silenced questions" for 180 years. `[text+bible]`
- **"Gus never signs anything"**: he funds, and what he funds moves on its own — the face of procedure. This trait is also the working answer to the clean archive log (see Inquiries, below). `[text ch4, resolved]`

## Institutions and places

- **The Foxed Page**: narrow two-story brick bookshop on the square, a bookshop for over a century; Callie lives upstairs; back room = archival workspace (oak table, scanner, magnifying tools) and Eleanor's deathplace. The front floorboard creaks. Building owner unnamed — documented blank; Ralston's lease leverage reads as positional (council/bank goodwill), not documented ownership. `[text+bible+decision]`
- **Blackwood Ridge Town Archives**: basement of the town hall on the square; stone, climate-controlled vaults, fluorescent hum; access by personal key + a leather-bound sign-in log that is "the law of the place"; the town council can revoke access by motion (done in ch4, "pending the investigation"). `[text+bible]`
- **Ridgeway Auction House**: converted tobacco warehouse on the town's far edge; high ceilings, exposed brick, track lighting; back offices down a rear corridor; keeps a staff entrance log (Ralston signed out 7:12 pm on Day 1). `[text]`
- **Blackwood Ridge Historical Society**: restored Victorian off the square, green gingerbread trim, Ralston foundation plaque; visitor register that regulars annotate in the margins; collections room holds the 1962 Ralston estate lot containing the **Wren keystone** (series reference name for the untitled clothbound volume of regional survey records). Mae effectively runs it. Shutters after the arrest (ch7). `[text+bible]`
- **Ralston Hall**: antebellum house on the hill above town, built field-by-field on the stolen acreage; portrait gallery (the patriarch's eyes follow visitors); a locked desk drawer; open for tours and charity events per bible. `[text+bible]`
- **Sheriff's office**: attached to the county courthouse; small department, three deputies, two cells (one polished and empty for years — until now). Cross owns warrants, interviews, evidence handling, arrests. `[text+bible]`
- **Town square**: town hall (archives below), the shop facing it, bakery, barbershop, post office; a bench under an old oak where retired men render silent verdicts; brick walkways, gas-lamp-style lights. `[text+bible]`
- **Hartwell House**: Mae's warm Victorian on a side street; the kitchen hosts every important conversation; the Hartwell family Bible (her grandmother's hand in it) sits on the parlor shelf. `[bible]`

## The historical fraud (the engine)

- 1840–41: surveyor **Jacob Wren** is coerced into filing a false township survey — the recorded line runs northwest along the ridge, the true line three degrees west; over that boundary, three degrees ≈ **140 acres** taken from the **Hartwell grant** into Ralston holdings. `[text ch3, ch6]`
- 1843: **Rev. Thomas Hartwell** finds the fraud; killed in a staged riding accident on a road he had ridden a thousand times; ruled accidental by a **bought magistrate**. Silas Ralston ordered it ("S.R. iussit", "iudex emptus"). `[text ch4]`
- Wren's testimony survives in three strata: Latin margin shorthand readable by a classicist, a **private letter-substitution cipher** readable only by someone who has learned his habits, and the keystone volume (five families named: **Hartwell, Tucker, Fenn, Cole, Marr**; farewell written the night he fled). `[text ch3–5]`
- Legal exposure if exposed: Ralston land claims challengeable in court; families could sue for restitution; the founding narrative collapses. Consequences on the page are social, not legal — the suits never arrive in Book 1 (open series thread). `[text ch6]`

## The margins mechanism (series-critical)

Handwriting carries the living presence of writers; Callie reads hands the way she reads documents — pressure, rhythm, joins, hesitation, purpose. Strata established in Book 1: **Eleanor** (warm, looping, in the shop's oldest binding), **Wren** (frightened, Latin + cipher), **Pike** (eager downhill slant, then fear), **Mae's grandmother** (the family Bible), and the **unknown annotator** of the ch8 botanical (small, precise, steady, modern blue-black ink, patient, almost playful — "not quite English and not quite anything else"). The ch8 hand is Book 2's engine. `[text]`

## Inquiries (resolved working canon)

- **Who tore pp. 31–36, and why is the log clean?** Gus Ralston, between Day 1 night and Day 2, 3:47 pm — the pages are recovered from his locked desk drawer (ch7). The log shows nothing because Gus never signs anything (ch4); he entered on patronage/council access. Working canon, `[text-inferred]`.
- **The poison**: never named — "the compound in Pike's tea" (ch7), delivered in the corridor during lot four; a glass dropper bottle recovered from Ralston's desk. Later books may name it; do not invent retroactively.
- **A council master key opens any lock on the square** (stated ch5) — the shop's front door is keyed to town-council access. Odd but explicit; keep.
