# Characters — LOCKED REFERENCE

The cast roster for The Annotated Murder, retrofitted from the finished
manuscript and reconciled against the production bibles (2026-10-11).
Source tags: `[text]` = on the page; `[bible]` = production bible (fills a
text-silent fact); `[decision]` = canon decision recorded in
`../canon-decisions.md`. The `### Name` heading structure is load-bearing —
the linter derives character names from it.

### Callie Thorne

- **Role**: Protagonist; sole POV.
- **Age**: 35 at Book 1 (born ~1990) `[text-derived; bible concurs: early-to-mid thirties]`
- **Appearance**: Practical cardigans; reading glasses on a chain; ink-stained fingertips she stops scrubbing (ch7); deliberately low-maintenance; quiet vanity — a collection of vintage magnifying tools `[bible]`; no hair/eye description exists in text `[documented blank]`
- **Speech**: Spare, level, declarative; deflects personal questions into facts; thinks in historical/document analogies; complete sentences, rarely fragmented; almost never asks questions
- **Wants**: To finish what Pike started before Eli Townsend is arrested for it
- **Wound**: Eleanor died alone in the back room two years ago; Callie sat across from her and finished the open page before calling for help (never told anyone) `[bible: private memory]`; grief transmuted into work and walls
- **Arc**: Guardian of the dead at arm's length → chooses the living: hands the truth to Cross, loses the town's comfort, keeps the work as a choice ("the inheritance was not a destiny but a choice", ch6)
- **Relationships**: Eleanor Thorne (mother, †2024); Mae Hartwell (surrogate family, 30 years); Wendell Pike (colleague, never named friend); Augustus Ralston (patron turned killer); Dalton Cross (adversary → respect); Eli Townsend (protective)
- **Consistency notes**: Steps over the creaking floorboard until ch8 (steps ON it deliberately); brews Eleanor's bergamot (same tin, chipped mug with hairline-crack handle) every morning since 19; carries the brass magnifying glass on its felt rest; reads hands like handwriting; "files" observations without deciding them

### Eleanor Thorne

- **Role**: Deceased mother; absent presence structuring the book's rituals.
- **Age**: Unstated `[documented blank]`
- **Appearance**: Only via traces: looping precise handwriting, tortoiseshell reading glasses, the chipped mug `[text]`
- **Speech**: One line of quoted speech, the series' talisman: "The truth is always there, Callie. Most people just don't look closely enough."
- **Wants** (as reconstructed): the shop kept, the work continued, Callie looked after (extracted Mae's deathbed promise `[bible]`)
- **Wound/legacy**: Owned The Foxed Page from ~1985 and served as the town's unofficial archivist `[bible]`; died alone at her work
- **Arc**: n/a (died ~2024, two years before the book; death canonically natural heart failure — deliberately unresolved on the page, revisitable)
- **Relationships**: Callie (daughter); Mae Hartwell (close friend, 30 years of walking in); Augustus Ralston ("I knew her well. She was a careful woman")
- **Consistency notes**: Her reading glasses (tortoiseshell, wrong prescription) wait in the bottom drawer's leather case until ch6; her handwriting annotates the shop's oldest binding; bergamot was hers first

### Wendell Pike

- **Role**: Victim; Callie's dark mirror.
- **Age**: 68 `[text ch3; bible: late sixties]`
- **Appearance**: Rumpled tweed, unruly white hair, reading glasses pushed up into it, cleans them on a cuff worn thin; perpetual glasses-indentations on the nose `[text+bible]`
- **Speech**: Academic hedge and self-correction ("well, not hoping, that's strong, but"); speaks in footnotes and caveats; enthusiasm breaks through dashes
- **Wants**: To matter — the exposé that makes a lifetime in archives count `[bible]`
- **Wound**: Brilliant and completely alone; shared Callie's gift and her isolation taken to its end
- **Arc**: n/a — dies night 1; presence persists through notes, margins, and the trail he left
- **Relationships**: Callie (closest thing either had to a friend); Augustus Ralston (trusted the wrong patron — showed him preliminary findings); Eli Townsend (the misfiled-document argument); Mae (warned him to stop)
- **Consistency notes**: The only other bergamot drinker Callie knows; a spare pair of reading glasses on his archive table; his working hand deteriorates as fear grows (ch2)

### Augustus "Gus" Ralston

- **Role**: Antagonist; killer.
- **Age**: Early sixties `[bible; text-silent]`
- **Appearance**: Silver hair, tailored casual wealth (cashmere), effortless patrician; the perfect smile
- **Speech**: Warm patron's baritone; diminutives ("sweetheart", "Good girl"); word-doubling ("Wonderful. Wonderful."); menace dressed as concern; never gives a direct order
- **Wants**: The Ralston name and fortune protected at any cost — "A Ralston protects the family name. Everything else is negotiable" (his father's advice) `[bible]`
- **Wound**: His identity IS the legacy; he knows the scandal is true and chose the lie yearly until the choice became murder
- **Arc**: Beloved benefactor → exposed destroyer; arrested without resistance; bewildered rather than caught ("a man who had been misunderstood")
- **Relationships**: Silas Ralston (ancestor whose stare the text lays over his face); Pike (liked him, funded him, killed him); Callie (genuinely likes her, threatens her); Cross (council budget influence); Mae (fifty years of knowing what the other knows); has a granddaughter he fears will learn the truth `[bible, unnamed]`
- **Consistency notes**: Cuff-link adjustment (twice, same spot) when calculating; hands *effortfully* still in public — the over-control is the tell; cracks to a visible tremor only when being READ (ch5) — never a private-alone tremble; never signs anything; uses a town-council master key

### Silas Ralston

- **Role**: Historical antagonist (1840s); source of the fortune.
- **Age**: Unstated `[documented blank]`
- **Appearance**: The patriarch's portrait: flat measuring stare, same eyes, same set jaw — hangs in BOTH the town hall and Ralston Hall's gallery `[text+bible, resolved]`
- **Speech**: n/a (never speaks; known only through Wren's record)
- **Wants**: The land and the name above the law
- **Wound**: n/a
- **Arc**: Orchestrated the survey forgery (1840–41) and ordered Rev. Thomas Hartwell's death (1843, "S.R. iussit"); bought the magistrate
- **Relationships**: Augustus (descendant); Jacob Wren (coerced instrument); Rev. Thomas Hartwell (victim)
- **Consistency notes**: Town lore treats him as founder-benefactor; the fraud gave Ralston holdings ~140 acres of the Hartwell grant

### Jacob Wren

- **Role**: Historical witness/victim (1840s); the original margin voice.
- **Age**: Unstated `[documented blank]`
- **Appearance**: Known only by hand: small, careful, cramped by writing in margins; deteriorates — nib tears the paper — under fear `[text]`
- **Speech**: n/a; writes in Latin shorthand + a private letter-substitution cipher readable only by someone who has learned "a dead surveyor's habits"
- **Wants**: The truth on the record where the men who made him lie could not read it — "I pray God someone finds this who can do what I cannot"
- **Wound**: Coerced to file a false survey; fled town the night he finished writing
- **Arc**: Filed the forged 1840–41 survey under duress; hid the true line (3° west; ~140 acres) in margins; recorded Silas's order in the 1843 ledger; wrote the keystone volume (now the Society's "Wren keystone", Ralston estate lot, 1962); fate after flight unknown (open)
- **Relationships**: Coerced by Silas Ralston; posthumous collaborator of Pike and Callie
- **Consistency notes**: The five robbed families as he named them: Hartwell, Tucker, Fenn, Cole, Marr

- **Age**: Unstated `[documented blank]`
- **Appearance**: Unstated `[documented blank]`
- **Speech**: n/a
- **Wants**: To expose the fraud

### Mae Hartwell

- **Role**: Supporting; Callie's surrogate family; keeper of the secret.
- **Age**: ~59–60 at Book 1 (text arithmetic: "nine years old" + "fifty years", ch4) — the bibles' "early seventies" is superseded planning drift `[text > bible]`
- **Appearance**: Silver hair, small commanding frame, good cardigan, apron when cooking; smells of lavender (she grows and dries it) `[text+bible]`
- **Speech**: Unhurried musical Southern cadence; gardening/cooking metaphors in comfort mode; "honey"/"darlin'" sparingly; goes flat and metaphor-free when grave (the kitchen, ch4)
- **Wants**: The past kept where it lies — for her grandchildren, the town, and herself
- **Wound**: Fifty years of chosen silence; complicity grown heavy; the promise to Eleanor ("look after Callie") at war with the plea to stop
- **Arc**: Warmest presence in town → the moral crux (begs Callie to stop) → the knock at the door: soup, no absolution asked, the Bible offered as record
- **Relationships**: Callie (surrogate granddaughter since Eleanor died); Eleanor (promised to look after Callie); Augustus Ralston (fifty years of mutual knowing); Cross (known him since he was a deputy); deceased husband ("God rest him", name never canon) and unnamed children/grandchildren
- **Consistency notes**: Hands always busy (knitting, stirring, straightening) — stillness is the tell; runs the Historical Society in practice `[bible]`; serves punch at its annual lecture; the Hartwell family Bible on the parlor shelf

### Dalton Cross

- **Role**: Sheriff; institutional guardrail turned ally.
- **Age**: Mid-fifties, nearing retirement `[bible]`
- **Appearance**: Tall, broad-shouldered, going soft at the middle; permanent hat-line tan; clean-shaven; irons his own uniform
- **Speech**: Clipped, direct, minimal; deposition cadence ("We clear?"); dry wit shown rarely; shortens under pressure
- **Wants**: A case that survives scrutiny — "I work baggies. Chain of custody."
- **Wound**: His father's lawman creed ("Evidence first. Gut feelings get innocent men hanged.") plus fear of being out of his depth in the first murder in decades
- **Arc**: Procedural obstacle (nearly jails Eli) → converts on his own evidence (exit log 7:12 vs. body 7:21) → the handshake on the level of equality
- **Relationships**: Callie (antagonism → grudging respect); Augustus Ralston (never called him anything but "Gus" until the arrest); Eli (wrong suspect); Mae (known him since he was a deputy — she can reach him)
- **Consistency notes**: Removes his hat on thresholds (genuine); leans on doorframes, arms folded, when listening; rubs the back of his neck when calculating; keeps one cell polished

### Eli Townsend

- **Role**: Supporting; the wrong suspect; the stakes.
- **Age**: Early twenties `[bible]`
- **Appearance**: Lean, restless, dresses to disappear — hoodies, plain tees, worn sneakers; looks younger than he is; perpetual dark circles `[bible]`
- **Speech**: Fast, breathless, reflexively apologetic; sentences started and abandoned ("I didn't — they moved the display table, I was just—"); over-explains because silence feels dangerous
- **Wants**: To belong somewhere without apology
- **Wound**: A lifetime of expecting to be blamed; a drifter who reached Blackwood Ridge because it was "the last bus stop before he ran out of money"; father's standing instruction — "Call when you've figured things out" — never answered `[bible]`
- **Arc**: Scapegoat assembling itself → saved by Callie's evidence → cleared, hands still for the first time, standing in his own shape
- **Relationships**: Callie (first person on his side); Cross (prime suspect); Pike (the misfiled-document argument, ~1 week pre-murder)
- **Consistency notes**: Hoodie-string fidgeting (fades after clearing); flinches at voices; borrowed a dog-eared paperback and returned it to a wrong shelf by the back office the night of the murder — his unspoken alibi, photographed in place
