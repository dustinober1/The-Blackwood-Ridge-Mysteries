# Voice Samples — The Annotated Murder

**Locked reference.** Edit only through the `canon-update` skill.

Passages that *are* this book's voice. Seeded at reverse-scaffold import
(2026-10-11) from Book 1's own finished, published prose — the usual
gate-passed harvest rule does not apply here because these passages *are*
the author's prose, not a draft's. The voice bible states the rules in prose
and `book.config.yaml` encodes the mechanical ones; this file is the third
leg — the positive examples.

Every drafting and editing brief pulls 2–3 passages from here, matched by
tag to the scene being written. Keep each one short (80–200 words) — the
point is rhythm, register and restraint, not plot.

## How entries get here

1. **Seeded at reverse-scaffold import** from the book's published chapters
   (representative passages chosen by the voice-profiling pass as typical of
   the demonstrated voice, not its "best" lines).
2. **Harvested** entries may be added later only via `canon-update` — though
   for a frozen book this file should be considered complete.

## Sample: The bergamot ritual

**Tags**: open, interiority
**Source**: ch1
**Why it's here**: The opening move of the series voice — ritual objects carrying unnamed grief, the triadic accumulation, the verdict-fragment close.

> The back room of The Foxed Page smelled of leather bindings and cold stone and, faintly, of bergamot. It always smelled of bergamot. Callie had brewed the same tea every morning since she was nineteen, had kept the same tin on the same shelf, had poured it into the same chipped mug with the hairline crack running down its handle — Eleanor's mug, Eleanor's tea, Eleanor's tin. She told herself it was habit. Habits were clean. Habits didn't require an explanation she couldn't give.

## Sample: Pike's table

**Tags**: description
**Source**: ch2
**Why it's here**: Scene description as forensics — the room read like a document, the dead man present only in traces.

> His manuscript pages were fanned across the oak in the order of a mind mid-thought — notes here, a photocopied survey there, his annotated working copies stacked at angles. His reading glasses, the spare pair, folded on a book. A cold cup of tea — bergamot, bergamot, of course it was bergamot — at his elbow, half-finished, a faint brown ring already drying at the watermark. Callie stood over the table and did not touch anything for a full minute. She could smell him here. Old tweed and paper and the sweet dust of a man who lived in archives.

## Sample: The cipher opens

**Tags**: interiority
**Source**: ch3
**Why it's here**: The decode-music — the fragment pair, the em-dash aside, historical voice quoted inside the present-tense hunt.

> And the cipher opened.
>
> Not three degrees. Not a number. A line of attestation, abbreviated to its skeleton and then letter-substituted so that only another surveyor — or a woman who had taught herself a dead surveyor's habits — would read it clean: *I filed this contrariwise, and under constraint, and I set the true line here for the record.* Wren had not made an error. Wren had been made to lie, and he had buried his retraction in the only place the men who made him could not read.

## Sample: Mae's kitchen testimony

**Tags**: dialogue
**Source**: ch4
**Why it's here**: Mae's grave register — metaphor dropped entirely, the plain cadence; the beat tag mid-speech doing the work a tag would not.

> "She told it to me in this kitchen. She closed the door first, like the walls could hear. She told it in a voice I had never heard her use — low, and flat, and final, the voice of a woman saying a thing once because she would not say it twice. She said the Ralstons took the land. She said they took it from us, and from the Tuckers, and from the Fenns, and from two other families whose names I've forgotten because I was young and I didn't want to remember. She said a minister in our family found out. She said he died for it." Mae looked down at her folded hands. "She never said the word murder. She didn't have to. I was nine years old, and I understood her anyway."

## Sample: The patron's mask slips

**Tags**: dialogue
**Source**: ch5
**Why it's here**: The menace grammar — warmth unscrewed like a bulb, the threat dressed as sympathy, every sentence deniable.

> "I knew her well. She was a careful woman." He turned, and looked at her, and the warmth was gone from his face. Not replaced — gone, as if it had been a lamp and someone had unscrewed the bulb. What was left was the architecture underneath, and the architecture was older than he was. "Wendell didn't know when to stop, Callie. Terrible thing. He was a brilliant man, and he could not leave a matter settled, and it cost him." He held her gaze. "I would so hate to see what happened to Wendell happen to someone else."

## Sample: Eleanor's glasses

**Tags**: interiority
**Source**: ch6
**Why it's here**: The book's thesis in one beat — inheritance reframed as choice; the triple repetition ladder resolving into the body.

> They did not fit.
>
> They sat wrong on her nose, the prescription wrong for her eyes, the arms too tight at her temples, the whole weight of them wrong in a way that had nothing to do with optometry. She had known they would not fit. She had known it as she knew the floorboard creaked. But she had needed to put them on, once, and feel them sit wrong, and understand in her body that she was not Eleanor, that she had never been Eleanor, that the inheritance she had been carrying was not a destiny but a choice, and that she could set it down.

## Sample: The glass, reclaimed

**Tags**: close, interiority
**Source**: ch8
**Why it's here**: The closing register — the simile earned by the whole book, the grief carried inside the object without being named.

> It was lighter than she remembered. Not in any way she could have proved on a scale — the brass weighed what brass weighed — but lighter in the hand, the way a name is lighter once you have stopped carrying it for someone else. For two years she had held it as a relay runner holds a baton, waiting to hand it back to the woman who had given it to her, except the woman was dead and there was no hand to take it and the running had been the point, the endless running, the carrying that had filled the space where a life was supposed to be.
