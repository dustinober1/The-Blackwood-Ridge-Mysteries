# Voice Bible — LOCKED REFERENCE

The Annotated Murder (Blackwood Ridge Mysteries #1), Vesper Blythe.
Retrofitted 2026-10-11 from the finished manuscript's Voice & Style Profile
(reverse-scaffold). This is description, not prescription: Book 1 is
published and frozen, and this document exists so later books can be checked
against — and held to — the voice the series actually launched with.
Mechanical facts here stay in sync with `book.config.yaml` (`narrative`,
`voice_rules`). Positive examples live in `voice-samples.md`.

## The narration

Third-person limited, past tense, entirely inside Callie Thorne — no
head-hopping, ever. Offstage action (the arrest, the search of Ralston Hall)
arrives only as reported speech to her. The narration itself "catalogues":
it reads people, rooms, and events the way Callie reads documents —
pressure, provenance, condition, discrepancy. Forensic metaphor comes from
book conservation (foxed, binding, watermark, meniscus, leaves, margins);
emotion is carried by objects and ritual, almost never named. When the book
needs to say something true about feeling, it says it through a thing: tea,
a floorboard, lavender, rain, a glass that gets lighter.

Rhythm: long, layered declaratives that accumulate by anaphora and triad
("She saw it in... She saw it in... She saw it in..."), closed by short
verdict fragments that land the beat ("Killed." / "They did not fit." /
"The pages were gone."). Motif-repetition is structural, not accidental:
"She filed it," "the room narrowed," "the pressure of the hand." Em-dash
asides are frequent. The book favors asyndetic lists for rhythm but uses
the serial comma when a list closes with "and" ("motive, means, and access").
Years in numerals; quantities and clock times spelled out ("a hundred and
forty acres," "seven-twelve," "a quarter to nine").

Dialogue attribution: predominantly untagged exchanges and action beats;
when a tag appears it is plain "said," occasionally with one measured adverb
("Cross said it mildly, almost kindly, which was worse"). No expressive
tags — attribution is done with prop business: hats set on counters, glasses
cleaned, cuff links adjusted, hands read.

## The register pair

Two registers, deliberately contrasted, and the tonal shift between them IS
the reading experience: the cozy warmth (leather bindings, rain, hot tea in
a chipped mug, soup at the door) and the gothic chill (the basement archives
after hours, a margin note turning to dread, a warm smile tightening). The
cozier the warmth, the sharper the chill. Grief is restrained — never
wailed; dread is quiet — never jump-scared.

## Per-character speech

- **Callie** (dialogue): spare, level, declarative; deflects personal
  questions into facts; almost never asks questions; professional register
  as armor ("He was a historian, Sheriff. Academics disagree about
  everything. It's how they talk to each other.").
- **Gus Ralston**: warm patron's baritone; diminutives ("sweetheart,"
  "Good girl"); word-doubling ("Wonderful. Wonderful."); menace dressed as
  concern; never a direct order; warmth that can unscrew like a bulb.
- **Dalton Cross**: clipped procedural fragments; deposition cadence
  ("We clear?"); plain nouns (baggies, chain of custody); speech shortens
  under pressure.
- **Eli Townsend**: fast, breathless, reflexively apologetic; sentences
  started and abandoned; over-explains; accelerates as anxiety rises.
- **Mae Hartwell**: unhurried musical cadence; gardening/cooking metaphors;
  "honey" used sparingly; goes flat and metaphor-free when grave — the
  register drop IS the signal.
- **Wendell Pike**: academic hedge and self-correction; enthusiasm breaking
  through dashes; speaks in footnotes and caveats; "the pressure of the
  hand... it always matters."
- **Jacob Wren** (written voice only): Latin shorthand under pressure of
  haste; a private cipher "for the record"; frightened but determined.

## The motifs (deliberate repetition — never flag as crutch)

- **Bergamot tea** — remembrance disguised as habit; its taste tracks
  whether Callie is present in her own life (ch2 wrong, ch8 right again).
- **The floorboard** — grief as habit; stepped over for the whole book
  until ch8's deliberate step-on ("done being quiet for a ghost").
- **Rain on windows** — inside vs. outside; the safety reading is
  renegotiated by the end ("a thinner one... she was only listening to it").
- **Margins as voices** — the dead speak through what they wrote; the
  series' core mechanism.
- **Reading hands** — Callie reads people as documents; over-control, not
  tremor, is the tell (Gus canon: effortful stillness louder than any
  tremor; cracks only when being read).
- **Lavender** — Mae's signature; the smell that will not wash out.

## Boundary notes for future books

- POV never rotates mid-chapter and never leaves Callie.
- Historical material arrives only through documents Callie reads — no
  flashback scenes.
- No supernatural claims; Callie's readings are skill, demonstrated against
  authenticated samples, and are separated from legal conclusions.
- No cutesy banter, no romance-forward Cross dynamic, no thriller chase
  structure, no vague "handwriting feels wrong" solutions. `[series production constraints]`
