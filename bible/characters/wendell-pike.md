# Dr. Wendell Pike

## Desire (external)
Was about to publish a devastating historical exposé using marginalia evidence he'd
spent decades assembling — the book that would make his career and, he believed,
set the historical record straight.

## Need (internal)
To matter. To prove that a lifetime spent in dusty archives added up to
something the world would have to acknowledge.

## Wound / flaw
He was brilliant and completely alone. Decades of obsessive research replaced
every human connection. He understood Callie's gift because he shared it — and
he shared her isolation, taken to its extreme. He is the ghost of Callie's
future if she never steps out of the stacks.

## Contradictions
- He claimed to seek justice for the wronged dead, but he was also driven by
  vanity — he wanted the credit, the book, the recognition.
- He was kind and generous with knowledge, but emotionally walled off from
  everyone who tried to know him.

## Private shame / fear
That no one would care. That he'd spend his entire life proving something true
and the town would yawn and go back to its comfortable lies.

## Social mask
The eccentric professor. Tweed, mumbling, distracted, perpetually carrying
stacks of paper. People found him endearing but kept their distance — exactly
as he intended.

## Voice & mannerisms
Academic, tangential, prone to lecturing. He spoke in footnotes and asides,
qualifying everything. He never finished a sentence without adding a caveat.

## Body under stress
Fidgeted with his glasses, cleaned them compulsively. Pulled at his collar.
His hands shook when he was excited about a find — which was the only time he
showed visible emotion.

## Misreadings
He assumed the truth was enough — that if he published, justice would follow
automatically. He underestimated how far powerful people would go to suppress
it. He also misread the killer's warmth as genuine friendship.

## Appearance
Late sixties. Rumpled tweed, ink-stained shirt cuffs, unruly white hair. He
looked like a man who dressed from memory, not from mirrors. Perpetual
reading-glasses indentations on his nose.

## Arc
Dead before the story begins. His murder is the inciting incident. But his
presence lingers — through his research notes, his margin annotations, and the
trail of clues he left behind. Callie is effectively finishing his work, which
means finishing the thing that got him killed.

## Relationships
- **Callie Thorne:** Mutual respect, perhaps the closest thing either had to a
  real friendship. He came to her shop to discuss findings. She was one of the
  only people who understood his excitement. Neither of them called it
  friendship until it was too late.
- **Augustus Ralston (killer):** Pike trusted Gus. Gus patronized his research,
  funded the historical society. Pike didn't see the threat until the night he
  died.
- **Mae Hartwell:** She warned him to stop. He didn't listen.

## Recurring memories / images
- His unfinished manuscript, pages scattered across his desk at the time of
  death.
- A specific annotated book — the keystone document — that Callie must find.
- The last margin note he wrote, which becomes a clue only Callie can read.

## Things they refuse to say
- That he was lonely.
- That he was frightened in the weeks before his death — he'd started to sense
  he was being watched but told no one.

## Notes
- Pike functions as Callie's dark mirror: the same gift, the same isolation,
  taken to its terminal end. His death is a warning to her, narratively and
  thematically.
- The keystone annotated book he was working from is the central physical
  object of the mystery.
