### Chapter 1 — The Auction
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Beat:** Callie opens The Foxed Page — the shop, the back room, Eleanor's
  absence, bergamot tea, the magnifying glass. She walks to the Ridgeway
  Auction House for the rare-book auction. Old-money crowd; Gus Ralston holds
  court, warm and storytelling. Callie and Pike exchange a brief, understated
  greeting — colleagues, not quite friends. The auction begins. Then:
  disruption. Pike is found collapsed in a back office. Looks like a heart
  attack. Callie notices his cold cup of bergamot tea — identical to hers — and
  something cold settles in her. Sheriff Cross arrives, takes statements,
  initially treats it as natural causes. Eli Townsend is visibly shaken,
  over-explaining, unable to meet anyone's eyes.
- **Purpose:** Inciting incident. Establishes Callie's world and wound
  (Eleanor), plants the cozy register before pivoting to chill. Seeds: bergamot
  clue, Gus's warm mask, Eli's anxiety, Cross's procedural nature. The
  two-register pivot (signature move) gets its first full deployment.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Attend the auction for professional interest.
  - *Private want:* Connection — she's drawn to the bookish community, to Pike
    specifically, but won't name it.
  - *Opposition:* The murder shatters the evening. Her own emotional avoidance
    (she processes shock through cataloguing, not feeling).
  - *Turn:* Discovery of the body. Cozy evening becomes crime scene.
  - *Cost:* Pike is dead. The closest thing she had to a peer — and she never
    called it friendship.
  - *Aftertaste:* The cold cup of bergamot tea. A sensory ghost.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Gus:* Gus is expansive, anecdotal, asks about the shop.
    **Subtext:** he's gauging what she knows about Pike's research. Power
    imbalance: patron vs. quiet expert. She doesn't yet read the calculation
    behind the warmth.
  - *Callie & Eli:* Brief, awkward exchange. Eli over-explains something
    trivial. **Subtext:** his anxiety about the witnessed argument with Pike is
    already leaking. Callie registers it as odd, connects nothing yet.
  - *Callie & Cross:* Clipped statement-taking. **Subtext:** he sees a witness;
    she sees a man blind to context. Neither knows the other yet.
- **Memory / callback use:** Eleanor's magnifying glass (touched before
  leaving). Bergamot tea (Eleanor's ritual, Pike's cold cup). The creaking
  floorboard (stepped over out of habit). Weather established — rain (plant
  early; multiple witnesses should reference it consistently). The Foxed Page

### Chapter 2 — The Aftermath
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 2,800
- **Beat:** Callie processes the shock — going through motions in the shop,
  making tea that tastes wrong. Cross is going through the motions too, still
  leaning toward natural causes but taking statements. Eli is being pulled in
  as a person of interest (the witnessed argument). Callie goes to the Town
  Archives — she has a key, it's her dual home — and finds Pike's usual table
  undisturbed: manuscript pages, research notes, his annotated copies. But
  specific pages are missing, torn out cleanly. She knows what those pages
  contained — the most explicit findings about the Ralston fraud. Someone
  didn't just kill Pike; they tried to erase his work. The shift happens: she
  stops grieving and starts hunting.
- **Purpose:** Callie's entry into the investigation. Transitions from passive
  shock to active sleuthing. Establishes the archives as a key location with
  its own sensory register (cold stone, fluorescent hum, filing cabinets).
  Plants the missing-pages subplot thread. Begins seeding the moral weight —
  she knows what Pike uncovered and what pursuing it will cost.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Understand what happened to Pike. Honor his work.
  - *Private want:* The unspoken debt — she never called it friendship;
    finishing his work is how she pays that back.
  - *Opposition:* Someone got there first. The missing pages mean she's already
    behind. Also her own instinct to retreat, not engage.
  - *Turn:* The cleanly removed pages. This was deliberate. The murder and the
    erasure are the same act.
  - *Cost:* She can no longer stay uninvolved. The archives — her fortress — is

  - *Aftertaste:* The weight of knowing what Pike found, and knowing that

    Callie) collides with her terror of what Callie might uncover. First seeding

- **Memory / callback use:** The archives' sensory register (cold stone,

### Chapter 3 — The Trail
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Beat:** Day 3 — the toxicology confirms poison. It's officially a homicide.
  Cross shifts gears: zeroes in on Eli as prime suspect (the witnessed argument,
  the nervousness, no alibi), and warns Callie to stay out of an active murder
  investigation. Callie ignores him. She returns to the archives and begins
  retracing Pike's research path to the source — Jacob Wren's original margin
  notes in the foundational land records. First full decoding sequence: she
  cracks one of Wren's cipher notes and the meaning lands — the survey records
  were forged. True measurements hidden in the margins prove land was stolen and
  transferred to Ralston ownership. The fraud is real. This is what Pike died
  for. Meanwhile, Gus visits The Foxed Page — warm, folksy, bearing concern. He
  asks how she's holding up, reminisces about Pike, then gently, smoothly, asks
  whether Pike left any "papers or research" that ought to be "properly
  archived." Callie notices his hands: remarkably, unnaturally steady.
- **Purpose:** Rising action proper. Delivers the franchise's signature pleasure
  — the first full decoding moment (prose narrowing to ink and intention, the
  meaning clicking). Establishes the clue-escalation structure that will carry
  through the middle chapters. Cross's warning creates procedural friction and
  raises Eli's stakes (he's now a murder suspect, not a witness). Gus's visit
  plants the mask and the hands detail for later payoff. Callie's arc deepens —
  she's not just solving a murder now, she's uncovering the buried history she's
  spent her life guarding.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Decode Wren's notes and understand what Pike found.
  - *Private want:* To prove Pike's death mattered — that his work wasn't
    erased. The unspoken debt.
  - *Opposition:* Cross (stay out), Gus (the gentle surveillance), the cipher
    itself (Wren's notes are cryptic, layered, hard-won). Her own growing dread
    that each decoded note pulls her deeper into something that will cost her.
  - *Turn:* The first margin note breaks open. The fraud is real, documented,
    provable. Pike didn't die over nothing.
  - *Cost:* She can't unknow this. Each clue binds her tighter to the
    investigation and to the scandal.
  - *Aftertaste:* Gus's steady hands and his too-smooth question about Pike's
    papers. Something she can't name yet — a wrong note in a warm voice.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Cross:* He comes to warn her — clipped, serious, hat in hand.
    "This is a homicide now, Ms. Thorne. Not a research project."
    **Subtext:** he's protecting his investigation, but underneath, quietly
    worried about her. She hears rigidity; she doesn't hear the care. First real
    antagonism. She gives nothing away.
  - *Callie & Gus:* Gus is expansive, generous, storytelling. Asks about the
    shop, the archives equipment he funded. Then pivots — does Callie think
    Pike had "unfinished work" that might be "in unreliable hands"?
    **Subtext:** he's fishing for whether she's continuing the research,
    whether the threat is contained. He needs to know if he has to act again.
    Callie responds carefully — professional, guarded, revealing nothing about
    the archives visit. She reads his interest as a patron's concern. **Power
    imbalance:** he funds her equipment, her access. She doesn't yet understand
    the leash that implies.
- **Memory / callback use:** The decoding-moment signature move — first full
  deployment. Callie's brass magnifying glass in her hand as she reads Wren's
  cipher (Eleanor's tool, Eleanor's gift). The contrast between Wren's
  frightened-but-determined hand and Pike's urgent, frightened annotations
  layered on top — two voices across two centuries, both scared, both
  compelled. The archives' cold-stone register deepening into something darker
  now that the case is murder. Gus's steady hands planted for later payoff
  (tremor that cracks under pressure).

### Chapter 4 — The Escalation
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,400
- **Beat:** Callie decodes the critical margin note — Wren's most explicit
  cipher yet. It names Silas Ralston directly and describes Reverend
  Hartwell's murder: the staged riding accident, the magistrate bought. The
  full scope of the historical crime is clear. She understands now why Pike
  was killed — someone with a direct stake in keeping this buried. Then the
  walls start closing: she arrives at the archives and finds her key suspended
  — "pending review," a town council decision, ostensibly about preserving
  evidence. Clean, bureaucratic, deniable. Gus's invisible hand. Meanwhile
  Cross is closing in on Eli — the word around town is an arrest is coming.
  Then Mae comes. She invites Callie to Hartwell House — the kitchen, where
  every important conversation happens. Mae reveals she knows: her grandmother
  whispered the story once, with the door closed, as if the walls could hear.
  The Ralstons stole the land. Someone died for it. She doesn't know who killed
  Pike, but she knows why. And she begs Callie to stop — for her grandchildren
  playing on stolen ground, for the families who had no part in it, for the
  town itself. Callie can't say yes. She can't unknow what she's found. She
  says she can't stop. Mae's hands go still.
- **Purpose:** Midpoint turn. The full historical revelation lands (Silas named,
  Hartwell's murder documented). Gus escalates from warm surveillance to
  bureaucratic suppression. Eli's danger becomes acute — arrest imminent.
  Mae's plea is the thematic fulcrum: truth vs. protecting people. The moral
  dilemma is no longer abstract — it has a face, and it's in Mae's kitchen.
  Callie's arc pivots: she's now actively choosing between her code (honor the
  dead) and her community (protect the living). The first real fracture in the
  Callie-Mae relationship.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Decode the notes, understand the full scope, find the
    killer.
  - *Private want:* To serve justice for Pike without destroying everything she
    loves. A path that doesn't exist.
  - *Opposition:* Gus (bureaucratic suppression — cutting her access), Cross
    (arrest of Eli imminent), Mae (the moral plea harder to refuse than any
    threat). And herself — the growing certainty there is no clean path.
  - *Turn:* Mae's revelation and plea. The opposition becomes moral, not just
    procedural. Callie has to say no to the person she loves most.
  - *Cost:* The first fracture with Mae. She chooses truth over comfort, and it
    costs her the closest thing to family she has.
  - *Aftertaste:* Lavender on Mae's clothes as she leaves the kitchen. The scent
    she won't be able to wash out later.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Mae:* Mae's hands busy — stirring, kneading (her tic: still hands
    let the guilt in). She speaks in her unhurried cadence, gardening metaphors.
    Reveals the grandmother's story not as exposition but as confession. Her
    voice doesn't crack; it goes quiet — her real stress tell. **Subtext:** Mae
    is asking Callie to let her off the hook too — decades of complicit silence
    stay buried if Callie stops digging. But Mae doesn't fully recognize this
    about herself. Callie hears the plea as love, not self-preservation. The gap
    between what Mae says (protect the innocent) and what Mae needs (absolution
    for silence) is the chapter's deepest tension. Callie says she can't stop.
    Mae's hands go still. No anger — just recognition that everything has
    changed.
  - *Callie & Gus (indirect):* The archive suspension arrives through
    bureaucracy — a clerk, a letter, a phone call. Gus may appear briefly to
    commiserate: "I'm sure it's just temporary, Callie. They're being careful."
    **Subtext:** he's testing whether the pressure works, whether she'll fold.
    His warmth is now weaponized. She gives nothing.
- **Memory / callback use:** Mae's grandmother's whispered story — paid off from
  the private-memory seed in story memory (kitchen, door closed, as if the
  walls could hear). The Hartwell family Bible glimpsed on the parlor shelf

  "killed" decoded and the sentence snaps short. Eleanor's magnifying glass

### Chapter 5 — The Squeeze
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,400
- **Beat:** Callie's access to the archives is cut, but she's resourceful — she
  shifts to the Historical Society's collection, retracing where Pike found his
  keystone source. In the stacks funded by Gus Ralston, she finds it: a book
  annotated by Jacob Wren before he fled town — his most explicit cipher, laying
  out the full fraud and Silas Ralston's orchestration of Reverend Hartwell's
  murder. This is what Pike was working from. Cross-referencing the Historical
  Society's records, she finds the archive signature log — and Gus Ralston's
  name, signed in days before the murder. Then, in Pike's own marginal
  annotations on his surviving research notes, she finds his excited notation:
  showed preliminary findings to G.R. — very interested, asked to see the
  manuscript. Pike trusted him. Pike noted it the way academics note everything
  — blind to the danger. The chain locks: Gus saw what Pike found, signed in to
  assess the threat, then moved. Meanwhile Callie quietly resolves Eli's
  situation — she finds the book he was returning the night of the murder (a
  personal novel, reshelved wrong, left near the back office). Innocent. His
  alibi checks out, but Eli is too terrified to come forward with it himself.
  Then Gus comes to The Foxed Page after hours. He starts warm — concerned,
  paternalistic. Then his sentences shorten. He mentions the shop's lease, the
  equipment he funded, the people who depend on the town council's goodwill. He
  says he'd hate to see what happened to Wendell happen to someone else. His
  hands, always steady in public, are trembling. Callie sees it — and reads it
  the way she reads handwriting: the pressure beneath the control. She sees
  Silas Ralston looking out through the descendant's eyes. She is scared for the
  first time.
- **Purpose:** The squeeze — all sides tighten. Callie assembles the full
  evidentiary chain (keystone document + signature log + Pike's marginal note =
  Gus is the killer). Eli is cleared — the ticking clock becomes Callie's
  choice, not his vulnerability. Gus's mask drops for the first time. The
  confrontation makes the stakes personal and physical: she's no longer solving
  a puzzle, she's in a killer's crosshairs. The story pivots from investigation
  to decision.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Find the keystone document, assemble the evidence, clear
    Eli.
  - *Private want:* To find a way out that doesn't require destroying everything
    — a path that's already closing.
  - *Opposition:* Gus's threats (her shop, her funding, her life), Cross's
    impending arrest of Eli, her own fear. The realization that the only way to
    stop Gus requires the very thing that will wound everyone she loves.
  - *Turn:* Gus's confrontation. The mask drops. His hands tremble. She's
    looking at a killer who will act again.
  - *Cost:* Safety. The Foxed Page — her fortress — has been invaded as a threat
    space. She can no longer hide in the work.
  - *Aftertaste:* Fear. Real, physical fear for the first time. The back room
    where Eleanor died now feels like a place she could die too.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Gus (the confrontation):* Gus starts expansive — "Callie, you've
    been under such strain." Then shortens. The charm drains. He mentions the
    lease, the equipment, "people who don't know when to leave things alone." He
    references Pike without confessing: "Wendell didn't know when to stop either.
    Terrible thing." **Subtext:** he's telling her he killed Pike and will kill
    her. He never says it directly — the threat lives in the gaps, the pauses,
    the short clean sentences that have lost all warmth. Callie's response is
    minimal — she's cataloguing, reading him the way she reads a document.
    **Power shift:** for the first time, Callie sees the real Gus, and Gus sees
    that she sees. The tremor in his hands is the tell — the mask cracking at
    the edges. She recognizes the portrait's eyes in his face. Neither speaks
    the truth aloud. Both know the other knows.
- **Memory / callback use:** The keystone document — Wren's handwriting at its
  most desperate (margin notes as voices). The signature log (planted in
  world.md and story memory as evidence — paid off). Pike's marginal note about
  "G.R." — his trust made tragic in hindsight. The Foxed Page's back room
  inverting from sanctuary to threat space (triple weight: Eleanor's workspace,
  her deathplace, now a space of danger). Gus's trembling hands — the payoff of
  the steadiness planted in Chapter 3 (tremor that cracks under pressure). The
  Ralston portrait callback — Silas's eyes in Gus's face. Eleanor invoked by Gus
  as a weapon ("Your mother knew when to leave things alone").

### Chapter 6 — The Decision
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,500
- **Beat:** Morning. The word comes through the town grapevine: Cross is moving
  on Eli today. The arrest is happening. Callie has hours, maybe less. She's
  alone in The Foxed Page's back room — Eleanor's room. The evidence is spread
  across the oak table: Wren's decoded cipher, the keystone document, the
  archive signature log with Gus's name, Pike's marginal note about "G.R.," the
  reconstructed manuscript pages. She knows what entering this into evidence will
  do — not just to Gus, but to Mae, to the town, to every relationship she has.
  She opens the drawer and takes out Eleanor's reading glasses — left on this
  table the night she died. She puts them on. They don't fit. She puts them
  back. Then she sets the brass magnifying glass down on the table — the tool
  her mother gave her at nine, the instrument of her gift, the grip of Eleanor's
  ghost — and leaves it there. She gathers the evidence and walks to the
  sheriff's office. Cross doesn't want to hear it. His method — evidence in
  baggies, chain of custody, forensic procedure — has already built his case
  against Eli. Callie lays out the chain: the cipher, the fraud, the motive, the
  signature log, Pike's note, the bergamot. She shows him not just who killed
  Pike but why — and why his investigation was looking at the wrong man. Cross
  is quiet for a long time. He rubs the back of his neck (his tell for being out
  of his depth). He picks up the phone.
- **Purpose:** The moral and narrative climax. The impossible choice is made —
  Callie breaks her own code, using history not to preserve but to shatter. The
  Eleanor callbacks pay off: the reading glasses that don't fit (she can't be
  her mother), the magnifying glass set down (letting go of the ghost's grip).
  The Cross confrontation is the external climax — two worldviews colliding. His
  forensic rigidity versus her contextual intelligence. He's forced to confront
  that he nearly jailed an innocent man. The chapter ends with the machinery of
  justice in motion and the cost already landing.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Get to Cross before Eli is arrested. Present the evidence.
    Stop the wrong arrest.
  - *Private want:* To find some version of this that doesn't destroy
    everything. It doesn't exist.
  - *Opposition:* Time (Cross is moving on Eli today). Cross's pride and
    methodology (he doesn't want to be wrong). The moral weight (she knows what
    this will cost). Her own grief — letting go of Eleanor's ghost means stepping
    into the living world unprotected.
  - *Turn:* Setting down the magnifying glass. The internal climax — she chooses
    the living over the dead, truth over preservation, at devastating cost. Then
    the external turn: Cross picks up the phone.
  - *Cost:* Everything. The community's trust. Mae's friendship. The town's
    identity. Eleanor's ghost — released, which is both liberation and loss. Her
    own comfortable isolation — she's stepping into the world, and the world will
    wound her for it.
  - *Aftertaste:* The quiet after the phone call. The machinery in motion. The
    weight of what can't be undone.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Cross:* The scene's engine. Cross starts dismissive — "Ms. Thorne,
    I have a case." Callie doesn't argue; she presents. Document by document,
    cipher by cipher. She lets the evidence speak. Cross's resistance isn't
    corruption — it's the horror of realizing he was wrong. **Subtext:** his
    identity as a competent lawman is cracking. Every piece of Callie's evidence
    is also an indictment of his investigation. He doesn't thank her. He doesn't
    apologize. He goes quiet — and then he acts. The closest thing to respect he
    can offer is taking the evidence seriously. **Power shift:** Callie walks in
    as the amateur nuisance; she walks out as the person who solved his case.
    Neither of them is comfortable with that.
- **Memory / callback use:** Eleanor's reading glasses — taken from the drawer,
  put on, don't fit, put back (story memory: paid off). The brass magnifying
  glass set down on the table — the act of letting go (story memory: climax
  callback). The back room as triple-weighted space — Eleanor's workspace, her
  deathplace, the place where Callie makes the decision that changes everything.
  Rain on windows — inverted for the final time: rain on the back room windows
  as she works alone, no longer safe, the outside pressing in. Eleanor's line:
  "The truth is always there, Callie. Most people just don't look closely
  enough" — echoing in her mind with its heaviest weight yet: moral burden.
  Cross's tell — rubbing the back of his neck. The empty cell he keeps polished

### Chapter 7 — The Reckoning
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,000
- **Beat:** Cross moves. Gus is arrested at Ralston Hall — the estate built on
  the stolen land — calm, dignified, controlled to the end. No resistance, no
  scene. He's brought to the sheriff's office, where Callie is still waiting.
  Their eyes meet across the room. What she sees isn't hatred — it's
  bewilderment. The genuine incomprehension of a man who cannot fathom why the
  truth mattered more than the peace. He's led to the cell Cross has kept
  polished for years — finally occupied. Then the story breaks: the historical
  scandal goes public. The town's founding narrative collapses. Callie catches
  glimpses of the fallout — townspeople on the square whispering, a shopkeeper
  who won't meet her eye, the Historical Society's doors closed. The Ralston
  name becomes synonymous with what it was built on. Eli is cleared — Cross
  tells him quietly, and Eli doesn't know what to do with being believed. He
  looks at Callie and can't form the words. Cross finds Callie afterward. He
  doesn't apologize — that's not who he is. But he extends his hand. She takes
  it. It's small, and it's enormous. Then, that evening, a knock at The Foxed
  Page's door. Mae. She doesn't walk in — she knocks. She's holding soup. They
  look at each other. Not anger. Not forgiveness. Recognition that everything
  between them has changed. Mae says quietly that there's a Bible on her parlor
  shelf with her grandmother's handwriting in it — she could have shown Callie,
  could have shown Pike. She didn't. She doesn't ask for absolution. Callie
  doesn't offer it. Mae leaves. Callie smells lavender on her own clothes and
  can't wash it out.
- **Purpose:** Bittersweet resolution. The external plot resolves (killer
  caught, innocent cleared, justice served) while the emotional cost lands in
  full. Each relationship gets its final beat: Gus's bewilderment, Eli's
  wordless gratitude, Cross's handshake (professional equality hard-won), Mae's
  knock (love persisted, intimacy lost). The town itself is wounded — the
  community Callie feared losing is wounded by her hand, just as she foresaw.
  The theme resolves not with an answer but with the question intact: was it
  worth it? The story says yes — and the cost was real.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* None. The goal is accomplished. This is aftermath.
  - *Private want:* To know she did the right thing. She won't get confirmation
    — not from Mae, not from the town.
  - *Opposition:* The silence after. The looks that won't meet hers. The cost
    she can't undo.
  - *Turn:* Mae's knock. The soup still warm, the door now closed between them.
  - *Cost:* Fully paid. Trust, warmth, belonging — wounded, some of it
    permanently. The community she was trying to protect from the truth is hurt
    by the truth. She saved Eli and caught the killer, and the town flinches
    when she walks by.
  - *Aftertaste:* Lavender that won't wash out. The warmth of the handshake
    fading. The quiet of a solved case that doesn't feel like a victory.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Gus (the last look):* No dialogue. Just the look across the
    sheriff's office. **Subtext:** two people who guarded the same history for
    opposite reasons, finally seeing each other clearly. His bewilderment is the
    most honest thing he's ever shown her. She holds it without flinching.
  - *Callie & Cross (the handshake):* Minimal words. Maybe: "Good work, Ms.
    Thorne." Clipped, uncomfortable, sincere in spite of itself. The handshake
    replaces the apology he can't make. **Subtext:** he's shaking her hand and
    indicting himself — he almost jailed an innocent man. The gesture is
    professional equality and personal reckoning in one.
  - *Callie & Eli:* Eli can't speak. Maybe a nod, a look. **Subtext:** no one
    has ever been on his side. He doesn't have the language for gratitude
    because he's never needed it. The seed of his arc — he'll start introducing
    himself to customers soon. Not yet. But soon.
  - *Callie & Mae (the knock):* Spare, devastating. Mae acknowledges the
    Hartwell Bible — not as confession or justification, just truth. "I could
    have shown you. I didn't." Callie doesn't respond with comfort or anger. She
    receives it. **Subtext:** Mae is choosing honesty for the first time in
    decades — but it doesn't heal the fracture, it deepens it. The knocking is
    the new shape of their love: present, but guarded. Mae still cares enough to
    bring soup. She cares too much to walk in.
- **Memory / callback use:** The empty cell Cross kept polished — finally
  occupied (paid off). Gus's bewilderment — a man who doesn't understand why
  truth mattered more than peace (paid off). The handshake — first gesture of
  professional equality, small and enormous (paid off). Mae's knock — she knocks
  now where she used to walk in (paid off). The Hartwell family Bible —
  acknowledged at last, not as evidence but as confession. Lavender on Callie's
  clothes that won't wash out (paid off). Eli's seed of hope — too early to
  bloom, but planted. The town as chorus — glimpses of the wounded community.
  Callie's ink stains — she's stopped scrubbing them (acceptance).

### Chapter 8 — After
