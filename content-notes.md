# Content Notes — Murder Most Annotated

(Consolidated from sensitivity-reader passes; accumulated across chapters for KDP listing surfacing.)

## Chapter 1 — The Auction
- Death on-page (Pike found dead; killing not witnessed; aftermath depicted, non-graphic)
- Poison (implied murder method — the bergamot tea; strongly signaled, not explicitly stated in-chapter)
- Grief / bereavement (Eleanor, two years prior; depicted as unprocessed; Pike's death triggers a grief response)
- Anxiety depiction (Eli — portrayed as a recognized condition, not stigmatized; flag for readers who may find the misreading-of-anxiety-as-guilt uncomfortable before later subversion)
- Body discovery scene (clinical, non-graphic)

## Chapter 2 — The Aftermath
- Grief and bereavement (depicted via dissociation, sensory disruption, embarrassed hunger — the tea tasting "wrong")
- Death aftermath referenced (deceased's belongings, scent, untouched workspace) — non-graphic
- Implication of murder and deliberate evidence destruction/erasure (the cleanly torn pages)
- Law-enforcement character framing a vulnerable person's anxiety as guilt (may land for readers with anxiety disorders or experience of police profiling)

## Chapter 3 — The Trail
- Homicide confirmed (medical examiner finding stated on-page)
- Poison referenced (in tea — method of murder)
- Law-enforcement intimidation of an amateur sleuth (sheriff's implicit arrest-threat)
- Low-level dread / fear of a male benefactor; after-hours uninvited visit; patronizing physical contact (shoulder touch) and gendered diminishment ("Good girl") from an older, wealthier male character the protagonist is financially beholden to
- Grief (dead colleague; intrusive memory of deceased mother)

## Chapter 4 — The Escalation
- Historical premeditated murder (minister, staged as riding accident, 1840s) — documented on-page via cipher
- Judicial corruption / bought magistrate (historical)
- Contemporary bureaucratic suppression under color of procedure (archive access revoked)
- Psychological intimidation and gendered paternalism ("Good girl," unwanted touch, implied threat) from a wealthier male patron
- Emotional coercion / moral plea to suppress truth
- Low-level dread throughout
- Reference to imminent wrongful arrest of a vulnerable young man
- Grief / loss (Eleanor, referenced via the glass)
