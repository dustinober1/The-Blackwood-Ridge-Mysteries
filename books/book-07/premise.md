# Premise — The Reckoning

## Commercial hook

When a conservator is killed inside a heritage center's powered shelving, Callie Thorne must prove that a celebrated civic treasure was assembled from genuine pages into a false history. At the same time, she sets a controlled provenance trap for the person who has been deciding which truths reach her—and discovers that her own apprentice learned her method too well.

## Standalone mystery promise

The immediate mystery is a complete fair-play cozy murder rooted in preservation, accession records, mechanical archive systems, grant compliance, and the dangerous authority of a coherent provenance story.

The central object is the **Blackwood Civic Petition Book**, publicly described as an intact 1912 volume documenting one town-wide petition campaign. Its five leaves are genuine, but they come from five different civic efforts between 1911 and 1913. Their signatures and text are not forged. The lie is their assembly, sequence, binding, catalog history, and representation as one surviving artifact.

Independent conservator **Imogen Price** recognizes the false unity through mismatched sewing stations, incompatible edge patterns, differing paper formats, displaced accession numbers, and guard-sheet chronology. She intends to stop the exhibit and notify the state grant office. Executive director **Vivienne Croft**, who assembled the composite and certified its unified provenance, kills her using the compact shelving's maintenance override.

## Long-arc promise

Callie's Book 6 question—who knew which page she would open next?—becomes testable. She does not accuse on intuition. With Cross aware of the plan and its limits, she creates one private, reversible, explicitly unverified shop catalog path that cannot affect the homicide or any innocent person.

Eli moves a genuine item to make the false path true.

That response does not stand alone. Book 7 combines it with independent evidence that Eli:

- controlled the Book 5 repair-drawer interval;
- authored the mismatched Mercer routing slip;
- had documented post-image/pre-discovery access opportunities for the Book 6 hosts;
- processed the atlas route that delivered the marked road pamphlet;
- used the unpublished apprenticeship comparison order;
- accessed and printed the relevant source sequence under his staff credentials;
- repeatedly converted authentic gaps into paths designed for Callie.

The proof establishes deliberate curation and redirection beginning in Book 5. It does not claim he created Books 1–4, manufactured a killer's guilt, forged decisive evidence, or contaminated police custody.

## Emotional promise

Callie must confront the resemblance between Eli's manipulation and her own controlled test. The distinction is real—her test is lawful, reversible, bounded, documented, and incapable of harming an innocent person—but it does not permit moral self-congratulation.

She loses an apprentice she loved as family without losing the truth of what he did.

Mae offers food, presence, and boundaries, not universal forgiveness. Cross preserves authority and consequences. Eli's need to belong explains his conduct without excusing it. The book ends with the relationship ruptured and Book 8's final choice still alive.

## Distinctiveness lock

The book does not repeat:

- auction poisoning;
- botanical cipher confession;
- academic challenge;
- fire concealment;
- simulated suicide note;
- brass map weight or staged ladder fall.

Its central mechanics are a false composite made from genuine leaves, a powered compact-shelving homicide, controller and access logs, grant-provenance fraud, and a controlled catalog-route test.
