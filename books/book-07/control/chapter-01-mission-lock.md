# Chapter 1 Mission Lock — The Petition Book

## Dominant function

Establish Book 7’s immediate murder case and its thematic problem: five genuine leaves can be arranged into one false historical object. Introduce Imogen Price as a living, skilled, opinionated professional; establish the Blackwood Civic Petition Book, the Heritage Center, the principal suspect pressures, and Callie’s continuing provenance unease; then close on Imogen’s restrained death-scene aftermath under Cross’s authority without interpreting the shelving system.

## Opening state

- **Date:** Thursday, June 10, 2027.
- **Time:** Approximately 3:35 p.m.
- **Location:** The Foxed Page.
- Early-summer humidity has built ahead of an evening thunderstorm.
- Callie remains a bounded county records consultant and publicly trusted documentary specialist.
- Her private route ledger exists in a locked shop drawer. The identity fields remain blank, and she does not name or test anyone.
- Mae and Callie operate in their practical, permission-based new normal.
- Eli is visibly useful, trusted, increasingly skilled, careful with source and custody distinctions, and willing to leave unknown fields blank.

## Chapter objectives

1. Open at approximately 3:35 p.m. at The Foxed Page.
2. Introduce Imogen Price alive as an independent paper conservator and grant-compliance reviewer with professional judgment, dry humor, preferences, and limits.
3. Establish the Blackwood Civic Petition Book as the Heritage Center’s celebrated purportedly intact 1912 volume.
4. Establish Imogen’s concern that the object may be a composite without solving all five source histories.
5. Show why Callie agrees to a narrow, record-specific observation under Heritage Center custody.
6. Keep Eli at The Foxed Page after 4:12 p.m. and inside ordinary apprentice duties.
7. Log Callie and Imogen into the Heritage Center exhibit lab at 4:24 p.m.
8. Conduct the controlled review between 4:31 and 5:16 p.m.
9. Make reader-visible the five leaves’ incompatible sewing stations.
10. Make reader-visible one paper edge corresponding to an authenticated photograph from a separate source box.
11. Make reader-visible remnants of different accession numbers.
12. Establish Asha Benton’s anxiety about catalog dates without making her sinister.
13. Establish Julian Voss’s demand concerning a family signature through an attributed message or exhibit request.
14. Establish Celia Pryor’s pressure for Imogen’s findings through an attributed message or call.
15. Establish Theo Lanier’s resentment concerning safety or compliance and his knowledge of the shelving override system.
16. Establish Vivienne Croft as competent, pressured, human, and aware of Imogen’s planned Box `B-17` check.
17. Have Callie recognize that genuine components can be arranged into a false whole without making a fraud, grant, or legal conclusion.
18. End Callie’s written observation scope and have her leave at 5:31 p.m.
19. Keep Imogen’s 5:41 voice note, the 5:43–5:51 death window, and the fatal act outside Callie’s POV and off-page.
20. Route Asha’s 6:18 discovery, the 6:22 emergency call, and Cross’s 6:31 scene control through attributed information and visible aftermath.
21. Return Callie only under Cross’s narrow identification-and-room-description authorization.
22. Keep Bell in control of scene documentation and Callie behind a marked threshold.
23. End no later than 6:52 p.m. with a still-visible maintenance indicator that Callie does not interpret.

## Success condition

By the chapter end, the reader understands:

- who Imogen is and why her loss matters;
- why the Heritage Center values the petition book;
- why Imogen doubts the object’s claimed historical continuity;
- that the leaves and signatures may be genuine even if the unified object is false;
- that five distinct suspect pressures exist without identifying a killer;
- that Imogen intended to verify Box `B-17`;
- that Vivienne knew the exact review plan;
- that Theo understands maintenance override operation;
- that Callie remained within written scope and handled no original leaf;
- that Imogen died after Callie left and the fatal act occurred off-page;
- that Cross and Bell control the scene;
- that the shelving end panel still displays a maintenance condition whose meaning remains unresolved.

## Failure conditions

The chapter fails if it:

- reveals Vivienne as the killer;
- reveals the commissioning bridge or exact maintenance-override method;
- explains the complete controller cycle;
- proves grant fraud or states a legal fraud conclusion;
- identifies all five final source histories;
- recovers or reveals Imogen’s complete assembly worksheet;
- designs, activates, or discusses the controlled false-discovery test;
- identifies Eli as curator or gives him suspicious private knowledge;
- uses an Eli POV, guilt-coded behavior, or coded confession;
- places Eli at the Heritage Center or gives him an original, police evidence, case access, route theory, or test knowledge;
- makes Imogen a clue-delivery mechanism rather than a living professional;
- villain-codes Vivienne or makes any suspect obviously guilty;
- lets Callie handle an original leaf, move the petition book, direct a custodian, or make a mechanical, medical, homicide, grant, or legal conclusion;
- lets Cross surrender scene authority or Bell surrender documentation control;
- gives Callie knowledge of the 5:41 voice note or exact death interval without an attributed source;
- places the fatal act on-page;
- removes, separates, recatalogs, or deaccessions any leaf;
- drafts any Chapter 2 prose;
- uses Book 8’s warning, future murder, or final moral choice.

### Procedure and custody

The Blackwood Civic Petition Book remains on its cradle under Heritage Center custody. A Heritage Center custodian controls all physical handling and page turns. Callie signs a record-specific written observation scope, uses only authorized tools, handles no original leaf, and makes no grant, fraud, mechanical, medical, homicide, or legal conclusion.

After the discovery, Cross controls the scene, emergency response, witnesses, access, official language, investigative decisions, and all later legal steps. Bell controls photographs, room documentation, and the marked route. Callie first supplies a timed witness account, then receives one narrow authorization to identify the petition book and describe the earlier room arrangement from behind a marked threshold. She touches nothing and asks no witness questions.
