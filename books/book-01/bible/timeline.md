# Timeline

## Deep backstory

- **1780s:** Blackwood Ridge founded. Original land grants issued to
  settling families, including the Hartwells. The Ralstons arrive later and
  establish themselves through trade and milling.

- **1840s:** **Silas Ralston** orchestrates a land fraud, forging and
  invalidating original land grants to seize hundreds of acres from several
  families. **Jacob Wren**, the town surveyor, is coerced into creating
  falsified survey records but secretly encodes the true measurements and
  details of the fraud in margin notes across foundational documents and a
  family Bible. Wren flees town in fear after completing the annotations.

- **1843:** **Reverend Thomas Hartwell** (Mae's ancestor, the town minister)
  discovers the fraud and confronts Silas Ralston. Days later, he is found
  dead — thrown from a horse on a familiar road. Ruled a riding accident
  through Ralston influence over the local magistrate. The Hartwells and
  other affected families lose their land. The Ralston fortune is built on
  the theft.

- **1840s–present:** The fraud calcifies into accepted history. The Ralstons
  become the town's benefactors. The Hartwells remain in town but diminished,
  eventually rebuilding modestly. Oral fragments of the truth survive in
  whispers — Mae's grandmother hears the story and passes it to Mae once.

- **~1985:** Eleanor Thorne establishes The Foxed Page as Blackwood Ridge's
  antiquarian bookshop and begins serving as the unofficial town archivist.
  Callie is born.

- **~1994 (Callie is ~9):** Eleanor gives Callie the brass magnifying glass
  and teaches her to read margin notes. The seed of Callie's gift.

- **~2024 (two years before story-present):** Eleanor Thorne dies alone in the
  back room of The Foxed Page — natural causes, a book open on her lap. Callie
  inherits the shop, the archival role, and the unprocessed grief. She buries
  herself in the work.

## Recent backstory (months before story-present)

- **~6 months before:** Dr. Wendell Pike, reclusive town historian,
  approaches Callie at The Foxed Page. He's been decoding Jacob Wren's margin
  notes in the town archives and believes they describe a massive historical
  fraud and a covered-up murder. He's excited. Callie is intrigued but
  cautious. They begin an informal collaboration — he shares findings; she
  helps with the harder cipher passages. Neither calls it friendship.

- **~3 months before:** Pike discovers the keystone document — an annotated
  book that contains Wren's most explicit description of Reverend Hartwell's
  murder and Silas Ralston's orchestration of the fraud. He begins assembling
  a manuscript for publication.

- **~6 weeks before:** Pike starts behaving erratically — paranoid, checking
  over his shoulder. He tells Callie he feels watched. He doesn't say by whom.
  Callie doesn't press. (She will regret this.)

- **~2 weeks before:** Gus Ralston learns of Pike's manuscript through his
  influence over the Historical Society and the archives. He reads excerpts.
  He realizes the scandal is about to break. He makes the decision to kill
  Pike and silence the story permanently.

- **~1 week before:** Eli Townsend has a public argument with Pike at the
  Ridgeway Auction House — Pike accused him of misfiling a rare document. It
  was minor, but witnessed. Eli is rattled.

## Story-present (chronological)

- **Night 1 — The auction:** The rare book auction at the Ridgeway Auction
  House. The town's old-money crowd is in attendance, including Gus Ralston.
  Callie attends for professional interest. At some point during the evening,
  Pike's body is discovered — dead, in a back office of the auction house.
  The auction stops. Sheriff Cross arrives. Eli Townsend is visibly shaken and
  evasive. **Opening — inciting incident.**

- **Day 2 — Aftermath:** Callie processes the shock. Cross begins his
  investigation — focused on physical evidence and obvious suspects. Eli
  becomes a person of interest due to the witnessed argument. Callie visits
  the crime scene (or the archives) and begins to think about Pike's
  research — what was he about to publish? She realizes the murder may be
  connected to the scandal he was uncovering. **Callie's entry into the
  investigation.**

- **Days 3–5 — The trail:** Callie retraces Pike's research path through the
  town archives. She begins decoding Wren's margin notes — each one revealing
  another layer of the fraud. Cross warns her to stay out of the
  investigation; she doesn't. Mae brings soup and asks careful questions. Gus
  stops by the shop, warm and concerned — and Callie notices his hands are
  steady, his smile perfect, his interest a little too specific. **Rising
  action — clues accumulate, friction with Cross builds.**

- **Days 6–8 — Escalation:** Callie finds a critical margin note that names
  Silas Ralston explicitly and describes Reverend Hartwell's murder. She
  realizes the scope of what Pike found — and why someone killed him. Gus
  begins to lean on her indirectly (through the town council, through
  cutting her archive access). Cross zeros in on Eli; an arrest feels
  imminent. Mae realizes what Callie is tracking and confronts her — begging
  her to stop. **Midpoint — the moral dilemma crystallizes. Callie knows the
  truth is devastating and the wrong person is about to go down for murder.**

- **Days 9–11 — The squeeze:** Callie narrows the suspect list. She finds the
  keystone annotated document (possibly at the Historical Society, hidden in
  plain sight in the collection Gus funded). She's now certain Gus is the
  killer — but proving it means entering the archive into evidence, which
  exposes the scandal. Gus escalates to direct threat — a confrontation that
  makes clear he will kill her. Callie is scared for the first time. **The
  squeeze — stakes tighten from all sides: the killer, the sheriff, the moral
  cost.**

- **Day 12 — The decision:** Callie makes her choice. She cannot let Eli take
  the fall. She cannot let Gus bury the truth. She enters the full archive
  into evidence — the marginalia, the keystone document, the decoded
  manuscript. She goes to Cross with everything. He's forced to act. **Climax
  setup — the impossible choice is made.**

- **Day 13 — Resolution:** Gus is arrested. The scandal breaks publicly. The
  town's narrative shatters. Mae's family — and others — face the fallout.
  Callie has solved the case and caught the killer, but the cost is real:
  fractured relationships, lost trust, the community wounded. Eli is cleared.
  Cross shakes Callie's hand. **Resolution — bittersweet.**

- **Epilogue — After:** A quiet scene in The Foxed Page. Callie alone in the
  back room. She sets Eleanor's magnifying glass down on the table — then
  picks it up again. She's lost the fortress of isolation but kept the gift.
  The shop is still standing. She's still here. The doorbell rings — a
  customer, or maybe Mae with soup, knocking this time. **Closing image —
  wounded but alive, isolated but not permanently.**

## Open timeline questions

- **The keystone document's exact location:** Historical Society collection?
  Hidden in The Foxed Page's own rare shelves? Pike's personal effects? Decide
  during outline.
- **Gus's method:** How did he kill Pike? Poison (quiet, controlled,
  premeditated)? Staged as a fall or heart attack at the auction? The method
  should reflect Gus's character — deliberate, clean, deniable. Resolve during
  outline.
- **The missing manuscript pages:** Gus took some but not all of Pike's
  manuscript. Which pages, and what do they contain? This is a clue trail to
  design during outline.
- **How Callie proves Gus specifically (not just the scandal):** The marginalia
  proves the historical crime, but how does it connect to Pike's murder? There
  needs to be a present-tense clue that links Gus directly — possibly his
  presence in the archive signature log, or a margin note Pike wrote about his
  last conversation with "G.R." Resolve during outline.
- **Eli's alibi:** He was in the building the night of the murder (returning a
  borrowed book). How does Callie verify his innocence without exposing his
  technically incriminating behavior? Resolve during outline.
