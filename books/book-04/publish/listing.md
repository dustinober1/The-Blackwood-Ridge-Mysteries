## Ideal blurb direction

The eventual blurb should open on the archive fire and the formal Cross/Callie ask. It should make the commercial promise clear in the first paragraph: the town archive burns, a retired clerk is dead, and the record she tried to save points toward Cross's own predecessor.

The middle of the blurb should emphasize the book-specific mystery device: damaged records, water lines, duplicate cards, an old night ledger, a forged note, and Callie's ability to read what remains. The emotional hook should be Callie moving from useful outsider to reluctant consultant while Cross has to decide whether procedure means protecting the office or correcting it.

The final movement should promise fair-play cozy closure: a resolved case, restrained stakes, and deeper series arc without a cliffhanger.

## Commercial hook line direction

Potential hook shapes to refine later:

1. The archive burned. The record did not stay silent.
2. Cross finally asks Callie for help — just as the records point back at his own office.
3. Some secrets survive fire because someone made a duplicate.
4. Callie Thorne can read a margin. This time she has to read what the smoke left behind.
5. Blackwood Ridge's records are damaged. Its silence is not.
