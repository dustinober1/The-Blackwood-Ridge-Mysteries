# Ruth Mallory

## Function

Ruth Mallory is the Book 4 victim and the ethical record-keeper whose habits let Callie reconstruct the truth after the archive is damaged.

## External desire before death

Correct the record she has found before someone smooths it away again.

## Internal pressure

Ruth spent her career believing that a careful duplicate was not paranoia but duty. She is not sentimental about records. She is faithful to them.

## Personality

Prickly, precise, allergic to flattery, difficult to charm. She is the kind of woman who alphabetizes emergency forms and corrects a mayor's punctuation without noticing he is embarrassed.

## Voice and mannerisms

- Crisp sentences.
- Rare praise, but exact when offered.
- Refers to records by box, range, and accession number rather than general names.
- Taps a finger once beside a line when someone is missing the point.
- Does not waste adjectives.

## Handwriting

Narrow, upright, inventory-clean. Small hooked sevens. No decorative loops. Dates are always complete. Equipment or shelf references are exact. This is why the fake note does not hold.

## Relationships

- **Callie:** Ruth knew Eleanor and regards Callie as capable but too solitary. Her last call trusts Callie with the phrase "a shelf that lied twice."
- **Cross:** Respects his office but does not defer to it blindly.
- **Eli:** Would likely have frightened him at first, then respected his carefulness.
- **Ben Calder:** Treats civic charm as a form of fog.

## Clue legacy

- Ruth's call.
- Ruth's pocket card.
- Ruth's duplicate protocol.
- Ruth's real indexing habits.
- Ruth's refusal to let a smooth file stay smooth.

## Drafting note

Ruth should feel alive through systems, not flashbacks alone. Let the reader know her through how she labeled, duplicated, objected, corrected, and refused to let a box pretend it was whole.
