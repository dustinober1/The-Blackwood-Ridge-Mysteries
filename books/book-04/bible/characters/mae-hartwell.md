# Mae Hartwell — Book 4

## State entering

Mae and Callie are in a slow thaw. Mae knocks where she once walked in. The relationship is not healed, but it can now bear small work together.

## External desire

Help Callie and the town preserve what can be preserved.

## Internal need

To offer help without trying to manage the truth, Callie's grief, or the town's response. Mae has to let practical love be enough for now.

## Book 4 function

- Brings practical supplies and emotional steadiness to the sorting table.
- Offers memory of Lila Crowe and the Calder family atmosphere in 1991.
- Helps Callie accept help in Eleanor's space.
- Represents the town's warmth without asking Callie to soften the truth.

## Arc

Mae begins with the old impulse to feed and soothe. She realizes the better gift is work: towels, labels, hands, and memory offered without control. By the end, her thaw with Callie advances because neither of them names it too loudly.

## Voice notes

Keep Mae's cadence warm and precise. Avoid caricature. Gardening and cooking metaphors are allowed, but they should carry sharp observation. Her stress tell remains still hands and dimmed warmth.

## Relationship movement

- **Callie:** Shared work replaces apology. Mae enters the Foxed Page back room by permission.
- **Cross:** She can still move him, but Book 4 should not require Mae to unlock him. Cross must choose the record himself.
- **Eli:** Treats Eli as belonging at the table, which quietly advances his sense of home.
- **Lila Crowe:** Mae's memory humanizes Lila and the town's old silence without turning Mae into the source of proof.

## Sensory marker

Lavender remains present but thinner, mixed now with damp wool, paper, bread, and work.
