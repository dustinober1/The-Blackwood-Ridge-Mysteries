# Eli Townsend — Book 4

## State entering

Eli is Callie's Saturday apprentice and increasingly steady. In Book 3 he helped by making copies and staying out of the room where he did not belong. He is learning that usefulness does not have to become apology.

## External desire

Help Callie reconstruct damaged records without making a mistake that costs the case.

## Internal need

To trust that belonging can be quiet. He does not have to solve the case, prove himself in a dramatic way, or apologize for taking up space.

## Book 4 function

Eli learns archival reconstruction:

- duplicate indexing;
- reading damaged labels;
- carbon impressions and transfer traces;
- basic evidence-boundary discipline;
- how to make copies without changing the record;
- how to be useful while staying inside the role Callie gives him.

## Arc

Eli starts eager and careful, afraid of mishandling damaged records. He grows into a steadier assistant whose small noticing helps Callie understand Ruth's duplicate sequence. He does not solve the case for her. He helps make the record visible enough that Callie and Cross can act.

## Relationship movement

- **Callie:** Student / apprentice. She teaches him more directly in Book 4, but also draws boundaries.
- **Cross:** Cross sees him as helpful but not official. This reinforces the amateur-sleuth lane.
- **Mae:** Mae treats him as someone who belongs at the salvage table, which matters more to him than praise.

## Long-arc constraint

Do not surface Eli as a steerer or manipulator. Do not let him plant records, guide Callie invisibly, or become too competent too quickly. Book 4 only grows the skill that will later make the long arc believable.

## Open thread

His father's line — "Call when you've figured things out" — remains unpaid. It may be echoed quietly if Eli feels, for the first time, that he has figured out one small thing.
