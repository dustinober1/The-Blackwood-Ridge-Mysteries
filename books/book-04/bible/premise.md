# Premise — The Archive Fire

## One-paragraph premise

When the Blackwood Ridge Town Archives burn days after the events of *The Challenger*, Callie Thorne loses more than shelves and paper: one of the women who knew the archive before Callie inherited it is found dead below Town Hall. Sheriff Dalton Cross formally asks Callie to help reconstruct what was lost, but the salvage table reveals the damage was aimed at one old night ledger — a record tying Cross's predecessor to a decades-old wrong that a present-day town leader is still desperate to keep buried.

## Series slot

Book 4 is the formal partnership book. Book 3 opened the door when Cross saw Callie's reading become actionable. Book 4 walks through it: he asks her for help before he has solved the frame, and he stands beside the reconstructed record even when it implicates the Sheriff's Office history.

## Case engine

- **Present engine:** Ruth Mallory's death during the archive fire.
- **Archive engine:** Reconstruction of smoke- and water-damaged records, duplicate cards, carbon impressions, and old night-ledger fragments.
- **Historical engine:** The 1991 Lila Crowe hit-and-run and the suppressed statement that protected Benedict Calder.
- **Emotional engine:** Callie must share the work of preservation, Cross must correct the institutional record, Mae must help without trying to manage Callie's grief, and Eli must learn to be useful without becoming the answer.

## Title candidates and decision

| Candidate | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| The Archive Fire | Selected. Direct, atmospheric, already seeded in the series arc. |
| The Ash Ledger | Strong clue-object feel, but less clear as a series title. |
| The Burned Index | Elegant but slightly colder and less commercial. |
| The Charred Folio | Good archival specificity but too object-focused. |
| The Reconstruction | Thematically accurate but less mystery-forward. |
| The Smoke Register | Atmospheric, but could sound softer than the case. |
| The Night Ledger | Strong clue title, but Book 4 should center the archive wound first. |

**Decision:** Use **The Archive Fire**.

## Theme statement

The novel is about reconstruction after damage. Saving the record is not the same as keeping it untouched. Sometimes the honest work is admitting what burned, drying what remains, and letting other hands help you read the pieces.

## Reader promise

A fair-play atmospheric cozy mystery where the solution depends on records, indexing habits, damaged paper, institutional language, and Callie's ability to read both written evidence and living evasions. No supernatural turn, no thriller escalation, no serial-killer framing, and no retcon of Books 1-3.

## Ending state target

- Ruth Mallory's case resolved.
- Lila Crowe's old record corrected.
- Benedict Calder arrested / exposed as the Book 4 culprit.
- Roland Tavis's predecessor failure named without turning him into the present culprit.
- Cross and Callie become formal working partners.
- Mae and Callie's thaw advances through shared work.
- Eli gains reconstruction skill but the future steering thread remains hidden.
- The archives remain damaged but not gone.
