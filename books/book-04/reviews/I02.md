# Developmental Editor (I02) — Book 4: The Archive Fire
**Entry point:** Series (read Books 1–3)  **Format read:** print (manuscript)
**Stars:** ★★★★☆  **Would read next book?** yes  **Would recommend?** yes
**DNF point (if any):** none

## Gut reaction (3–5 sentences, in the editor's voice)
A procedural mystery that treats records as living things rather than set dressing. Callie's expertise has earned her a formal foothold this time around — the "bounded partnership" is now written down, and Cross expands those boundaries as the case widens, rather than retreating into gatekeeping. The series formula (body found → Callie reads documents → Cross constraints → Eli lesson → arrest) is recognizable by now, but the *scale* has grown: a present-day murder tied to a 35-year-old hit-and-run cover-up that implicates the town's civic architecture itself. My only structural objection is that the suspect pool collapses to one name too early, which robs the mystery of its second-half tension even while the procedure dazzles.

## What worked (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Midpoint pivot:** Ch 3:1601-1606 — "the fire didn't create the lie, it tried to make the lie unreadable." The investigation shifts from archive fire aftermath to murder-concealment.
- **Stakes escalation:** Ch 1:445-446 (bad luck) → Ch 4:1781-1787 (death investigation) → Ch 4:2986-3003 (Tavis's "kept the town whole") → Ch 8:4097-4111 (record correction across decades). The historical cover-up is genuinely woven, not pasted on.
- **Bounded partnership:** Ch 1:427-440 (written boundaries) → Ch 3:1109-1119 (Cross: "tell me where to begin") → Ch 8:4163-4168 (formal consultant arrangement as "case by case, boundaries written"). Cross *expands* trust, not just tests it.
- **Callie's pariah evolution:** Ch 1:47 ("useful, then. Not welcome") shows progression from Book 1's pure shunning. By Ch 8:4151-4166 she's offered a formal, documented consultant role — integration earned, not granted.
- **Eli's slow-burn:** Ch 1:63-66 (hands beginning to believe him) → Ch 2:533-535 (no apology, restraint) → Ch 6:3137-3152 (blank eighth line = best structural clue) → Ch 6:3637-3639 (relief that usefulness fits inside rules). His arc is properly incremental.

- **Ash index concept:** Ch 7:3369-3384 — "An ash index... a way to search what the fire tried to make unsearchable." The metaphor serves the procedure.

## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Suspect pool collapse:** By Ch 3:1601-1606 the Tavis/Crowe/B.C. path points squarely at Benedict Calder. B.C. = brought in by father (Ch 5:3347-3349), father present (Ch 5:3348), and Calder is the only living person with both 1991 access and present-day archive control (Ch 6:2820-2822, Ch 7:3497-3498). The mystery's tension leaks out before the halfway mark.
- **Formula visibility:** Ch 1:48-49 (shop becomes "evidence way station") and Ch 6:2820-2822 (explicit statement that Ben remains "protected by the gap") foreground the series rhythm so loudly that it reads as awareness rather than surprise. M08's structuralist concern is warranted.
- **Language-parallel evidence:** Ch 7:3580-3586 (fake note vs. Ben's statement) is clever, but the habit comparison arrives after the suspect is locked (Ch 3), so it confirms rather than convinces.
- **Eli's revelation timing:** Ch 6:3137-3152 (blank eighth line) is the book's best structural clue, but it confirms a killer already named rather than redirecting suspicion.

## Public review draft (the review this editor would actually post, 80–250 words)
Vesper Blythe's fourth Blackwood Ridge mystery deepens the series' central tension — records as living truth vs. curated comfort — while widening its scope from a single murder to a decades-old cover-up. When retired clerk Ruth Mallory dies in an archive fire after reporting that a shelf "lied twice," antiquarian bookseller Callie Thorne steps into the breach with Sheriff Dalton Cross's written boundaries and his reluctant trust. The novel's strength is its procedural precision: duplicate-index cards, water-damage mapping, and pressure-transfer reading all serve genuine detection rather than window dressing. Callie's pariaship has evolved into a formal consultant role; Cross's "bounded partnership" expands as the case demands; Eli's slow-burn apprenticeship culminates in the structural breakthrough of the missing duplicate card. The series formula is visible, however, and the suspect pool collapses to Benedict Calder by Chapter 3, which robs the mystery of some of its second-half tension. Still, the character work across four books pays off richly, and the ash-index concept turns archive destruction into detection engine. Four stars; recommended for readers who love procedural detail over surprise.

## One change that would flip my rating
Introduce a second viable suspect — someone with documented 1991 county/sheriff access and present-day leverage over the archive committee — so B.C. doesn't collapse to Benedict Calder alone by Chapter 3. A living relative or colleague of Tavis, or a courthouse figure who could have coerced or collaborated, would restore the second-half suspect tension without disturbing the procedural brilliance or the historical arc. The language-parallel evidence in Chapter 7 would then feel like conviction rather than confirmation.
