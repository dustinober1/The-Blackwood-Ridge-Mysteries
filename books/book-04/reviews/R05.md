**Entry point:** Cold (first in series)  **Format read:** ebook (Kindle Unlimited)

## Gut reaction (3–5 sentences, in Priya's voice)
Slow first page — frost, a bookshop, a kettle, a map repair — and I almost drifted. Then a retired clerk calls to say a shelf "has lied to me twice" and Town Hall starts smoking, and I read the rest in one sitting. This is a mystery built out of pencil marks, water lines, and a missing number in a card sequence, and it never let me get comfortable. The 1991 hit-and-run thread lands harder than any graphic scene would have. I finished it and immediately wanted Book 1.

## What worked (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- Phone-call hook: Ruth's "a shelf in the east municipal bay that has lied to me twice" (Ch 1:113) and the smoke at Ch 1:209–223.
- Procedural fairness: every solution beat was planted — duplicate-card mismatch (Ch 5:2438–2457), blank number eight (Ch 6:3135–3152), brass cat charm under the stair grate (Ch 7:3242–3252), pressure transfer "B. Cal... brought in by father" (Ch 7:3339–3361).
- Character turns: Cross writes "Ruth Mallory death investigation" (Ch 4:1783); Eli's grid and the empty eighth line (Ch 6:3127–3152); Mae's "By Monday everybody knew not to know it in a useful way" (Ch 5:2515).
- The fake-note autopsy: "It explains her away" (Ch 4:1841–1847) — the smartest idea in the book.
- Ending: arrest without a shout (Ch 8:4027), the supplemental entry writing Lila's whole name (Ch 8:4059–4095), glass set beside the ledger, not on it (Ch 8:4197–4201).

## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- The hook is ~1.5 pages late; Ch 1:41–78 is mood without stakes.
- Procedure echoes: photograph/log/label beats repeat across Ch 2 (591, 613, 647, 755, 847) and Ch 3 (1111, 1139, 1555–1561), dragging a binge reader.
- Simon Vale's visit (Ch 3:1257–1351) is a speed bump; "same warning" refrain recurs (Ch 2:607/611, Ch 3:1349).
- Ch 5 middle sags in the contact-list debate (2404–2426).
- Culprit's "proof" is a language-habit match (Ch 7:3584, 3594), not hard authorship evidence; fine, but a skeptic may want one more concrete beat.

## Public review draft (the review Priya would actually post, 80–250 words)
Picked this up cold on KU and finished it in one sitting. The first page is a little slow — frost, a bookshop, a kettle — but by page two a retired clerk calls Callie Thorne to say a shelf "has lied to me twice," and Town Hall is smoking twenty pages later. From there it's procedure, paper, and a dead woman's pencil marks, and I kept turning. The mystery mechanics are the real pleasure: duplicate cards, water lines, a missing number eight in a sequence, a brass cat charm found under a stair grate. The 1991 hit-and-run thread lands hard without a single graphic scene. Some chapters repeat the photograph-log-label ritual more than needed (chapters 2–3), and Simon Vale's visit in chapter 3 is a speed bump. But "It explains her away" and the pressure-transfer scene in chapter 7 are exactly why I binge. Quiet ending, no cheap twist, and the final image — a magnifying glass set beside a damaged ledger, not on it — made me immediately want Book 1. Four stars: fast, fair, respectful of the reader.

## One change that would flip my rating
Cut the repeated photograph/log/pencil ritual beats in Ch 2–3 by half and compress Simon's Ch 3 visit (1257–1351); move the phone call into the first paragraph of Ch 1. That would make it a 5★ for me.
