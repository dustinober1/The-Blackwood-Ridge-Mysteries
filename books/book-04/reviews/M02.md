## Gut reaction (3–5 sentences, in Jules's voice)
The prose is tighter than most cozies dare to be, and Ruth Mallory's duplicate-card system is the kind of nerdy procedural hook I live for. But I had Benedict Calder pegged by Chapter 3 — the moment the water damage centered on the night-desk packet tied to Tavis and Crowe, the suspect pool collapsed to one living name. Ben's "community stability" language is smooth, but it's the same smoothing habit the fake note uses, and the book signposts it so hard it stops being subtext and becomes text. Cross and Callie's partnership earns its stripes, and Mae's quiet grief-work is the emotional anchor. Solid craft, wasted surprise.

## Public review draft (the review Jules would actually post, 80–250 words)
The Archive Fire has the best paperwork in the genre — Ruth Mallory's duplicate-card system turns archive procedure into detection engine, and Callie Thorne reads the water lines like tea leaves that actually mean something. Sheriff Cross's slow pivot from "not a partnership" to "help me reconstruct it" earns every inch. Mae Hartwell brings the grief that isn't performative. Eli Townsend's blank eighth line is the structural clue of the year.

But — and this hurts because the craft deserves better — I had the killer at Chapter 3. The moment the fire damage centered on the old sheriff's night-desk packet (the one tied to Tavis and Crowe), the suspect pool collapsed to one living name: Benedict Calder. B.C. on Ruth's pocket card. The 1991 hit-and-run. "Brought in by father, father present, no report until morning." Everyone else — Simon, Clara, Nell, even Tavis — is a record-keeping failure, not a murderer. The book knows it too; it clears them efficiently by Chapter 6. Ben's "community stability" smoothing is the same habit as the fake stair note, but the parallel is signposted so hard it stops being subtext.

Three stars for prose and procedure. Docked two for a mystery that shows its hand before the halfway mark. I'll read Book 5, but I'm bringing my own suspect list.

## One change that would flip my rating
Give Ben Calder a viable alternative — a second living person with 1991 access and present-day archive control — so the Tavis/Crowe/B.C. path doesn't collapse to a single name by Chapter 3. Or delay the water-damage-centers-on-night-desk revelation until Chapter 5.
