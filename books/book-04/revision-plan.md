# Revision Plan — Book 4: The Archive Fire

## Executive summary

### Overall readiness

Book 4 is structurally strong and ready for a controlled revision pass, but it is not yet ready for prose polish, export, package, or publication. The full draft delivers the intended Book 4 function: the archive is wounded, Ruth Mallory's system survives her, Cross formally makes room for Callie's method, and the Ben Calder solution is assembled through records rather than sudden confession.

The draft is best described as **revision-ready with a sound mystery spine**. Its largest remaining needs are not wholesale rewriting. They are targeted proof-path reinforcement, clue integration, pacing/tightening, and repetition control.

### Biggest strengths

- **Ruth Mallory is present through systems.** Her narrow upright hand, complete dates, duplicate cards, pocket card, working cards, and recovery-path ethic make her feel alive without relying on sentimental flashback.
- **The Callie/Cross partnership lands.** Cross's log, Bell's photographs, and his insistence on boundaries make Callie's reading portable without turning the book into a police procedural.
- **The old Lila Crowe thread is distinct from Book 2.** Lila is a public death with a smoothed hit-and-run record, not a disappearance. The draft repeatedly protects that distinction.
- **Ben Calder's villainy fits the series.** He is not melodramatic. His weapon is public language, smoothing, access, and the civic habit of making a clean story more valuable than a damaged true one.
- **The emotional close is restrained and on-brand.** Cross files a supplemental record instead of speechifying; Mae and Callie thaw through labels and work; Eli remains useful but bounded; Callie places Eleanor's glass beside damaged paper as a tool, not a relic.

### Biggest revision risks

1. **Missing key-charm / access-path clue.** The outline and Book 4 story memory include Ruth's brass cat key charm / key-ring clue, but the drafted manuscript does not appear to set up, recover, or pay it off. This weakens the present-day access/handling path against Ben.
2. **Ben's present-day proof path needs one more concrete physical/access thread.** The current proof path is strong in language, motive, early knowledge, and reconstructed old-record pressure, but the arrest would be firmer if the missing key/charm/access clue is integrated cleanly.
3. **Draft length is far above target.** The progress target remains 25,000 words, while the drafted chapters total approximately 36,026 words. If the novella target remains important, the later style pass must tighten significantly.
4. **Several analytical beats repeat.** The fake Ruth note, smoothing language, procedure-as-protection, and context-not-proof distinctions are all strong, but they recur often enough that a style pass should consolidate them before export.
5. **Chapter 6 carries a heavy load.** Simon, Clara, Nell, Tavis, and missing-eight all resolve or advance in one chapter. It works, but it is the likeliest place to feel crowded.

## Revision priorities

### Critical fixes

1. **Restore or deliberately remove the key-charm clue.**
   - Current planning expects Ruth's archive key ring / brass cat charm to help prove someone handled Ruth's keys after she could not.
   - Manuscript search showed the key-charm thread appears in planning/bible material but not in the Book 4 draft itself.
   - Best fix: integrate it lightly as a present-day access/handling clue.
   - Minimum path:
     - Chapter 1 or 2: establish Ruth's keys or brass cat charm as part of her known key ring / personal system.
     - Chapter 3 or 6: distinguish Clara's K-2 key-log lie from Ruth's own key handling.
     - Chapter 7: recover the charm or a record of its displacement near the rear stair / transfer route / logged effects.
     - Chapter 8: include it as one strand in Cross's present-day evidence assembly.
   - Avoid detailed fire-setting mechanics.

2. **Sharpen Ben's current-crime access path.**
   - Ben has committee access, a sign-in entry, redevelopment authority, early knowledge, public language, and old motive.
   - The draft needs a slightly clearer bridge from those pieces to Ruth's present-day death and the staged note.
   - Use the key-charm/access clue to pin handling, not to create an action-thriller reconstruction.
   - Cross's arrest should read as grounded in accumulated present-day evidence, not only old Lila Crowe guilt.

3. **Clarify final arrest basis in Chapter 8.**
   - Current Chapter 8 says Ben is arrested on suspicion of Ruth Mallory's murder and related destruction/concealment. That is correct in tone.
   - Add one or two controlled lines in Cross's evidentiary walk that explicitly separate:
     - old motive: Lila Crowe / Calder history;
     - present opportunity/access: redevelopment committee / key path;
     - present guilty knowledge: Crowe packet status before disclosure;
     - staged narrative: fake Ruth note and smoothing language;
     - survivor system: Ruth's duplicate path defeating erasure.
   - Do not turn Ben into a confessor. Keep the arrest procedural and restrained.

4. **Protect the Lila/Tavis distinction through the revision.**
   - Any new key/access edits must not blur Tavis into the present killer.
   - Tavis remains morally responsible for the 1991 failure, not Ruth's murderer.
   - Lila remains a public hit-and-run death with a smoothed record, not a Lauren Coalter-style disappearance.

### Recommended fixes

1. **Reduce repeated fake-note analysis after the proof path is secure.**
   - Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 all return to the fake note's lack of date/shelf/range/recovery path and its smoothing/reassurance function.
   - Keep the escalation, but make each repetition do new work:
     - Chapter 3: Ruth-system mismatch.
     - Chapter 5: worldview mismatch.
     - Chapter 7: comparative language with Ben.
     - Chapter 8: evidentiary use in the confrontation.
   - Trim or vary the rest during style tightening.

2. **Tighten Chapter 6 pacing.**
   - Simon, Clara, Nell, Tavis, and missing eight all appear in a tight sequence.
   - The chapter works, but the red-herring resolutions may be tightened so Tavis and missing-eight have more oxygen.
   - Keep the false suspects human, not cleanly innocent; their record failures reinforce the theme.

3. **Balance Ben's visibility.**
   - Ben's civic language is excellent and fair-play.
   - Because it becomes conspicuous by Chapter 5 and especially Chapter 7, consider giving Nell a slightly stronger public-language echo so Ben remains fair rather than over-obvious.
   - Do not make Ben cartoonish or more villainous.

4. **Decide whether 36,000 words is acceptable.**
   - If Book 4 can be a longer novella, update target metadata later.
   - If the 25,000-word target remains firm, plan an aggressive tightening pass after critical proof edits.
   - Do not cut the Ruth system or Cross log architecture; those are the book's engine.

5. **Mark outline metadata later if desired.**
   - `books/book-04/outline.md` still carries `draft_state: pending` in its front matter, while progress.yaml correctly marks draft complete.
   - This is not urgent for Batch 1 unless metadata cleanup is included.

### Optional polish opportunities

- Vary repeated sentence shapes such as "Not X. Not Y.", "The room held/changed/altered," and "context — not proof."
- Trim procedural repetition once Bell/Cross logging is established.
- Preserve the archive sensuality while reducing duplicate descriptions of smoke, damp paper, wool, and gray light where they repeat without adding new pressure.
- Keep Mae's sharp warmth, but watch for too many perfect aphoristic lines in close succession.
- Ensure every chapter closes on forward motion, not only thematic resonance.

### Ben proof path

The draft exposes Ben through accumulation, not confession. The path currently includes:

- **Motive:** the old Lila Crowe record threatens his clean civic identity and family history.
- **Access:** archive-redevelopment chair / committee access / Town Hall sign-in.
- **Old-record pressure:** `B. Cal...`, father present, no report until morning.
- **Guilty knowledge:** Ben refers to the Crowe packet as gone or unrecoverable before official disclosure.
- **Language habit:** public drafts and memos use passive/smoothing constructions that discourage reconstruction.
- **Fake note mismatch:** the stair note imitates Ruth's edges but not her system.
- **Ruth's duplicate system:** missing eight, freezer-box mislabel, traveling-card fragment, separator-board transfer.
- **Cross's procedure:** the case is assembled through log, photographs, comparative language, and recorded limits.

**Main weakness:** The access/handling path is currently more implied than pinned. Restoring the key charm clue would make the current-crime case firmer without requiring melodrama.

### Fake Ruth note path

The fake note works well because the problem is not simply handwriting. It fails as Ruth because it lacks:

- complete date;
- exact shelf/range reference;
- equipment or packet description;
- duplicate notation;
- recovery path;
- Ruth's belief that writing makes the next person responsible.

The note's wording is strongest when treated as a staged explanation, not as a handwriting puzzle. Preserve that.

### Lila/Tavis path

This thread is coherent and emotionally effective.

- Lila Crowe is a public death with a funeral, not a disappearance.
- Tavis is morally responsible for old procedural burial, not the present killer.
- "I kept the town whole" is clearly named and rejected.
- Cross's supplemental record corrects the path without pretending to restore what was lost.
- Lila feels human through ordinary details: library cart, green scarf, careful signature, whole name.

### False suspect resolution

- **Simon Vale:** cleared by timing; guilty of sloppy documentation and pride.
- **Clara Hensley:** key-log alteration tied to unauthorized postcard box, not night-desk packet or stair note.
- **Nell Pritchard:** donor-card smoothing / public optics, not murder.
- **Roland Tavis:** old cover-up, no present access/timing; morally central but not present culprit.

False suspects work best when they remain record failures rather than distractions. Keep that.
