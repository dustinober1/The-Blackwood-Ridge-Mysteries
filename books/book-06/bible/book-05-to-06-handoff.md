# Book 5-to-Book 6 Canon Handoff

## Narrative endpoint

Book 5 closes in early February 2027 after Nora Mercer Shaw is arrested for Alton Mercer's murder. Callie's public reputation is at its highest point: Blackwood Ridge treats her as an indispensable reader of records, although usefulness brings entitlement and pressure. Her case-specific records-consultant arrangement with Sheriff Dalton Cross has worked in practice. Cross retains warrants, interviews, evidence custody, legal conclusions, and arrest authority. Mae and Callie remain in a workable new normal expressed through permission, labor, food, labels, and presence rather than full reconciliation. Eli remains a familiar, careful, increasingly skilled apprentice.

The exact Book 5 closing line remains: **“She closed the file.”**

## Case closure

- Nora Mercer Shaw is the proven killer.
- Alton Mercer's death is fully solved.
- The $286,400 split-invoice misuse and the approximately $2.4 million residual inheritance/control motive are resolved.
- The trust and foundation move under independent supervision.
- Nora's guilt does not depend on Eli's hidden activity or a confession.
- No Book 6 revelation may weaken the medical, financial, access, documentary, or warrant evidence supporting Nora's arrest.

## Knowledge boundaries at entry

### Callie knows

The repair ticket is genuine, recent, useful as a current-hand comparison, and reached her through a route nobody can fully reconstruct. She understands that provenance includes route, but she does not know the route was intentionally shaped.

### Cross knows

The ticket was independently authenticated and non-dispositive. Its custody before the returned Mercer volume is incomplete. He treats that as a procedural gap, not proof of a hidden actor.

### Mae knows

She found the ticket inside the returned Mercer volume and gave it to Callie without interpreting it. She does not know who placed or redirected it.

### Eli knows

Internal series truth: Eli redirected a genuine recent repair ticket into Callie's lawful comparison path. He did not forge it, create Nora's guilt, alter police evidence, or make it decisive.

### Reader may suspect

The ticket did not reach Callie neutrally. The reader may suspect deliberate placement but may not receive confirmation of the curator's identity.

## Unresolved series material

- Genuine evidence can be curated without being falsified.
- Discovery paths may be shaped through timing, routing, shelving, return boxes, catalog slips, and ordinary access.
- Manipulating access is distinct from manipulating evidence.
- Steering may affect attention and timing without changing who committed a murder.

## Hidden-series controls

- Eli's active placement/redirection begins in Book 5.
- Book 6 makes a repeatable pattern visible.
- Book 6 does not conclusively expose Eli.
- Eli did not manufacture prior murders, forge decisive evidence, frame innocent people, or become the immediate killer.
- His hidden role is limited to curation, routing, timing, and controlled access to genuine material.
- Criminal and ethical conclusions remain evidence-limited until later books.

## Prohibited retcons

Book 6 must not make Nora innocent; suggest Alton's murder was staged by Eli; make Eli the architect of earlier murders; change Book 5's motive, forged-note method, or arrest basis; convert Cross into an unrestricted partner; instantly repair Mae and Callie's relationship; give Callie unearned knowledge; or reveal Eli before Book 7.
