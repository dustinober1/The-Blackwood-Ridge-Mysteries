# Character Arcs — The Pattern

## Callie Thorne

Callie enters professionally confident and publicly trusted. The murder confirms her skill, but the annotation route destabilizes her confidence in neutral access. Her movement is from “I can read what is present” to “I must also ask why this reached me.” She does not reject documents, invalidate prior cases, or conclude Eli is responsible.

## Sheriff Dalton Cross

Cross preserves the case-specific written consultant arrangement. He authorizes documentary observation, protects custody, conducts interviews, obtains warrants, controls legal conclusions, and makes the arrest. He accepts that the route pattern is a procedural problem while refusing to name a curator without evidence.

## Mae Hartwell

Mae remains in the workable new normal with Callie. She contributes Bellweather civic memory, notices the green volunteer coats and brass-cleaning routine, manages practical interruptions, and recognizes Callie's unease without demanding disclosure. She does not solve the case or reopen the old reconciliation argument.

## Eli Townsend

Eli is visibly useful, familiar, careful, and increasingly sophisticated. He catalogs the returned road pamphlet, notes custody gaps, and helps build a route chronology without overclaiming. Internal truth: he created and routed the curation marks. Book 6 contains no Eli POV, confession, villain behavior, private confirmation, or evidence sufficient for Callie or Cross to identify him.

## Recurring-cast limit

Recurring characters appear only where they provide continuity, town atmosphere, procedure, practical access, or emotional movement. No cameo may displace the case or create false continuity.
