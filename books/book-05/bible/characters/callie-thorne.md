# Callie Thorne — Book 5

## State entering

Callie is a county-approved records consultant on a case-by-case basis. Blackwood Ridge increasingly seeks her help. She remains useful rather than universally welcomed, emotionally restrained, and protective of evidence and private space.

## External desire

Determine whether Alton Mercer's apparent suicide note is authentic and, once homicide is established, help Cross make the document evidence portable enough to support the official case.

## Internal need

Distinguish being needed from being known. Callie must share interpretation without surrendering rigor and must recognize that evidence has a route, not only a meaning.

## Book 5 tests

- Defend a handwriting conclusion through observable form, rhythm, pressure, language, and verified samples.
- Accept Cross's written boundaries without treating them as distrust.
- Let Mae find a crucial sample and Eli organize comparisons.
- Resist converting public praise into endless obligation.
- Stop short when financial motive or vocabulary feels convincing but is not yet authorship proof.

## Arc

Callie begins with requests stacked at her door and a private satisfaction she refuses to name. She converts attention into work because usefulness feels safer than belonging. The Mercer note gives her a public test: a conclusion she can demonstrate but cannot control once it enters police procedure. She succeeds by allowing Bell, Cross, the examiner, Mae, and Eli to hold separate parts of the proof. She ends respected, overneeded, and quietly disturbed by the unresolved route of a genuine page.

## Relationship movement

- **Cross:** Functional bounded trust. He asks for her expertise, challenges overreach, and owns official action.
- **Mae:** New normal through practical limits. Mae's `NOT TODAY` label protects Callie without asking for emotional disclosure.
- **Eli:** De facto apprentice. Callie gives him more complex comparison work and assumes his provenance discipline is naturally transparent.
- **Town:** High point of public dependence; gratitude and entitlement arrive together.

## Method notes

Callie never says merely that the hand “feels wrong.” She names repeated constructions, pen lifts, pressure patterns, variation, baseline guides, graphite, sample currentness, and language purpose.
