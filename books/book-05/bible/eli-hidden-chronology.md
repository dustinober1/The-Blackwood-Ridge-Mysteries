# INTERNAL ONLY — Eli Hidden Chronology, Book 5

> Do not quote, summarize, or expose this file in reader-facing metadata, chapter prose, retailer copy, public production notes, or ordinary Book 5 drafting prompts. Use only to preserve fair long-arc continuity through Books 5–8.

## Guardrails

Eli may curate access to genuine truth. He does not:

- forge Alton Mercer's note;
- create or alter Nora's evidence;
- manufacture guilt;
- frame an innocent suspect;
- remove decisive evidence from police custody;
- contaminate the official chain of custody;
- know the complete murder solution before Callie and Cross establish it;
- become legally responsible for Alton's death.

## Hidden objective

Eli sees that Callie is being asked to compare a suspicious note mainly against Alton's older formal hand. He remembers a recent natural sample in The Foxed Page's repair paperwork. He believes Callie needs the page in context but fears that presenting it as his interpretation will cause Cross to exclude him or Callie to dismiss the observation as eagerness. He chooses to make the genuine page available without making himself the reason it matters.

## Detailed chronology

### Monday, January 25 — Before the murder

- Eli accompanies Callie to Mercer House as shop assistant.
- He sees Alton complete a repair ticket for a copy of *A Digest of Virginia Chancery Practice* and later sees the ticket placed with general shop-return paperwork rather than inside the volume.
- He notices Nora identify the bench notebooks containing Alton's “best hand.” This reads as ordinary collection competence.
- He does not suspect murder.

### Tuesday, January 26 — Apparent suicide

- Eli remains at The Foxed Page while Callie works under Cross's new case authorization.
- Callie tells him only that comparison samples may be needed and that he is not to search Mercer materials independently.
- Eli obeys the official boundary. He does not enter the scene, handle the note, or contact a suspect.

### Wednesday morning, January 27 — Recognition

- During legitimate shop work, Eli checks the general repair drawer for the Mercer invoice copy.
- He finds the genuine repair ticket, dated January 25 and written entirely by Alton in a quick natural hand.
- He recognizes that its line pressure and joins differ from the older formal samples Callie has described.
- He does not know that Nora wrote the note. He only believes the ticket may prevent a false conclusion.

### Wednesday late morning — The relocation

- A returned Mercer chancery volume is already scheduled for an authorized comparison/provenance sort at The Foxed Page.
- Eli places the genuine repair ticket flat inside the volume, centered between the front endpaper and title page.
- He adds no writing to the ticket, does not alter its date, and does not mark the book.
- He writes an ordinary pencil routing slip for the return box: `MERCER / CHANCERY / repair papers`.
- His innocent-cover logic: loose repair paperwork belongs with the returned book; putting it back is competent shop practice.
- His concealed purpose: he expects Callie or Mae to encounter the ticket while the Mercer comparison set is open.

### Wednesday afternoon — Discovery

- Mae opens the chancery volume during the authorized sort and finds the ticket.
- Mae is the person who tells Callie and physically hands the closed volume to her.
- Callie alerts Cross before using the page.
- Cross logs the ticket as a voluntarily produced Foxed Page business record, verifies the date against the shop invoice, and obtains Alton-authenticated comparison support from Pruitt and foundation records.
- The ticket never enters police custody before being disclosed and logged.

### Eli's omission

- When Callie asks how the ticket was with the volume, Eli says: “It belonged with the Mercer return.”
- The sentence is true as catalog judgment and false by omission about prior location.
- He allows Callie to assume the page remained with the book or was naturally re-paired during ordinary sorting.
- He does not claim Mae or Callie found it first.

### Chapters 4–7 — After disclosure

- Eli helps build the comparison-source grid but does not direct Callie's findings.
- He marks the ticket's source as `Foxed Page repair record / found in returned volume / prior loose-paper location unverified` once Cross requires exact custody wording.
- He experiences genuine anxiety that the route gap will cause the page to be excluded.
- Its independent authentication and non-dispositive role allow it to remain a comparison sample.
- Eli interprets this outcome as proof that careful placement can help truth survive without falsifying it.

### Chapter 8 — Quiet residue

- Cross's provenance form records that Mae found the ticket in the returned volume and that the prior loose-paper location is not established.
- Callie dislikes the gap but accepts it because the forgery and arrest stand without the ticket.
- Eli is not present when Cross and Callie discuss the gap.
- No character accuses him or treats the route as suspicious.

## Retrospective tells reserved for Books 6–7

1. The ticket lies squarely centered in the volume rather than loosely caught where active repair paperwork normally rides.
2. The pencil routing slip is in Eli's hand and uses the category `repair papers`, though the shop invoice lists the ticket separately.
3. Eli says “It belonged with the Mercer return,” not “It was in the Mercer return.”
4. The provenance form identifies Mae as finder but cannot identify the first person who relocated the page.
5. Eli is unusually precise about marking the custody gap after the page is logged, suggesting he has anticipated the question.

## Psychological continuity

- Eli is frightened of being excluded, grateful to Callie, and increasingly convinced that belonging depends on usefulness.
- Book 4 taught him that a missing place can become an index and that not every useful thing is his to say immediately.
- He misapplies those lessons: he curates the route, tells himself he has only restored provenance, and believes the result justifies the omission.
- His action should be morally troubling in retrospect because he manipulates Callie's attention and the apparent naturalness of discovery, not because he falsifies the murder case.

## Series protection

- **Book 6:** A second discovery route may echo “found where it belonged” and make Callie notice a pattern without identifying Eli.
- **Book 7:** The Mercer ticket's provenance form, routing slip, and wording become fair retrospective evidence of Eli's method.
- **Book 8:** The distinction between curating access to truth and controlling another person's choices remains central to the finale.
