# Carry-Forward into Book 5

> The final Book 4 manuscript controls this handoff. Older planning shorthand describing an unrestricted partnership or complete emotional healing does not control.

## Timeline position

Book 5 opens in late January 2027, approximately eleven weeks after the early-November 2026 events of *The Archive Fire*. The county has approved a written, case-by-case records-consultant arrangement between Sheriff Dalton Cross and Callie Thorne. The Town Archives remain in phased recovery. Ruth Mallory's murder, Benedict Calder's arrest, and the supplemental Lila Mae Crowe record remain recent communal wounds.

## Callie Thorne

- Callie is no longer merely an unofficial nuisance. She is a **records consultant, case by case, with written boundaries**.
- She is not a deputy, police employee, unrestricted partner, co-sheriff, interviewer, evidence custodian, or warrant officer.
- Her work remains observational, documentary, comparative, historical, and interpretive.
- Cross controls scenes, searches, interviews, evidence handling, warrants, legal conclusions, and arrest decisions.
- Book 4 taught Callie that preservation and interpretation can be shared work. Bell's photographs, Cross's log, Mae's labels, Eli's grid, and Ruth's duplicate path all held the table with her.
- Eleanor's brass magnifying glass is now a chosen working tool. At Book 4's end it rests beside damaged paper, within reach of whoever needs to read next.
- The floorboard creak is movement through grief, not erasure. Callie deliberately steps on the board and lets it sound; no one turns the moment into a speech.
- Callie remains emotionally restrained. She does not become socially relaxed, praise-seeking, or easily comforted.
- New Book 5 pressure: the town seeks her out. She must distinguish public trust from dependence and usefulness from identity.

## Sheriff Dalton Cross

- Cross trusts Callie's documentary analysis enough to formalize the narrow role his office actually needs.
- The arrangement is procedural rather than sentimental: consultant copy, working file, written scope, case-by-case authorization.
- Cross remains responsible for official movement. He sets access boundaries and challenges Callie when interpretation outruns what the paper gives.
- Their warmth is controlled and earned. Do not convert it into romance, banter-heavy partnership, or informal co-detective behavior.
- His tells remain: neck rub when out of depth; jaw set when duty conflicts with loyalty or institutional comfort; hat behavior as a respect signal; shorter sentences under pressure.
- Book 5 must show him translating Callie's handwriting observations into comparison requests, medical questions, warrants, financial records, and an independent arrest basis.

## Mae Hartwell

- Mae and Callie have reached a workable new relationship, not a restored old one.
- Mae still asks permission where she once entered automatically.
- Their thaw is expressed through labor, labels, food placed without coercion, practical memory, and presence that does not demand explanation.
- Mae has not delivered a complete apology and Callie has not erased the harm.
- Book 5 should show the new normal rather than reopen the same confrontation.
- Mae's Book 5 role is to provide Mercer family and civic context, notice the cost of Callie's new public usefulness, and intervene practically rather than morally grandstand.

## Eli Townsend

- Eli works part time at The Foxed Page and functions as Callie's de facto apprentice.
- His established skills include provenance noticing, duplicate paths, indexing, water-damaged record handling, carbon/pressure transfer recognition, comparison grids, evidence-boundary discipline, and the restraint to leave a blank unfilled.
- Cross has affirmed that narrow work matters because it stays narrow.
- Eli's competence must make his later manipulation plausible without making him implausibly omniscient.
- In Book 5 he learns more about Callie's comparison habits: what she treats as a verified sample, how she separates class characteristics from individual habits, and how she builds a portable explanation.
- He must appear helpful, loyal, careful, and understandable.
- Do not reveal him as the long-arc manipulator. The reader may later recognize that a genuine current-hand sample did not reach Callie naturally.
- Eli does not forge the suicide note, fabricate murder evidence, frame anyone, contaminate decisive police evidence, or become legally equivalent to Nora.

## Blackwood Ridge

- The town now consults Callie because it needs her.
- She is increasingly indispensable but not universally welcomed.
- The Archive Fire and Ruth Mallory case remain recent; public records and civic reputation still feel unstable.
- People bring Callie documents, questions, family boxes, and expectations. Some are grateful. Some resent the fact that she is now an unofficial gate through which truth seems to pass.
- Do not reset the town to Book 1 hostility or Book 2 pariah conditions.

## Prior cases — do not retcon

- **Book 1:** Wendell Pike's murder and the Ralston/Hartwell/Wren founding scandal are resolved and public.
- **Book 2:** Lauren Coalter's death is resolved; Wells Ambler confessed; Marguerite Dabney's botanical cipher was vindicated.
- **Book 3:** Vivian Larter's case is resolved; Harlan Beech was arrested; Larter's method stands; Callie learned her reading could be incomplete without being false.
- **Book 4:** Ruth Mallory's murder and the archive fire are resolved; Benedict Calder was arrested; Cross filed the Lila Mae Crowe supplement; the consultant arrangement is pending only routine county administration at the final scene and is active by Book 5.

## Book 5 continuity constraints

- Single third-person-limited POV through Callie.
- Eight chapters, approximately 25,000 words.
- No Chapter 1 prose exists at initialization.
- Violence restrained and primarily off-page.
- The case resolves in Book 5 without exposing Eli.
- The forged handwriting conclusion must be demonstrated through observable features and verified samples.
- Cross independently establishes present-day arrest grounds.
- The final provenance gap around the current-hand sample remains quiet and non-dispositive.
