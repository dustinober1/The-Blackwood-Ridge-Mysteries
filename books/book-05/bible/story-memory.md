# Book Story Memory — The Planted Page

## Book 5 obligations

- **Handwriting-as-identity peak:** The central note looks like Alton Mercer because someone has studied his hand as an image. Callie proves that identity is carried in movement, pressure, rhythm, variation, language, and purpose—not merely letter shape.
- **Consultant arrangement in practice:** Cross gives Callie a written scope and enforces it. She does not independently search, interview, seize, or arrest.
- **Public reputation high point:** People now bring records to Callie. The attention should feel useful, invasive, flattering, and dangerous at once.
- **Case closure:** Nora Mercer Shaw's murder of Alton resolves completely with a legally plausible arrest and no forced confession.
- **Planted-page seam:** A genuine current-hand repair ticket reaches Callie through an apparently ordinary shop path. The case remains solvable without learning who redirected it.

## Motifs and callbacks

- **Brass magnifying glass:** A working instrument, not a shrine. Callie uses it to make construction visible and allows Cross/Bell to photograph what she sees.
- **Hands as identity:** Alton's current fatigued hand, the killer's simulated hand, Eli's steady labeling hand, Cross's logging hand, Mae's practical labels, and Callie's own ink-stained fingers.
- **Floorboard:** The board may creak without a ceremonial pause. Its sound is part of the shop's present life now.
- **Bergamot:** A compass that can be interrupted by callers and work. Callie repeatedly lets tea cool because being needed is becoming another form of avoidance.
- **The Foxed Page back room:** No longer entirely private. It can hold comparison work, but official evidence stays under Cross's control.
- **Town as chorus:** Envelopes at the door, requests on the counter, gratitude phrased as entitlement, and resentment at Callie's influence.
- **Labels:** Mae's `NOT TODAY` slip becomes a practical defense against Callie's growing queue.
- **Hearing / examination:** Alton's judicial language is imitated in the note. The book asks who is allowed to examine whom.

## Book-specific document motifs

- **Current hand versus formal hand:** Older public handwriting remains recognizable but does not represent the body's present movement.
- **The comparison grid:** Verified source, date, custody, writing purpose, line quality, joins, pressure, rhythm, language habit.
- **The repeated `g`:** Same retraced descender in two words; a copied solution repeated where living writing would vary.
- **Graphite under ink:** A path drawn before the sentence pretends to be spontaneous.
- **The red portfolio:** The absent trust restatement leaves a pressure image and an appointment trail.
- **The repair ticket:** Genuine, recent, authenticated, and quietly relocated.

## Character memory additions

### Alton Pierce Mercer

- Writes his current catalog cards in blue-black ink with pressure that fades along a line.
- His lowercase `g` varies with speed; his `th`, `nd`, and `ri` joins are continuous.
- Uses exact account names, dates, and amounts in financial writing.
- Corrects people as a form of authority.
- Public generosity is real and conditional.
- Last verified living communication: 8:47 p.m. voicemail confirming the trust-restatement appointment.

### Nora Mercer Shaw

- Raised partly in Mercer House after losing her parents.
- Learned to equate usefulness with belonging.
- Runs the foundation capably and has saved real projects.
- Split-invoice misuse totals $286,400.
- Current trust residue is approximately $2.4 million.
- Murder logic is preservation weaponized: protect the institution, herself, and the story of service.

### Claire Mercer Duvall

- Alton's estranged daughter and an architectural historian.
- Removes her mother's sealed letters without authorization because she fears the foundation will claim them.
- Her theft creates a paper-path lie but does not connect to the murder.

### Gideon Shaw

- Restoration contractor and Nora's husband.
- His company received split invoices and concealed overruns.
- He is financially compromised and not present for the killing.
- He suspects Nora has protected him more than she admits but does not know the murder plan.

### Tess Rooke

- Longtime house manager with lifetime gate-cottage occupancy under the 2021 trust.
- Maintains medication-return and house-access ledgers.
- Hid Alton's benign memory lapses out of loyalty and fear of being displaced.
- Finds the body.

### Caleb Voss

- Antiquarian dealer and Mercer collection consultant.
- Has tracing materials and extensive handwriting access because of legitimate catalog work.
- Conceals a provenance substitution and removes a disputed folio, creating the strongest false authorship path.

## Callie arc memory

- Begins with public requests waiting at her door and feels safest converting attention into work.
- Confidently identifies the first repeated form but submits each observation to comparison rather than declaring fraud.
- Learns to let Cross, Bell, the document examiner, Mae, and Eli each hold a different part of proof.

- Ends knowing that evidence has a route as well as a content, but does not yet understand that someone she trusts is curating routes.

## Cross arc memory

- Opens a written consultant authorization with exact limits.
- Challenges Callie when she treats language habit as authorship before physical indicators are established.
- Converts her observations into sample warrants/consent requests, examiner review, financial subpoenas, scene re-review, and search-warrant affidavits.
- Arrests Nora on cumulative present-day evidence; does not use Callie's intuition as a substitute for probable cause.
