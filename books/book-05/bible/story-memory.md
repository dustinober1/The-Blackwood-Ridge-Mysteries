# Book Story Memory — The Planted Page

## Book 5 obligations

- **Handwriting-as-identity peak:** The central note looks like Alton Mercer because someone has studied his hand as an image. Callie proves that identity is carried in movement, pressure, rhythm, variation, language, and purpose—not merely letter shape.
- **Consultant arrangement in practice:** Cross gives Callie a written scope and enforces it. She does not independently search, interview, seize, or arrest.
- **Public reputation high point:** People now bring records to Callie. The attention should feel useful, invasive, flattering, and dangerous at once.
- **Case closure:** Nora Mercer Shaw's murder of Alton resolves completely with a legally plausible arrest and no forced confession.
- **Planted-page seam:** A genuine current-hand repair ticket reaches Callie through an apparently ordinary shop path. The case remains solvable without learning who redirected it.

## Motifs and callbacks

- **Brass magnifying glass:** A working instrument, not a shrine. Callie uses it to make construction visible and allows Cross/Bell to photograph what she sees.
- **Hands as identity:** Alton's current fatigued hand, the killer's simulated hand, Eli's steady labeling hand, Cross's logging hand, Mae's practical labels, and Callie's own ink-stained fingers.
- **Floorboard:** The board may creak without a ceremonial pause. Its sound is part of the shop's present life now.
- **Bergamot:** A compass that can be interrupted by callers and work. Callie repeatedly lets tea cool because being needed is becoming another form of avoidance.
- **The Foxed Page back room:** No longer entirely private. It can hold comparison work, but official evidence stays under Cross's control.
- **Town as chorus:** Envelopes at the door, requests on the counter, gratitude phrased as entitlement, and resentment at Callie's influence.
- **Labels:** Mae's `NOT TODAY` slip becomes a practical defense against Callie's growing queue.
- **Hearing / examination:** Alton's judicial language is imitated in the note. The book asks who is allowed to examine whom.

## Book-specific document motifs

- **Current hand versus formal hand:** Older public handwriting remains recognizable but does not represent the body's present movement.
- **The comparison grid:** Verified source, date, custody, writing purpose, line quality, joins, pressure, rhythm, language habit.
- **The repeated `g`:** Same retraced descender in two words; a copied solution repeated where living writing would vary.
- **Graphite under ink:** A path drawn before the sentence pretends to be spontaneous.
- **The red portfolio:** The absent trust restatement leaves a pressure image and an appointment trail.
- **The repair ticket:** Genuine, recent, authenticated, and quietly relocated.

- His lowercase `g` varies with speed; his `th`, `nd`, and `ri` joins are continuous.
- Uses exact account names, dates, and amounts in financial writing.
- Corrects people as a form of authority.

## Completed Chapter 1 memory — The Hand at the Door

- **Date and opening:** On Monday, January 25, 2027, Callie finds three document envelopes and a photocopied church-register page against The Foxed Page door in hard winter light. None has a proper intake form or established authorization. The requests combine gratitude, urgency, entitlement, and assumption.
- **Shop morning:** Callie moves the requests inside without opening them. Eli creates an intake/provenance grid with source, finder, claimed question, authorization, privacy limits, ownership, and disposition fields, leaving unknowns blank. Mae brings egg biscuits, asks permission to put them in the front room, and notices Callie has treated each request as an obligation. The floorboard creaks during ordinary movement; Callie does not pause over it.
- **Consultant arrangement:** Cross delivers the signed closeout and consultant copy for a minor courthouse road-ledger recovery. The form records Callie's narrow documentary role and the sheriff's office's custody and disposition authority. Cross reiterates that the arrangement is case specific and private document disputes are not automatically sheriff's-office matters.
- **Mercer summons:** Tess Rooke delivers Alton's signed same-day request for Callie to assess legal manuscripts before foundation transfer. The authorization names Eli as Callie's assistant and includes handling limits. Callie accepts with professional satisfaction and discomfort at the already-selected hour.
- **Mercer House access:** Tess admits Callie and Eli through the side library entrance and requires the ordinary visitor/handling ledger. Eli records only established information and leaves the material field open until work identifies what they handle.
- **Alton's current hand:** Alton writes catalog cards and a repair ticket for *A Digest of Virginia Chancery Practice* in blue-black fountain-pen ink. Callie observes pressure fading across longer lines, continuous `th`, `nd`, and `ri` joins, and naturally variable lowercase `g` descenders. Pain and fatigue slow the hand without making it inexact. His repair instruction names the volume fully, dates the request, and protects prior repair traces.
- **Older formal hand:** Nora identifies Alton's 1999–2007 bench notebooks as his “best hand”; Alton corrects the description to his most public or best-known hand. Nora's knowledge reads as competent collection management.
- **Suspect field through work:** Nora manages the foundation schedule and transfer requirements; Claire disputes institutional control of her mother's correspondence; Gideon brings restoration estimates under pressure over project categories and overruns; Tess manages access, tea, paperwork, and Alton's daily needs; Caleb photographs and traces catalog features while contesting the status of a disputed folio. None is treated as a murder suspect because no case yet exists.
- **Locked objects and pressure:** The chancery volume is authorized for limited shop work and placed in Tess's outgoing crate. The loose repair ticket remains with general outgoing paperwork and is later filed with the shop repair records. Alton guards a red portfolio and confirms a private 9:00 a.m. appointment with Pruitt through the side entrance. Family and foundation financial strain surface without revealing the appointment's terms.
- **Exact visible Eli work:** Eli builds the shop intake grid; follows Mercer House handling rules; leaves the chancery edition blank rather than risk the guarded hinge or guess; records the full title and separate spine wording; pads and logs the volume; completes the access ledger only after handling is known; and files the returned volume and general shop paperwork through ordinary repair intake. He neither reads unauthorized material nor interprets the family dispute.
- **Mae and Callie movement:** Mae provides concise civic context: Alton funded genuine preservation but made recipients feel examined. She offers food, labor, and observation without using care as leverage or demanding an emotional response.
- **Emotional endpoint:** Callie begins with difficult satisfaction that the town seeks her expertise and ends the day uneasy at how quickly requests become presumed obligations. She retains the ordinary visual memory of Alton writing alive that afternoon, without attaching forensic meaning to it.
- **Final beat:** Before dawn on Tuesday, Tess calls The Foxed Page and reports that Alton is dead and that there is a note in his hand. Callie does not see the body or note in Chapter 1.
