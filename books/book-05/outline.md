# Book 5 Outline — The Planted Page

## Book identity

- **Series:** The Blackwood Ridge Mysteries
- **Book:** 5
- **Author:** Vesper Blythe
- **Genre:** Atmospheric cozy mystery / amateur sleuth
- **POV:** Callie Thorne, third-person limited only
- **Target:** 25,000 words
- **Structure:** Eight chapters
- **Season:** January 25–February 1, 2027
- **Case:** Alton Mercer murder staged as suicide through a simulated handwritten note
- **Solution:** Nora Mercer Shaw
- **Protected-continuity constraint:** Internal later-series material remains confined to its dedicated control file. The outline records only reader-visible actions and the non-dispositive provenance limit.

## Structural promises

- The book opens with Callie being sought out before a death is known.
- The apparent suicide receives genuine initial credibility and procedural respect.
- The first handwriting anomaly appears in Chapter 2, but murder is not declared from handwriting alone.
- The forgery becomes demonstrable at the midpoint through current samples, magnification, and portable comparison.
- Medical evidence independently establishes homicide.
- The trust and financial mechanism become precise in Chapter 5.
- False suspects are meaningfully reframed rather than discarded.
- Cross develops and executes the legal case.
- Nora does not confess.
- The individual case closes; the page-route question remains quiet.

## Chapter 1 — The Hand at the Door

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Monday, January 25, 2027
- **Dramatic purpose:** Establish Callie's changed public status, the consultant arrangement's aftermath, Alton alive, his current handwriting, the Mercer family field, and the trust-pressure atmosphere before the case exists.
- **Opening image:** Before Callie unlocks The Foxed Page, three document envelopes and a church-register photocopy are waiting against the door in hard winter light. None has a proper request form.

### Major scenes

1. **The queue before opening**
   - Callie sorts unsolicited requests: disputed signature, family Bible date, deed copy, anonymous marginal note.
   - Eli arrives for his part-time shift and starts a provenance intake grid rather than reading the contents.
   - Mae brings breakfast, leaves it in the front room, and notices Callie treating every envelope as an obligation.
   - The floorboard creaks naturally during work; Callie does not stop for it.

2. **Cross's bounded role appears in ordinary use**
   - Cross stops by with a signed county consultant form from a small archive-recovery question, not the Mercer case.
   - He reiterates that the arrangement is case by case and that public requests are not sheriff's work.
   - Their ease is professional and controlled, not banter or romance.

3. **Mercer summons**
   - Tess Rooke delivers Alton's written request for a same-day assessment of legal manuscripts scheduled for foundation transfer.
   - Alton has specifically asked for Callie, showing the town's changed regard.
   - Callie accepts because the collection may support archive recovery; she resents that acceptance feels expected.

4. **Mercer House collection review**
   - Callie and Eli enter under Tess's ledger procedure.
   - Alton writes current catalog cards and a repair ticket in front of them. Callie observes fading pressure, continuous joins, variable `g` descenders, and a hand made less decorative by pain but not less exact.
   - Alton corrects the title on Eli's grid, then recognizes that Eli left an honest blank rather than guess.
   - Nora identifies the 1999–2007 bench notebooks as Alton's “best hand.” Caleb handles tracing tissue for catalog photography. Claire arrives for her mother's correspondence. Gideon brings a foundation estimate. The suspects enter through work rather than a lineup.
   - Alton refuses to discuss a red portfolio and confirms a private appointment at nine the next morning.

5. **Private tensions**
   - Mae later supplies civic context: Alton preserved buildings but made people audition for his approval.
   - Callie notices the difference between being requested for expertise and being granted authority over a family.

### Suspect movement

- Nora: competent, attentive, protective of foundation schedule.
- Claire: angry about family papers passing to an institution.
- Gideon: defensive about project costs.
- Tess: anxious about Alton's health and routines.
- Caleb: possessive of collection access and worried about one folio.

### Clue introduction

- Alton's current hand.
- Bench notebooks as older formal-hand source.
- Repair ticket and chancery volume.
- Red portfolio and next-morning appointment.
- Nora's knowledge of “best hand.”
- House access ledger and side entrance.

### Clue reinterpretation

None yet. The chapter builds verified normal before death.

### Callie / Cross movement

The consultant role is shown as written, limited, and functional. Cross does not recruit her into private disputes.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae notices the request queue and leaves food without asking Callie to explain herself.

### Eli visible action

Builds an intake/provenance grid, handles no private page without permission, and earns Alton's grudging notice for leaving a blank.

### Emotional turn

Callie begins pleased that her work is finally recognized and ends uneasy that recognition arrives as access other people assume she will accept.

### Chapter-ending hook

At 8:47 p.m., unseen by Callie but later established through records, Alton leaves Daniel Pruitt a voicemail confirming the morning restatement. The chapter itself ends with Tess calling The Foxed Page at dawn: Alton is dead, and there is a note in his hand.

## Chapter 2 — A Note in His Hand

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Tuesday, January 26
- **Dramatic purpose:** Present the apparent suicide, enforce consultant boundaries, introduce the forged note, and create resistance to a murder interpretation.
- **Opening image:** Mercer House's side windows reflect snow-bright morning while Cross keeps Callie outside the study until Bell finishes the first photographic pass.

### Major scenes

1. **Discovery frame**
   - Tess recounts finding Alton in the reading chair at 8:05.
   - Empty old zolpidem bottle, brandy, note, no forced entry, and known distress support suicide.
   - Cross gives no death details beyond what Callie's role requires.

2. **Written authorization**
   - Cross issues a case-specific consultant memo: observe documents, compare authorized samples, no independent interviews, no removal, no public comment, stop when directed.
   - Callie accepts the limits because Book 4 taught her that boundaries can preserve the path.

3. **First view of note**
   - Bell photographs the note under scale and angled light before Callie approaches.
   - Callie reads the exact text.
   - She recognizes Alton's proportions and signature but sees the repeated lowercase `g` descender in **entangled** and **hearing**.
   - She states only: two forms repeat with unusual similarity; comparison is required.

4. **Language observation**
   - Alton names “the Preservation Fund,” omits exact account/date/amount, and leaves no instruction for recovering the discrepancy.
   - Cross warns that grief or finality could change language. Callie agrees; language alone does not prove authorship.

5. **Town and family resistance**
   - Nora asks for privacy and emphasizes Alton's fear of decline.
   - Claire accuses Nora of managing the family story.
   - Caleb says the note resembles Alton's older judicial hand.
   - Town callers already ask whether Callie intends to turn the death into another scandal.

### Suspect movement

- Tess rises due to medication and discovery access.
- Claire rises through inheritance anger.
- Caleb becomes relevant as handwriting expert with tracing tools.
- Nora appears reasonable and institutionally protective.

### Clue introduction

- Repeated `g`.
- Imprecise financial naming.
- Expired bottle.
- Wiped pen/glass noted by Bell, significance uncertain.
- Settee cushion rotated, not yet interpreted.

### Clue reinterpretation

- The familiar hand becomes a question rather than proof of suicide.
- The note's reasonable language begins to look designed for closure.

### Callie / Cross movement

Cross permits the observation but refuses a forgery declaration until samples and medical evidence exist. Callie respects the correction.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae provides no amateur diagnosis. She asks whether Callie has eaten and labels the waiting public requests by date.

### Eli visible action

Remains at the shop, secures Alton's shop paperwork, and waits for an authorized sample request.

### Emotional turn

Callie discovers that confidence now has public consequences: if she is wrong, the town will say her new role has made her hungry for murder.

### Chapter-ending hook

Under magnification, one repeated `g` contains the same tiny retraced hesitation in both words. Callie tells Cross, “Someone may have remembered the letter instead of written it.”

## Chapter 3 — The Comparison Room

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Wednesday, January 27
- **Dramatic purpose:** Build the verified comparison set, deepen five suspects through distinct document secrets, and introduce the genuine current repair ticket without exposing its hidden route.
- **Opening image:** Cross's records-room table is divided into lanes labeled `verified current`, `verified older`, `disputed`, and `not admitted`.

### Major scenes

1. **Sample protocol**
   - Cross and Bell require source, date, writing purpose, custody, and authentication for each item.
   - Callie teaches Eli the comparison grid, but Cross controls what becomes part of the case file.
   - Older bench notebooks strongly resemble the note's overall shape, keeping forgery uncertain.

2. **Mercer House sample search**
   - Claire's mother's letters are missing; Claire answers evasively rather than directly confirming the removal.
   - Tess's medication-return ledger shows an old 10 mg zolpidem bottle sealed for disposal.
   - Nora had authorized access as healthcare proxy.
   - Caleb's catalog kit includes graphite transfer and tracing tissue.
   - Gideon's annotated invoices contain Alton's correction hand and expose cost tension.

3. **Varied suspect encounters**
   - Winter garden conversation with Claire about family papers.
   - Foundation depot visit with Nora and Gideon about project records.
   - Collection room exchange with Caleb over the disputed folio.
   - Tess in the gate cottage, where domestic ledgers reveal how much of Alton's life depended on her invisible work.

4. **Shop sort**
   - Mae works through the returned Mercer volume under Cross's authorization and finds the January 25 repair ticket inside the chancery book.
   - Callie immediately stops the sort and calls Cross.
   - Invoice and Tess's recollection authenticate Alton's authorship/date.
   - Provenance entry accurately states that Mae found it in the returned volume; prior loose-paper location is not established.

5. **Current-hand contrast**
   - Repair ticket and same-day catalog cards show fading pressure, continuous joins, and variable forms absent from the note.
   - Callie still stops short of a final conclusion.

### Suspect movement

- Claire rises then partially reframes through letter theft.
- Tess rises through medication path.
- Caleb rises sharply through transfer tools.
- Gideon/Nora rise through invoice tension.

### Clue introduction

- Source/custody/currentness grid.
- Medication-return ledger.
- Tracing tools.
- Missing letters.
- Current repair ticket.
- Split-invoice categories.

### Clue reinterpretation

- Older formal resemblance is no longer enough; it may be the model.
- Current hand reveals body rhythm the note lacks.

### Callie / Cross movement

Cross trusts Callie to define sample quality but requires every page's path to be stated, including the repair-ticket gap.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae finds the page, hands it over without claiming meaning, and accepts the stop-work boundary.

### Eli visible action

Builds the comparison grid and labels the custody gap without volunteering a solution.

### Emotional turn

Callie allows Mae to be the finder and Eli to organize the samples without treating interpretation as possession.

### Chapter-ending hook

The note's hand matches Alton's remembered public image more closely than it matches the man who wrote the day before he died.

## Chapter 4 — The Same Letter Twice

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Date:** Thursday, January 28
- **Dramatic purpose:** Midpoint inversion. Make the forgery demonstrable and independently convert the death from apparent suicide to homicide.
- **Opening image:** Two enlarged lowercase `g`s sit side by side in Bell's photographs, their retraced descenders matching like duplicated damage.

### Major scenes

1. **Microscopic demonstration**
   - Callie compares the note to current ticket/cards and older bench books.
   - Bell photographs pen-lift pools inside `th`, `nd`, and `ri` joins.
   - Faint erased baselines and graphite around the signature appear under angled light.
   - Callie maps pressure across lines; note stays uniform while current genuine writing fades.
   - Artificial tremor appears only where imitation is visible.

2. **Cross's challenge**
   - Callie initially links smoothing language to Nora's foundation statements too quickly.
   - Cross stops her: shared institutional language is not authorship.
   - She narrows the conclusion to simulated writing and transferred signature; author unresolved.

3. **Portable proof packet**
   - Verified samples, macro images, source table, and Callie's observations are sent to a state examiner.
   - Cross records exactly what is known and what remains inference.

4. **Medical turn**
   - Dr. Helen Arledge reports zolpidem levels were incapacitating but not lethal.
   - Cause of death is smothering; actual killing remains off-page.
   - Rotated cushion becomes physical-scene relevance rather than atmosphere.

5. **Official homicide frame**
   - Cross changes the case classification and restricts trust/foundation records.
   - Nora's public privacy statement now functions as pressure, not proof.

### Suspect movement

- Caleb becomes strongest apparent forger.
- Tess remains relevant through medication.
- Financial beneficiaries become formal investigative targets.
- Nora is still one of several people with motive/access.

### Clue introduction

- Pen lifts, graphite, baseline, pressure, selective wobble.
- Medical cause.
- State examiner review.

### Clue reinterpretation

- Familiar letter forms are construction, not identity.
- Empty bottle is staging rather than cause.
- Cushion rotation becomes part of murder method.

### Callie / Cross movement

Cross trusts the forgery conclusion enough to act but does not let Callie name an author from vocabulary.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae hears the homicide classification and quietly moves the food from front room to a place Callie cannot avoid without making the act maternal theater.

### Eli visible action

Updates the grid from `authenticity disputed` to `simulation indicators documented` only after Cross authorizes the wording.

### Emotional turn

Callie proves the note was built and feels no triumph; a dead man's identity has been used as a room someone else entered.

### Chapter-ending hook

Cross receives Pruitt's message: Alton was scheduled to sign a trust restatement at nine the morning after he died.

## Chapter 5 — What the Trust Passed

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Friday, January 29
- **Dramatic purpose:** Make the inheritance mechanism exact, reveal the audit threat, and reorganize suspects around money, control, and family record ownership.
- **Opening image:** Daniel Pruitt places the 2021 trust summary and a blank space where the red portfolio should be on Cross's records-room table.

### Major scenes

1. **Current trust explained**
   - House and manuscript collection to foundation.
   - Claire $300,000 and sealed letters.
   - Tess $75,000 and lifetime cottage occupancy.
   - Nora ~$2.4 million residue and immediate successor-trustee power.
   - Caleb's purchase option on selected duplicates.

2. **Restatement and voicemail**
   - Pruitt authenticates Alton's 8:47 voicemail and next-morning appointment.
   - Draft restatement removes Nora, appoints independent trustee, orders audit, redirects residue.
   - Original draft is missing from red portfolio; Pruitt retains a working summary but not the signed original because signature never occurred.

3. **Foundation records**
   - Split invoices total $286,400.
   - Gideon's company performed real work but descriptions and funding categories were manipulated.
   - Nora authorized transfers.
   - Gideon claims Alton knew; note appears to support that claim.

4. **Suspect reversals**
   - Claire's anger is about letters and conditional inheritance.
   - Tess feared losing home but the draft preserved her occupancy.
   - Caleb's contract benefited from death but the restatement would not affect his option.
   - Nora's loss is uniquely immediate and large.

5. **Cross reins Callie in**
   - Callie sees Nora's motive and wants to read the note's absolution as personal.
   - Cross states: benefit is not authorship; audit threat is not presence; they still need death-window proof.

### Suspect movement

Nora and Gideon rise. Claire/Tess partially reframe. Caleb remains viable because forgery method and contract motive persist.

### Clue introduction

- Exact trust terms.
- $2.4M residue.
- $286,400 split invoices.
- Missing red portfolio.
- 8:47 voicemail.

### Clue reinterpretation

- Note's command to let the trust pass is operational, not merely emotional.
- Apparent suicide offers successor-trustee access and blame absorption.

### Callie / Cross movement

Cross demonstrates why legal investigation needs independent strands. Callie accepts that a correct reading can still be premature as an accusation.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae explains that Alton made generosity conditional. She does not tell Callie whom to suspect.

### Eli visible action

Aligns invoice descriptions with project ledgers and flags category splits without interpreting motive.

### Emotional turn

Callie recognizes a distorted reflection: Alton made usefulness the price of belonging, and Nora may have built a life inside that rule.

### Chapter-ending hook

The study-access ledger places Nora out at 8:05, but the return-time ink has been written over an erasure.

## Chapter 6 — The Hand That Waited

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Saturday, January 30
- **Dramatic purpose:** Reconstruct access and time, clear strong false paths, expose Callie's overextension, and give Cross enough for targeted warrants without resolving the case.
- **Opening image:** The Mercer House access ledger lies beneath the glass while winter sun reveals a roughened patch where a time was erased.

### Major scenes

1. **Ledger and side-door reconstruction**
   - Nora's exit `8:05` is darker, compressed, and written over erased fibers.
   - Side-door record shows her code at 9:14.
   - She says she returned for a scarf.
   - Tess's 7:50 household note records the scarf already placed in Nora's car.

2. **Medication path**
   - Return ledger identifies the discontinued 10 mg zolpidem bottle and its incomplete return path.
   - Nora had healthcare-proxy access to the return box.
   - Empty bottle at scene matches old label/refill rather than Alton's current medication.

3. **False-path resolution in motion**
   - Claire's ride receipt and store camera place her away.
   - Gideon's crew records, fuel receipt, and witnesses place him at county site.
   - Caleb's 8:23 courier scan and shop camera place him away through the critical window, but his tracing tools still make him a plausible apparent forger.
   - Tess's authenticated exterior movement and door records place her at the gate cottage through the critical window.

4. **Caleb's separate wrongdoing**
   - The disputed folio is recovered. He admits provenance substitution and removal.
   - His graphite sheets lack the note's signature transfer and phrase practice.
   - Cross refers the misconduct but does not force it into the murder.

5. **Public cost at the shop**
   - Callie returns to find more requests and two townspeople waiting after closing.
   - She starts another intake rather than eat or rest.
   - Mae places a pencil label across half the stack: `NOT TODAY`.
   - No speech. Callie leaves the label instead of removing it.

6. **Warrant threshold**
   - Cross has forged note, homicide, motive, access conflict, and medication path.
   - He seeks targeted foundation/office warrants and examiner return, not an arrest yet.

### Suspect movement

- Claire, Gideon, Tess, and Caleb lose critical-window strength but retain separate secrets.
- Nora becomes the only suspect with a false exit, late code, trust benefit, and medication access.

### Clue introduction

- Altered exit.
- 9:14 code.
- Scarf contradiction.
- Old-bottle ledger.
- False suspect alibis.

### Clue reinterpretation

- Tess's medication irregularity helps identify Nora's staging path.
- Caleb's tools are capability, not authorship.
- Gideon's financial guilt is not physical guilt.

### Callie / Cross movement

Cross uses Callie's document observation in the warrant affidavit but states the electronic/access corroboration separately.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae sets a practical boundary on public dependence. Callie accepts it without gratitude language.

### Eli visible action

Completes comparison and invoice grids, then closes the shop register when Mae labels the request stack. He does not volunteer a solution.

### Emotional turn

Callie sees that being needed can become a way to never stop, never grieve, and never ask whether the work arrived honestly.

### Chapter-ending hook

Under angled light, Alton's appointment pad shows only the partial pressure image `REMOVE N.M.S. / IND. TRUSTEE / AUDIT`; the complete source document, writer, remover, and location remain unestablished.

## Chapter 7 — The Page Under Pressure

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Date:** Sunday, January 31
- **Dramatic purpose:** Synthesize the proof, recover decisive warrant evidence, and place arrest authority entirely in Cross's hands.
- **Opening image:** Words absent from the top sheet rise as shadow in the appointment pad beneath: removal, trustee, audit.

### Major scenes

1. **Pressure-image reconstruction**
   - Bell photographs low-angle views.
   - Callie reads only partial terms; Pruitt authenticates them against his draft summary.
   - The partial terms corroborate removal, independent-trustee, and audit pressure without identifying the complete source document or its location.

2. **Examiner corroboration**
   - State report confirms indicators of simulated body text and transferred signature.
   - Report notes repeated forms, unnatural pen lifts, guideline residue, and graphite.
   - Cross separates examiner conclusion from authorship identity.

3. **Financial and access synthesis**
   - Bank/foundation records confirm Nora authorization and personal/residual benefit.
   - Access records and medication ledger support warrant scope.
   - Cross walks the affidavit with Callie only for the document-observation paragraph.

4. **Warrant search of foundation office**
   - Practice sheets contain exact note phrases and repeated `g` exercises.
   - Graphite transfer sheet carries reversed Alton signature.
   - Bench-note photocopies have difficult joins circled.
   - Missing red-portfolio restatement is in a foundation-history box.
   - Empty medication-return envelope is in a shredding bin among ordinary disposal papers.
   - Evidence is photographed and logged by Bell; Callie does not participate in seizure.

5. **No easy emotional victory**
   - Nora remains at a board meeting elsewhere during the search, publicly competent.
   - Gideon realizes the audit and murder may both point toward his wife but does not provide a melodramatic betrayal.

6. **Arrest warrant**
   - Cross completes probable cause: homicide, staging, forged note, motive, access, practice, removed restatement.
   - He obtains the warrant.

### Suspect movement

Nora becomes the only viable killer. Others settle into separate misconduct, grief, or complicity.

### Clue introduction

- Pressure image.
- Examiner report.
- Practice sheets.
- Graphite signature transfer.
- Missing restatement.
- Medication envelope.

### Clue reinterpretation

- The note's resemblance to Alton is proof of study, not authenticity.
- The foundation office's orderly files are the hiding place because Nora believes work legitimizes possession.

### Callie / Cross movement

Callie gives Cross a precise document paragraph; Cross builds and owns the legal case. He does not ask her whether to arrest.

### Callie / Mae movement

Mae waits at the shop with the `NOT TODAY` label still in place and makes no demand for news.

### Eli visible action

Maintains the authenticated comparison list and notices that the repair ticket is not needed in the final warrant paragraph; he appears relieved for ordinary custody reasons.

### Emotional turn

Callie understands that proof becomes safe only when it can leave her hands and remain itself.

### Chapter-ending hook

Cross closes the warrant folder and says, “Now it can travel.”

## Chapter 8 — The Current Hand

- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,100
- **Date:** Monday, February 1
- **Dramatic purpose:** Conduct a legally credible arrest without confession, resolve the individual case and estate consequences, complete character movement, and leave the planted-page seam quiet.
- **Opening image:** Nora sits at the sheriff's records-room table with her hands folded over no paper at all.

### Major scenes

1. **Controlled interview**
   - Cross states recording, counsel rights, and document sequence.
   - He presents medical cause, 8:47 voicemail, trust/restatement, altered access, 9:14 code, medication path, and warrant returns.
   - Callie speaks only when Cross asks her to explain how the note was simulated.
   - She describes repeated forms, pen lifts, pressure, graphite, and current-hand mismatch without assigning motive.

2. **Guilty knowledge without confession**
   - Nora uses “pass as written” before Cross quotes that line from the note.
   - She argues that Alton was destroying the foundation and that Callie cannot understand the cost of preserving real institutions.
   - She does not confess. She stops answering and invokes counsel.

3. **Arrest**
   - Cross arrests Nora for Alton's murder and related forgery/evidence offenses based on the warrant and cumulative record.
   - No struggle, chase, or villain speech.

4. **Aftermath**
   - Court freezes trust administration; independent temporary trustee appointed.
   - Foundation board removes Nora temporarily and opens audit.
   - Claire receives controlled access to her mother's letters.
   - Tess's cottage status is protected pending probate.
   - Gideon's invoices and Caleb's provenance issue continue separately.

5. **Character resolution**
   - Town praise and new requests reach a peak. Callie does not enjoy being celebrated; the shop feels crowded by need.
   - Cross closes the case-specific consultant scope and gives Callie a copy of the final document-observation summary.
   - Mae keeps `NOT TODAY` on the request stack and asks where the next label belongs.
   - Eli returns to ordinary work, steady and apparently loyal.

6. **Quiet provenance limit**
   - Cross asks Callie to sign the repair-ticket provenance statement.
   - It records: `Found in returned Mercer volume by M. Hartwell; prior loose-paper location not established.`
   - The ticket was independently authenticated and is not cited as decisive arrest evidence.
   - Callie dislikes the unanswered route but files it as a minor shop gap.
   - No one treats the unresolved prior location as part of the murder resolution.

### Suspect movement

Nora arrested. Other suspect secrets receive proportionate outcomes without being made innocent saints.

### Clue payoff

- Repeated `g` and pen lifts.
- Current-hand ticket and catalog cards.
- Graphite signature.
- Medical cause.
- Trust/restatement.
- Access ledger and code.
- Medication path.
- Practice sheets and missing portfolio.

### Callie / Cross movement

The bounded arrangement succeeds. Cross trusts Callie's expertise, challenges it, translates it, and establishes the arrest independently.

### Callie / Mae movement

Their new normal holds through a practical limit and shared labeling, not apology or restoration speech.

### Eli visible action

Returns the chancery volume to its correct shelf and updates the shop log with the provenance gap exactly as Cross recorded it.

### Emotional turn

Callie accepts that being useful is not the same as being held, and that genuine evidence can remain genuine even when its complete prior route is unavailable.

### Final image

The brass magnifying glass rests over the genuine repair ticket while Mae's `NOT TODAY` label lies across the public request stack. Callie files the exact provenance statement, accepts that the route gap is non-dispositive, closes the case file, and the final line remains: `She closed the file.`
