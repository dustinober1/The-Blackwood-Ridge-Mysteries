## Gut reaction (3–5 sentences, in Priya's voice)
This is how you do a cold-entry mystery. Chapter 1 drops you into Callie's shop with four weird envelopes and a dead judge by page 30 — no exposition dump, no "as you know" dialogue. The handwriting gimmick (simulated note, transferred signature) is procedural catnip but never feels like a lecture; every comparison scene has Cross's fence keeping it honest. I tapped "next" at every chapter break. Only knock: the cast balloons fast and Nora's motive cluster (money + control + audit) feels slightly overdetermined by Chapter 5.

## What worked (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Hook by page 1:** Ch1 opens with envelopes pushed through the mail slot before frost leaves the brick (39–48). Dead judge call at line 401 — zero throat-clearing.
- **Tight chapters, cliffhanger-lite:** Ch2 ends on "Someone may have remembered the letter instead of written it" (757–758). Ch4 ends with Cross writing HOMICIDE (1361). Ch7 ends with arrest warrant "Now it can travel" (2310). Each lands a beat without cheating.
- **Procedural integrity:** Cross's authorization fence (Ch2 489–508), four-lane comparison room (Ch3 764–772), state examiner corroboration without ID (Ch7 2096–2112). The method *is* the pleasure.
- **Cold-entry clarity:** Never needed prior books. Callie's role (case-by-case consultant), Cross (procedural anchor), Mae/Eli (shop rhythm) establish fast. Mercer family dynamics conveyed through conflict, not backstory (Ch1 277–299).
- **Repair ticket as golden thread:** Found in Ch3 (1013–1031), becomes V-C-6, anchors current-hand comparison through Ch4–Ch7. Imperfect route (1085–1086) stays visible — honesty > neatness.

## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Cast bloat by Ch1 end:** Seven named Mercer-adjacent adults (Alton, Nora, Claire, Tess, Caleb, Gideon, Tess) in 370 lines. Hard to track who wants what until Ch5 trust breakdown (1447–1481).
- **Nora's motive pile-up:** By Ch5 she has residue ($2.4M), successor control, audit threat, independent trustee, signed transfers ($286K), altered ledger, 9:14 code, scarf contradiction, medication access (1651–1658). Feels like *every* arrow points at her — reduces suspense about *who* before Ch7 search confirms.
- **Ch5–Ch6 exposition density:** Trust terms, restatement clauses, financial grid, access ledger, electronic codes, medication ledger all in ~600 lines. Slows the "turn the page" rhythm that Ch1–Ch4 nailed.
- **Caleb's folio subplot resolves off the death question:** Ch6 1929–1937 shows his deception but Cross explicitly separates it (1937). Feels like a B-plot that doesn't pay into the A-plot.
- **Final interview (Ch8) leans on echo:** Nora's "structure he built should not pass as written" (2408–2410) mirrors note language *before* Cross shows it — clever but slightly theatrical for a procedural that's been rigorously anti-theatrical.

## Public review draft (the review Priya would actually post, 80–250 words)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ "The Planted Page" works cold — I'd never read this series and didn't need to. Callie Thorne is an antiquarian bookseller turned records consultant; Sheriff Cross is the procedural fence that keeps her honest. Judge Mercer dies with a confession note in his hand. Everyone in Blackwood Ridge wants to call it suicide. Callie sees the handwriting doesn't *move* right.

What follows is the most satisfying document-comparison mystery I've read in years. No magic, no gut hunches — just magnifying glass, macro photos, a four-lane evidence table (verified current/older/disputed/not admitted), and a state examiner who corroborates the method without naming the writer. The note simulates Mercer's *old* public hand but lacks his *current* fatigue, tremor, and rhythm. The signature was transferred via graphite. The medical examiner calls it homicide (smothering + zolpidem). The trust gives Nora $2.4M and control — unless the unsigned restatement removes her. She altered her exit time, her door code opened the study at 9:14, the scarf alibi contradicts the housekeeper's log, and the search warrant finds practice sheets, a reversed signature transfer, the missing restatement, and an empty pill envelope in *her* office.

Tight chapters, zero bloat, respects your intelligence. The cast balloons early and Nora's motive stack gets heavy, but the procedural spine never bends. One-sitting read. Buying Book 1 tomorrow.

## One change that would flip my rating
Trim the Mercer House ensemble in Ch1 by 2–3 characters (merge Gideon into Nora's staff role, fold Caleb's access into the reproduction-tools thread). Fewer names = sharper suspicion management without losing the household texture.
