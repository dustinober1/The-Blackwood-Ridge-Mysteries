# Lauren Coalter

> Present only as absence — in photographs, in memory, in the cipher, and at the
> bottom of Cedar Lake. She is the book's ghost and its engine.

## Role
The missing student. Seventeen the summer she vanished (1994). Officially a
presumed runaway for thirty-two years. In truth: dead at the old dock at
Blackwater Cove, Cedar Lake, the night of the graduation party — struck her head
in a fight with her boyfriend Wells Ambler and did not surface.

## Who she was (as remembered)
Bright, restless, ready to leave. Marguerite's favorite student — in the garden
club, sharp with plant lore, the kind of girl who annotated her own books for
fun (the habit Marguerite loved). She had a summer job linedued and a plan to
get out of Blackwood Ridge, which is part of why no one who loved her believed
she ran. She was leaving; she was not fleeing.

## The night
She was leaving Wells too. The fight on the dock was about her going — he
couldn't take it, shoved her, and the rest is the book's buried core. She did
not die because she was reckless; she died because she was leaving and someone
could not let her.

## What remains of her
- Photographs (in Doris's house, in Marguerite's effects, in old yearbooks).
- Her own annotations — she marked her books the way Marguerite taught her. A
  small mirror: Callie reads two hands in this book, Marguerite's and, through
  Marguerite's teaching, Lauren's ghost of one.
- The garden club's memory of her.
- Her mother's thirty-two years of not knowing.

## Relationships
- **Wells Ambler (killer):** Her boyfriend. She was ending it. He ended her.
- **Marguerite Dabney (mentor):** The teacher who saw her clearly and failed to
  save her.
- **Doris Coalter (mother):** The wound Doris carries. Lauren is the center of
  Doris's life and the reason for its shape.

## Function in the book
Lauren is the human cost made vivid. She must not be a symbol — she must be a
specific, almost-reachable girl. Every time Callie finds a trace of her (a
photo, a margin note, a garden-club anecdote), the reader should feel the
thirty-two years of absence as a physical weight. The moment her absence
becomes a recovered body is the book's procedural and emotional climax.

## Notes
- Keep her real, not idealized. Doris and Marguerite remember her fondly, but
  the text should let her be seventeen — funny, impatient, a little cruel
  sometimes, fully alive.
- She is the answer to the cipher. The flowers encode her name, her last night,
  and where she is.
