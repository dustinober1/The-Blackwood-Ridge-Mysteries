# Book Premise

## Logline
When beloved retired teacher Marguerite Dabney dies of a long illness and her
annotated botanical book surfaces at The Foxed Page, Callie Thorne decodes the
cipher the dying woman left behind — and finds not a confession of guilt but a
thirty-year-old accusation aimed at the most respected man in Blackwood Ridge.

## Theme
The cost of speaking too late. Marguerite waited thirty years and then waited
until she was dying to say what she knew, encoding it for a stranger she trusted
to read it. Callie must deliver a dead woman's truth to a living town that does
not want it — and ask whether a truth told thirty years late is still worth the
burn. Underneath: what do we owe the dead when their truth harms the living?

## Central conflict
Who vanished Lauren Coalter the summer of 1994, and can Callie prove it from
the ciphered margins of a dead teacher's botanical book — when the only named
suspect is Wells Ambler, the beloved high school principal, and the whole town
would rather the dead keep their secrets?

## Protagonist (sketch)
Callie Thorne — wants to honor the trust a dying stranger placed in her and
finish what the woman could not; her wound is that she has just learned (Book 1)
that uncovering the past wounds the town she lives in, and she is no longer sure
the cost is hers to pay.

## Opposing force
The town itself — polite, protective, exhausted by Callie after Book 1 — and
behind it Wells Ambler, a man whose respectable present is built on a buried
night in 1994. The opposing force is not a villain twirling a mustache; it is a
community that has decided, after the founding scandal, that it cannot afford a
second truth.

## Stakes
- For Doris Coalter, Lauren's elderly mother: thirty-two years of not knowing —
  answered, or shattered, because Doris trusts Wells and has for decades.
- For the town: losing a second pillar (after Gus Ralston) in a year, to the
  same woman's hand.
- For Callie: if she is wrong, she has falsely accused a beloved man from a dead
  woman's puzzle and confirmed the town's worst opinion of her; if she is right,
  she is useful, not welcome — exactly the pariah state she has not earned her
  way out of.

## Setting & tone
Late spring in Blackwood Ridge — the school year ending, Marguerite's garden
club preparing its annual show, peonies and roses in full bloom as a buried
death surfaces. The warmth is deliberate and ironic: everything flowering while
a thirty-year-old grave is opened by ink. Same two-register voice as Book 1
(cozy sensory blanket over a gothic chill), but the chill here is colder because
the town is already wounded and flinches from Callie on contact.

## Comps
1. *The Book of Cold Cases* (Simone St. James) — a cold case pursued through an
   old document and the handwriting inside it.
2. *A Potting Shed Mystery* (Marty Wingate) — botanical cozy with moral weight.
3. *Books Can Be Deceiving* (Jenn McKinlay) — bookish cozy scaffolding.

## Key objects (canon seeds for /novella-bible)
- **"Flora's Lexicon of the Virginias" (1873)** — fictional regional Victorian
  floriography; the annotated book. Marguerite built her cipher from its plant
  symbolism (each plate's flower = a coded letter/word) layered with her own
  shorthand. Callie reads it the way she reads historical marginalia.
- **The botanical book itself** — 19th-century, hand-colored plates, arrived in
  an estate lot at The Foxed Page (per Book 1 ending). In Book 2 Callie
  identifies the hand as Marguerite Dabney's and learns the lot was the Dabney
  estate — the dying teacher's deliberate message-in-a-bottle aimed at her.

## Series-slot alignment (tight mode)

  it exonerates the teacher and points to someone still in town (Wells Ambler).
- Honors series advancement: Callie solves the case but gains no goodwill;
  remains a pariah, "useful, not welcome."
- Honors carry-forward constraints: founding fraud is not re-exposed; Callie's
