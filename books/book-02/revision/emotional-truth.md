## Chapter 1 — The Estate Lot
- **Intended emotional movement:** Guarded reclamation (set bone, cold tea, town-flinching) → pressure (book she won't touch, Eli's revelation) → turn (picks up the glass) → residue (curiosity returns fused with cost-awareness; she drinks cold tea anyway).
- **Reader-felt movement:** Guarded distance → growing pull → earned turn → quiet commitment. The aconite passage (hatred crossed out) and the dying-hand detail (pen pressure fading) are the chapter's strongest emotional beats per beta reader.
- **Truth gap:** None. Intended emotion landed. The one flat stretch is the Marguerite memory block (L74), which reads as exposition rather than lived experience — the emotion is correct but the delivery stalls the chapter's momentum.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "She set the glass down. Gently. On the felt rest. She did not let go of it." + "Callie picked up her tea. It had gone cold. She drank it anyway." — the glass ritual and the cold-tea close are the chapter's emotional spine. Protect both.
- **Next-chapter carry:** Callie has chosen curiosity again, with full knowledge of the cost. The residue is: commitment fused with dread, the bergamot "almost right" (not yet), and the weight of a dead woman's trust. Ch2 should open with Callie acting on that commitment (going to the garden).

## Chapter 2 — Floriography
- **Intended emotional movement:** Intellectual curiosity (garden as cipher to decode) → pressure (the garden is a life, not a puzzle) → turn (the mystery is three decades cold; a mother has waited 32 years) → residue (rosemary/remembrance, the book heavier — it's a girl now).
- **Reader-felt movement:** Decoding eagerness → growing weight as garden reads as biography → gut-punch at Doris's flat revelation → quiet devastation at the gate scene. The empty bed (pulled-up hatreds) and Doris's palms-flat posture are the chapter's strongest beats.
- **Truth gap:** The puzzle→person turn lands at the close but Callie is passive through the first two-thirds — the private want (to deserve the bet) is felt but never dramatized as action. The opening lacks Ch1's emotional bridge (cost-accepted weight absent).
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "the rosemary meant remembrance, and the remembrance meant thirty-two years" recursive chain. "It was a girl. A girl who had not come home." Rosemary smell on Callie's hands.
- **Next-chapter carry:** Callie now reads *for* Doris, *for* Lauren — the cipher is charged, not clinical. The "W." initial from Ch1 is pulling her toward Ch3's meeting with Wells. She carries the weight of a dead woman's trust AND a living woman's command.

## Chapter 3 — The Open Palm
- **Intended emotional movement:** Intellectual pleasure (decoding the floriographic cipher) → pressure (Wells's warmth-as-perimeter; the liking-vs-looking conflict) → turn (open palm filed as notation, not suspicion) → residue (begun to look sideways at a beloved man; decided to pay the cost) + cipher's first clear phrase (date, place by water).
- **Reader-felt movement:** Decoding eagerness → ambush of her own past at the school → Wells's genuine warmth disarming her (the Gatsby story, the laughter she didn't want) → the held smile and the hand closing on the cup → Cross confirming the cost → the cracked shorthand landing as a stone. Strongest beats: "Everyone's sorry." (grief), the half-second palm (dread), "I've spent my whole life trying to be the kind of man she would have wanted" (the dangerous sympathy).
- **Truth gap:** The turn (open palm as notation) lands cleanly and is honored to the final line — Callie stays *committed, not certain*. BUT the opening drops Ch2's carry: the chapter opens on decoding *pleasure* ("for the first time in months, a pleasure") with the Doris-command residue absent; Callie reads for the puzzle before reading for Doris. The Ch2→Ch3 emotional bridge is missing at the open — now a structural habit across three consecutive soft openings.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "warmth could be a perimeter." The half-second palm held too long. The ice-over-moving-water image. "He came back alone." / "A fact. A stone." — the refusal to convert seeing into accusation, which is Marguerite's method and now Callie's inheritance. The bergamot still on her tongue from Wells's kettle, carrying suspicion into the Cross scene.
- **Next-chapter carry:** Callie has the first clear phrase and the open palm filed under *deliberate*. The cost is no longer hypothetical — she has begun to look sideways at a beloved man and knows exactly what that cost Gus Ralston's victims and the town. Ch4 ("The Wet Hemline") must either deepen the dock memory (if "He came back alone" stays in Ch3) or deliver it as the first true revelation (if pulled back to Ch4). The invoice the beta reader asks for should begin to arrive.

## Chapter 4 — The Wet Hemline
- **Intended emotional movement:** Decoding absorption (charged — the passage she decodes *is* the Doris-command made good) → wet hemline lands ("not proof and not nothing") → Wells's social cage via Harriet (town closes a rank) → witnessing Wells/Doris tenderness (the cruelest cover) → Wells at gate testing her → Doris's withdrawal → alone, archive closed, Doris closed, only dead woman as ally, cold tea, next plate.

- **Truth gap:** The middle stretch (L66-71) registers Wells's social cage as craft Callie admires rather than pressure she feels — analysis, not wound. One felt beat (the specific shape of the hurt — being made the town's wound again) would viscera it without breaking voice. Also: Callie never entertains doubt ("what if Marguerite was wrong?"), which the outline mandates — her certainty flattens the evidentiary problem.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** The wet hemline (middle-of-the-road dust-avoidance). "he had built it so carefully that he could no longer find the seam." - **Next-chapter carry:** Callie is now isolated — archive closed, Doris's kitchen closed, only the dead woman as ally. She carries a suspicion that isn't proof and has been told to "find the rest." The Doris thread is the reader's strongest open loop. Ch5 (midpoint) must deliver Mae's redemptive beat (relief) and re-touch Doris's kitchen, or the emotional weight compounds past the cozy ceiling. The cipher's remaining plates pull forward. Callie needs an active path past the closed archive.

## Chapter 5 — The Language of Flowers (MIDPOINT)
- **Intended emotional movement:** Doubt (cipher opens → "not enough for a warrant" → "Say it" → weight of trust) → Mae's relief (chosen, not alone — "It's enough") → decision (Mae tips Callie from doubt to resolve) → cost accepted (Cross reads first, door not opened) → aftertaste (empty hands, quiet of having decided).
- **Reader-felt movement:** Cipher-as-map reframe (inhale) → Marguerite's direct address "Say it" (broke a little — first time the dead woman speaks, and what she asks for is *voice*) → Mae's confession ("I understood enough to be afraid of it" — whole character in one sentence; shoulder touch = warmed, not healed) → bergamot ritual with agency underneath → Cross's "I'll think about it" (satisfying because he changed his method, not his mind) → empty hands + "the rest was not hers." Strongest beat: Marguerite's "Say it" (trust as weight). Second: Mae's "I understood enough to be afraid of it."
- **Truth gap:** The decision turn ("She decided." L88) is the chapter's weakest moment — the reader is told Mae tipped Callie from doubt to decision, but never *feels* the tipping. The doubt (not enough for a warrant, the town doesn't want to be dragged again) is established at the open and never directly wrestled with. It dissolves between paragraphs. Also: the Doris re-touch the emotional-truth carry mandated is absent — Doris's closed kitchen and shaking needles don't press on Callie's decision. The chapter has five big emotional beats (cipher reveal, Marguerite's plea, Mae's confession, bergamot, Cross) — rich, almost heavy, and Mae/Cross back-to-back slightly dilute each other.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "It was a map." Marguerite's directive "Say it" and the weight being trust, not accusation. Mae's "I did the thing, Callie. The thing I always do" and "I understood enough to be afraid of it." "It's enough." The bergamot ritual with Eleanor's tapping count — "Almost right. Not yet" (agency underneath, not defeat). Cross squaring the pages "carefully, the way a man squares a stack of paper he is not going to throw away." "the saying was in the sheriff's hands, and her hands were empty." "Marguerite had written *say it*. Callie had said it. The rest was not hers."
- **Next-chapter carry:** Callie has decided and acted — the cipher is in Cross's hands, her hands are empty. Cross said "I'll think about it" — the door exists but is not open. Ch6 ("The Closing Ranks") must re-arm threat fast (the cost arrives: town closing ranks, Doris pulling furthest away, Wells's charm offensive). The two-alibi gap (Harlow barn vs. party-then-home) must surface — Callie should name it. The Doris thread must return (she'll learn Callie went to the sheriff). The bergamot compass should NOT escalate further (Ch5's "Not yet" is the peak before Ch8's resolution).

## Chapter 6 — The Closing Ranks (ALL IS LOST / DARK NIGHT)
- **Intended emotional movement:** Isolation compounding (Mon–Thu calendar pressure, "Callie was the blade") → near-stopping (back room, "she could stop," simple animal fact of aloneness — true floor) → turn (Eli's question: ordinary animal fact of not being alone; door-bell inversion) → forward pull (Cross calls, acts without warmth, "wear boots") → end state (lying down as faith, glass hers by choice, thread still pulling).
- **Reader-felt movement:** Town-closing-ranks accumulation (Linda Coale, clerk, garden club through windows, Doris via Mae) landing in chest → the unfinished "The groceries —" as sharpest cut → the near-stopping ("no one would know, and no one would have to" — temptation written as kindness, believed for a full paragraph she might stop) → Eli's bell as air re-entering room → "She did not stop." as standalone hinge → Cross's "wear boots" as care-without-warmth. Strongest beat: Eli asking to be taught ("asking Callie Thorne to teach him to read"). Second: the unfinished groceries sentence.
- **Truth gap:** The near-stopping is the chapter's floor and earns its depth — but it duplicates Ch5's exit-beat ("she could be careful / that was the exit") without sufficient differentiation. The despair is mood-driven rather than evidence-defeated: Callie has a concrete evidentiary lever (the two-alibi gap, which she named to Cross in Ch5) but never reaches for it in the back room. One beat of her grasping for the contradiction and finding it still isn't enough would sharpen why stopping feels logical, not just exhausted. Also: the omniscient lean at L80 ("neither of them knows it will matter for years") breaks limited-third for the first time in six chapters — the apprenticeship seed doesn't need the narrator to announce it.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "Callie was the blade." The unfinished "The groceries —". "the simple animal fact of a woman sitting by herself in a building that used to be full." "Everyone's hand is a voice... some people's hands are — saying." "Could you teach me? To see that?" "I can try." "She did not stop." (standalone). "Wear boots. It's muddy at the cove." The lying-down-as-faith. The glass hers "by choice and not by ghost."
- **Next-chapter carry:** Tomorrow they go to the lake — the dive team, 6 a.m., "wear boots." The lake might give Lauren back, or it might give nothing. Callie carries Eli's question into the cove ("could you teach me" still in her). Doris does not know the search is happening. Wells is offstage — the longer he stays offstage the more ominous he gets. Ch7 ("Blackwater Cove") is the CLIMAX: the search, the body recovery, the confrontation. The bergamot compass must NOT appear in Ch7 (reserved for Ch8). Harlan Beech's Ch5 look-up still unresolved — needs pick-up in Ch7 or Ch8.

- **Intended emotional movement:** Boots-as-narrowing (contract to the doable) → cove breaks the narrowing (the town's weight returns, "the finding was not hers") → ring (private want satisfied without satisfaction) → bones/giving-back (Lauren recovered) → hallway dread (being right has become being afraid) → confrontation (Wells speaks to Lauren/room, forbidden question surfaces, Callie refuses absolution) → arrest-as-process-not-justice → shop ("the quiet was heavy, and it was earned").
- **Reader-felt movement:** The cove as held breath for six chapters finally exhaling into a body in a bag → the ring landing harder than bones (bones confirmed; ring *knew*) → Cross's hand staying at his neck (procedural mask over friendship collapsing — "the only part of him allowed to know what the rest of him was feeling") → Wells's confession breaking at the trousers ("I walked in the middle of the road so the dust wouldn't show" — margin note made flesh) → "Tell me it counted" as line of the chapter → hand closing (not into a fist but into a closing) → Callie's non-absolution → "she didn't run. She never ran." → shop smell as home, "not ready for tea." Strongest beat: Wells's confession at the trousers (margin-to-mouth collapse). Second: "Tell me it counted" + closing hand.
- **Truth gap:** The forbidden question (L120) is named cleanly but not inhabited — Callie states it then moves immediately to her answer. The reader registers it intellectually; it should land viscerally. Needs one beat of Callie genuinely tempted by the equivalence (she DOES exhume regardless of cost) before choosing non-absolution. Also: Wells's relief (outline's named aftertaste — "strange, terrible relief") is underplayed — terror dramatized fully, relief implied but not named as release. Also: the Marguerite-vindication half of Callie's private want ("Marguerite was not wrong; she was not cowardly; she was just too late") gets one retrospective beat but is never felt during the search itself.
- **Aftertaste to preserve:** "She wore boots." The cove — "water that does not move and has not moved in a long time." "four stumps... like rotted teeth." "She knew" (ring). "Small bones, the bones of a girl." Cross's hand at his neck — "the only part of him allowed to know." "this is a body now." Wells's broken confession syntax. "Tell me it counted." The forbidden question — stone in a current. "You built a good life, Wells. You built it over her. Both of those things are real." "not into a fist but into a closing." "I've always known." "she didn't run. She never ran." "She did not make tea. She was not ready for tea." "the quiet was heavy, and it was earned."
- **Next-chapter carry:** Ch8 ("What the Garden Knew") is the RESOLUTION: Doris learns the truth (the book's hardest scene — the grocery bag cannot stay closed); the garden dissolution (Marguerite's final cipher read); Eli's last question; Mae's quiet cup; the bergamot compass finally resolves ("almost right. Not quite."); Callie's pariah state confirmed (useful, not welcome). The bergamot compass MUST land in Ch8 — Ch7 withheld it correctly. Callie carries the weight of the arrest, the forbidden question (unanswered), and "not ready for tea" (the threshold not yet crossed). Doris is the reader's strongest open loop — the book owes that conversation.
