# Publishing listing — The Botanical Confession

## Title clearance

### Pressure-test

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Length & rhythm | Three words, literary rhythm. "Botanical" + "Confession" is an unusual, evocative pairing that signals both the floral device and the buried-truth theme. Fits the elevated, atmospheric register established by Book 1's "The Annotated Murder." |
| Genre signal | "Confession" carries mystery weight; "Botanical" signals the floral/floriography device. Not instantly legible as *cozy* — reads closer to literary mystery — which matches Book 1's positioning (the cover and blurb close the cozy gap). |
| Memorability & searchability | Distinctive phrase. No competing titles clutter the search results, so this book owns the query from launch. |
| Spoiler / theme alignment | Telegraphs the device (floriographic encoding) and the theme (a confession finally spoken) without revealing the accusation, the body, or the killer. Accurate to the book's moral weight. |

## Book description

### Blurb (primary)

A dying woman encoded the truth in flowers. Callie Thorne has to learn the language before the town buries it for good.

When retired teacher Marguerite Dabney passes after a long illness, the only thing she leaves behind at The Foxed Page bookshop is a nineteenth-century botanical volume — every plate marked in the silent language of flowers. Rosemary for remembrance. Rue for regret. Aconite for hatred.

To anyone else it's a curiosity. To Callie Thorne, who reads marginalia the way other people read faces, it's a cipher.

Marguerite didn't leave a confession. She left an accusation — one aimed at the most respected man in Blackwood Ridge. Thirty years ago, on the night of the Cedar Lake graduation party, seventeen-year-old Lauren Coalter vanished and the town called it "runaway." Marguerite saw something that night and never spoke it aloud. Now she's entrusted it, in ink and petal, to a stranger she believed would actually read it.

But Blackwood Ridge has had enough of Callie Thorne. After she pried open the town's founding fraud, she's useful — not welcome. And the man at the center of the cipher still brings groceries to the grieving mother every Thursday.

As the cipher yields its witness statement flower by flower, Callie must ask what the dead are owed when their truth costs the living everything.

*Perfect for readers of Simone St. James, Ellery Adams, and Richard Osman — atmospheric amateur-sleuth fiction for anyone who believes every book holds a secret only the right reader can hear.*

**The Blackwood Ridge Mysteries, Book 2.** Each novella resolves its own case; the series arc deepens with every volume.

## Pricing guidance

**Recommended launch price: $2.99** (matching Book 1 for series consistency).

- $2.99 is the floor of the 70% royalty band (~$2.09 earned per sale vs. ~$0.35 at $0.99).
- Matching Book 1's price avoids the "Book 2 price-gouge" perception that series readers are sensitive to.
- At ~22,600 words (~90 KENPC pages), one full KU read ≈ $0.41. Cozy readers are heavy KU consumers — enrollment is essential.
- The series-reader cross-sell from Book 1 is the primary growth driver; a Book 2 that costs the same as Book 1 removes price friction from the series continuation.
- After 90 days, if reviews are healthy and sell-through from Book 1 is strong, a raise to $3.99 is defensible. Do not raise above $3.99 for a novella at this stage.
- If Book 3 is approaching release, a temporary $0.99 Countdown on Book 2 in the launch window of Book 3 is the standard series-acceleration move.
