# H01 — Book 2 Ch 8: What the Garden Knew (lines 953–1084)
**Running impression:** 2/5 · **DNF risk:** none/low

## Gut reaction (in Nigel's voice)
The aftermath. Cozy mysteries love their aftermaths, and this one is mostly restrained. But the author cannot seem to resist over-explaining the emotional subtext. The Doris scene (959–1000) is the strongest part here — turning the photo back (993–996) is quietly devastating. The rest tends to spiral into navel-gazing.

## What worked (cite chapter + line numbers — begrudgingly, if at all)
- Doris aftermath: turning photo to wall then back (989–996) — small action, huge weight. No melodrama. Effective.
- Garden dissolution: beds scattered (1001–1011) — breaking the code as mercy. Clever metaphorical closure.
- Reconciliation restraint: Mae conversation (1043–1068) avoids big hug scene; boundary remains (asking permission knock, not staying long). I like that.
- Self-awareness: Callie recognizing she has Marguerite's silence in her (1007–1009) — sharp observation, cuts through sentimentality briefly.
- Passing craft: Eli's "what do you actually see?" (1027–1041) gives organic thread to next book without cliffhanger.

## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
- Over-wrought introspection: "the work of sitting with what she had done and not being told it was right" (957) — too self-conscious.
- Tea obsession continues: bergamot almost-right, counting one-two-three (1051, 1074) — we've heard this tic repeatedly. Enough already.
- Symbolic overreach: "the garden club voted to dissolve the Dabney beds..." and "the not-spelling was its own mercy" (1076) — the narrator over-explains the metaphor.
- Ending too tidy: tea "almost right" and "enough. For now, that was enough" (1078–1079) wraps everything up too neatly.
