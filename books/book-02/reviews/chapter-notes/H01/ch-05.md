# H01 — Book 2 Ch 5: The Language of Flowers (lines 558–707)
**Running impression:** 3/5 · **DNF risk:** none/low

## Gut reaction (in Nigel's voice)
Now we're moving. The full cipher assembly (564–577) is satisfying — the book-as-map finally clicks. The rose's final line "*You know what I could not say. Say it.*" (588–589) is good, restrained. And the Mae scene (598–637) is the emotional heart of this book, and it mostly earns it. Cross's response (680–697) also rings true — he won't act on a book of flowers, but he reads them carefully. I can respect that.

## What worked (cite chapter + line numbers — begrudgingly, if at all)
- Cipher payoff: water lily gives "old dock. The deep" (572–577) — specific location, not vague. Connects to cove/lake lore effectively.
- Honesty about evidence: Callie acknowledges "not enough for a warrant" (581–583) — self-awareness, avoids easy victory.
- Mae's late reckoning: (598–637) handles guilt and timing well; Mae admits knowing and fearing (622–631). Avoids melodrama, feels painfully real.
- Procedural truth: Cross's line "I can't take that to a judge. I can't get a search warrant on *pen pressure*." (680–681) is the most realistic police response in the book so far. I applaud that.
- Exit temptation: Callie considers walking away (640–643) — legitimate moral weight, not just cost to her.

## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
- "Like a body on a slab" (562) — overwrought simile for a book. Unnecessary.
- Excessive internal shaking/tears narration (592–593) — show the decision; the narrator tells us she was shaking.
- Repetitive cost motif: "the cost would..." echoes; also "the saying was in the sheriff's hands..." (700–701).
- Harlan Beech looking up (698) feels a bit too symbolic — small thaw, but perhaps unearned.
- Slightly tidy resolution of Mae tension (632–635) — two women resolve a lot in a few lines; might have carried more weight with silence.
