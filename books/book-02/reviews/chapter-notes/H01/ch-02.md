# H01 — Book 2 Ch 2: Floriography (lines 146–262)
**Running impression:** 3/5 · **DNF risk:** none/low

## Gut reaction (in Nigel's voice)
Ah. She stopped narrating the shop for a minute and showed me a garden. The book and garden mirroring each other is exactly what this story needed. I might even let it off the hook for some of the last chapter's moaning. But the scene with Mae still has too much breath held between words.

## What worked (cite chapter + line numbers — begrudgingly, if at all)
- Garden as text: planting beds to meaning (151–154) is clever — floriography becomes a built environment. Very few writers actually make the setting a cipher. (152–153) draws clear links to book.
- Minimal exposition via character: Doris Coalter gives Lauren's name only after Callie earns it (195–197, 226–230). That restraint is admirable for a cozy.
- Power in silence: "The silence that came after was not the testing silence..." (208–209) captures three decades of history in one sentence. Less verbiage elsewhere.
- Stakes become personal: "it was not a puzzle anymore. It was a girl." (257–259) — concise, effective, no over-egging.
- Showing the decoded bed: pulled-up stakes for hollyhock/yellow rose/columbine (176–177) is chilling; Marguerite tried to un-plant accusations.

## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
- Over-telling emotion: Mae's described smell "thinner now. Strained" (188–189) — perhaps; but narrator keeps hovering over subtext rather than letting awkward dialogue carry it.
- Gate-as-metaphor tic: "the ordinariness of a gate being held" (248–249) — the book likes to find profoundness in mundane objects a little too often.
- Self-awareness of cost: Callie keeps foretelling "the cracking would cost her, and the cost was the point" (258–259). We've already established cost; this is the narrator nudging reader.
- Flora lecture risk: (152–153, 178–179) explains meanings explicitly — necessary for reader, but borders on info-dump; garden visit mitigates somewhat.

## Cracks & tics (chapter:line)

- Chapter:258 — "the cost was the point" (narratorial nudge)
