# R01 — Book 2 Ch 8: What the Garden Knew (lines 953–1084)

**Running impression:** 5/5 · **DNF risk:** none

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

A quiet, earned ending that left me deeply satisfied. Callie goes to Doris and tells her everything — every date, every flower meaning, every decoded passage — and Doris listens with her hands flat on the bare table, dry and steady, the grief held so deep it's become structural (981). She turns the photo to the wall and then back again (989–995), and the war in her shoulders is the truest thing in the chapter. Then the garden, being dissolved, the cuttings going out into the town — "the flowers were going out into the town, scattered, the code broken into pieces that would no longer spell anything" (1001). And Callie sees herself in Marguerite: "the careful thing and the cowardly thing could look so much alike you could choose one and call it the other" (1009). She makes a small quiet turning: *not like this.* That's the whole book's moral, spoken in four words.

Eli comes on Friday and asks to learn, and she says yes (1038). Mae comes at five with nothing but herself, and they drink tea in silence (1051). And Callie washes the cups and makes one cup of tea and drinks it standing, and the bergamot is "almost right. Not quite" (1074) — but the reclamation held, and the holding "was not belonging — belonging was not hers to claim yet — but it was no longer just survival. It was a life. A small one. A useful one" (1074). That's the hope at the end, plain and unshowy, and it's the best kind.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- Doris listening (975–981): the flat voice, the hands flat on the table, the not-crying. A mother hearing the whole story of her daughter's last night.
- The photo turned to the wall and back (989–995): "the daily ritual of making the absent girl present... the grief could move now. It had been frozen for thirty-two years — not mourning, not living, held in the ice of not-knowing — and now the ice was breaking" (995). That's beautiful and true.
- The garden being dissolved (1001–1005): the stakes still standing — *hollyhock, yellow rose, columbine. Ambition. Infidelity. Folly.* — and Callie reading it as "a woman's life spelled in flowers, a record of what she had planted and what she had pulled up" (1005).
- Callie seeing herself in Marguerite (1007–1009): "She had Marguerite's silence in her. And standing in the garden being dissolved... she made a choice — not a grand one, just a small quiet turning: *not like this*" (1009).
- The tea at the end (1074): "the reclamation held. It had been tested and it had held, and the holding was not belonging... but it was no longer just survival. It was a life. A small one. A useful one." That's the whole series in a paragraph.

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- Nothing. If I wanted more of anything, it would be a little more time with Doris after the photo — but her silence is the point, and it's the right choice.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes, and at her most human. She goes to Doris knowing she's broken a promise, and she tells the whole truth anyway (977–979). She doesn't ask for forgiveness; she just sits with what she's done. That's likeable.
- Cozy contract: honoured, and this is the chapter that seals it. Hope at the end: the cuttings taking root, Eli learning to read, Mae sitting in the same room, the tea that's almost right. No grue, no swearing. The garden club's oiling of the gate (1011) is the gentlest image of community I've read in a year.
- Rudeness to elders: none. She's respectful to Doris, to Mae, to Marguerite's memory.

## Carried into next chapter

There is no next chapter — this is the end of Book 2. I carry forward the sense that Callie is still "useful — not welcome," but that she has chosen to be useful, and that the town's flowers are growing in a hundred yards where the rosemary means remembrance. I'd read Book 3 tomorrow.
