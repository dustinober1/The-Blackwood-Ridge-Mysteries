# R01 — Book 2 Ch 1: The Estate Lot (lines 50–145)

**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** none

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

A lovely, quiet opening — exactly the sort of chapter I like to settle into with a cup of tea. I felt for Callie right away, walking the long way round the square so the morning would still have her. That line about pretending she needed air when what she needed was proof the morning would still have her — that's real. And it's a hard thing to write, showing a woman who has been broken and is choosing, very quietly, to stand back up. The town's coldness is handled with a gentle touch, and I appreciated that: no melodrama, just the weight of not-quite-eyes and the flinch you pull back from. By the time Eli arrives with the crate and the word *provenance*, I was hooked.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- The peonies bowing in the square (54) — such a small, telling detail, and it sets the whole town's mood without a word about it.
- Callie reclaiming her tea, "the reclamation had held, more or less, the way a set bone holds" (56) — a lovely, plain metaphor a reader can feel in her bones.
- Harlan sweeping and not looking up (58), Donna's hand going slow on the key (60) — the town's ostracism shown through ordinary gestures, never explained, just *felt*. This is the small-town texture I love.
- Eli practicing being "a person who did not apologize for taking up space" (74), and his careful new handwriting on the note he leaves by the counter (110). That's a sweet, hopeful line.
- Marguerite's steady hand under the glass (116) — the description of the hairline stroke, the pool at the foot of each *L*, is exquisite. You can feel the woman through the ink.

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- Nothing really. The chapter is a slow burn, and I wondered briefly if it moved too deliberately (110, the pricing of paperbacks taking twenty minutes), but that's a feature, not a bug — it's the shop's heartbeat, and it grounds us. I'd rather have too much texture than too little.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes. She's a pariah and she knows it, but she's not bitter about it — she's tired, and careful, and she keeps choosing to get up. That's likeable.
- Cozy contract: honoured. No grue, no swearing. The worst thing is a flinch.
- Rudeness to elders: none. Callie is respectful to everyone, even the town that won't look at her.
- Animal cruelty: none. A dog barks in the distance (134) — harmlessly.

## Evidence & quotes (chapter:line)

- "she had got into the habit of pretending she needed the morning air when what she needed was to prove the morning would still have her" (54)
- "the reclamation had held, more or less, the way a set bone holds" (56)
- "the whole architecture of the last months had been built on not admitting that" (60)
- "a hand that careful isn't decorating. It wants to be read" (102)
- "the hand of a woman who had sat down with this book and gone through it page by page and written as if she had all the time in the world, and as if she expected, eventually, to be found" (116)

## Carried into next chapter

The book is Marguerite's, the hand is hers, and the first plate is rosemary — *remembrance*. The shape of the cipher is grief and accusation, and Callie has picked up the glass. The cost is already known to her. I carried forward the sense that this is going to hurt, and that it will be worth it.
