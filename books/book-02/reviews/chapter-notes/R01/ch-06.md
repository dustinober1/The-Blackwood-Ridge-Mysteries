# R01 — Book 2 Ch 6: The Closing Ranks (lines 708–817)

**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** low

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

The town turns, and it hurts to watch — but it's the right kind of hurt, the kind that makes the ending matter. Linda Parrish's bright loud voice (712), the archive key formalised on council letterhead (714), the garden club splitting over Marguerite's own beds (718) — it's all so precise, so earned. And through it all, Callie sits in the back room with the empty table, and she thinks about stopping, and she doesn't. The scene with Eli is the heart of it for me: he asks how she knows a hand means something (764), and she tells him the truth — "You can feel it the way you can feel someone trying to tell you something they can't say out loud" (768). And he asks to be taught (776). That's the small ordinary seed that makes the whole thing worth it. Then Cross calls, and we're going to the cove.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- The town's deciding, "and the deciding was not kind" (710) — a plain statement that carries the whole chapter's weight.
- Harlan slowing at her door and not coming in (728): "the not-coming-in was worse than the not-looking, because it meant he had decided." That's a masterstroke of small-town texture.
- The back room as "both sanctuary and tomb" (730), the room where her mother died — the gravity of it is something I felt in my own chest.
- Callie's moment of considering stopping (734–736): "It would be the kind thing. It would be the careful thing. It would be the thing Marguerite had done for three decades." And then she doesn't. That's the moral choice of the book, and it's made quietly, in a room with a cold cup of tea.
- Eli's question (764–768) and his asking to be taught (776): "It was a seed — small and ordinary, planted in the back room of a bookshop on a Thursday afternoon when one person was lonely and another was standing straight" (780). That's the line that made me smile through the ache.
- Cross's call (792): "We're going up to the cove tomorrow morning. Six a.m." — and "Mae came by" (800). The unspoken sentence is the best sentence.

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- Nothing. If I'm being very picky, the garden club split (718) is described in a single paragraph and I'd have liked a little more of it — but it's a background tremor, and it works as such.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes, and this is the chapter where she's most alone, and most herself. She considers stopping and doesn't, and she's honest about why: "Doris would learn it from the town, not from her, if she stopped now. That was the part she could not carry" (642 — that's Ch 5, but the thought carries here at 734). She's not a heroine; she's a woman who can't carry a wrong silence. That's likeable.
- Cozy contract: honoured. The ostracism is social, not violent. No grue, no swearing. The worst thing is a closed kitchen and a shop with no customers.
- Rudeness to elders: none. She's respectful to Mae, to Doris's memory, to the town's feelings even when the town has none for her.

## Carried into next chapter

Six a.m., the cove, muddy boots. The ring, the deep, the silt. I carry forward Eli's question — *could you teach me* — and the sense that whatever the water gives back, it won't be easy, and it won't be the end.
