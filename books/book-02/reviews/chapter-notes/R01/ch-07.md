# R01 — Book 2 Ch 7: Blackwater Cove (lines 818–952)

**Running impression:** 5/5 · **DNF risk:** none

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

This is the chapter I've been waiting for, and it delivered everything I hoped and then some. The cove is smaller than she imagined, the water "a heavier dark, silted, still, the dark of water that does not move and has not moved in a long time" (828) — and the divers go in without a sound, the surface closing over them like a held breath. When the ring comes up (846), a girl's 1994 graduation ring, I felt it in my knees. And then the bones. And then Wells in the interview room, and the confession — "She was leaving... I put my hand on her. To stop her. To make her stay and listen. And she pulled away, and I pushed, and she went in" (915). It's spoken to the ring, to the table, to a room where the person he's addressing is not there, and it's the truest thing in the book. I cried, and I'm not ashamed of it. The last line — "she didn't run. She never ran" (943) — is the whole novel in four words.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- The cove described (828): "smaller than she had imagined... the old dock was gone. The pilings remained — four stumps of treated lumber rising from the water like rotted teeth." Haunting, and exactly right.
- The divers going in "without sound" (836): "The water took them without sound. That was the wrong thing — the wrongness of it, the silence." The cove itself is a character, and it's withholding.
- The ring (846–848): "a girl's ring, a 1994 ring, the kind they gave at Blackwood Ridge High, small enough for a seventeen-year-old's hand." And the thought of Marguerite, who had been right — "the being right was the saddest thing about it, because being right had not saved Lauren" (848).
- Wells's confession (911–919): "She was leaving. We were on the dock... I put my hand on her. To stop her. To make her stay and listen. And she pulled away, and I pushed, and she went in, and she struck something — the piling, the edge, I don't know — and she didn't come up." Plain, broken, irrefutable. And "I stood there and the water closed and I didn't — I didn't go in" (919). My heart.
- The unasked question (927): "Are you so different, Callie Thorne, from the man who buried it? She who dug up truth and he who hid it, and the digging and the hiding were both compulsions." That's the moral complexity this book has been building toward, and it lands softly, without being answered. Perfect.
- The final line (943): "She never ran."

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- Nothing. If I must name something, the arrest scene (935) is procedural and quiet, and I'd have felt a small loss that there was no confrontation — but that's Cross's character, not a flaw, and it's truer for being understated.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes, and this is where she's most human. She stands near the door and doesn't sit (909), and she's "afraid of it" (909) — afraid of what Wells will say. She doesn't offer absolution, and she doesn't offer judgment. She says, "You built a good life, Wells. You built it over her. Both of those things are real" (929). That's the kindest, hardest thing anyone could say, and it's exactly what Dottie would say.
- Cozy contract: honoured. The recovery of remains is off-page; the confession is spoken, not depicted. No grue, no swearing, no graphic violence. The water "closed over the place where Lauren Coalter had been" (866) — that's the worst of it, and it's a sentence, not a scene.
- Rudeness to elders: none.
