# R01 — Book 2 Ch 3: The Open Palm (lines 264–401)

**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** low

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

I liked this chapter a great deal, though it's the one that made me uneasy — in the best way. Callie is working the cipher and she's *enjoying* it, which is honest and a little alarming, because she knows the cost. Then she goes to the school and meets Wells Ambler, and he's... charming. Genuinely. Warm tea, a kind story about Marguerite failing him on a *Gatsby* paper (316), and I found myself liking him, which is exactly the trap the book sets, and it sets it beautifully. When she says "Lauren Coalter" and the office goes quiet (320), and his hand closes on the cup for the first time (322) — that's a tell I'd trust. Then the sheriff arrives, and his jaw sets at the name (374), and I felt the whole town's weight shift. The open palm, filed under *deliberate* (298), is the image I can't shake.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- "The flowers were the key... Read in sequence, the flowers spelled something. Not a sentence, exactly. A ledger" (270–272) — a lovely way to describe it.
- The shorthand as "ten thousand graded papers worth of private telegraph" (276), and the connection to Eleanor's own code — that's a thread I love seeing come back.
- Wells's handshake: "His hand was open. Palm shown, fingers relaxed, the gesture of a man who had nothing to hide" (298). And later, the closing around the cup (322). A single gesture doing two jobs.
- The Marguerite story (314–316) — "she'd handed it back with a single word in the margin: *again*" — is perfect character work. It tells us who Marguerite was, who Wells is, and why Callie laughs when she doesn't want to.
- The sheriff's jaw as the tell (374, 668): "not the neck, the jaw." A small, repeatable signal that pays off later.

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- The sheriff's scene is a little on-the-nose — "I am not going to pull him into a thing like this on the strength of a dead woman's flowers" (376). It's good, but it's spoken out loud in a way that a real sheriff might not. Minor.
- I found myself wishing Callie had asked Wells one harder question about the Harlow barn, but that's the point — she didn't, and the reader feels the missed beat with her.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes. She's wary and she stays wary (316: "She liked him. That was the thing to watch — liking was what made you stop looking"). She's honest about her own pull toward him, and that honesty is likeable.
- Cozy contract: honoured. No grue, no swearing. The worst is a closed hand and a jaw that sets.
- Rudeness to elders: none. She's polite to Wells and to the sheriff, even when she's being pushed.

## Carried into next chapter

The open palm, the word *dock*, the Harlow-barn lie, and the sheriff's refusal. Callie has begun to look sideways at a man the whole town loves, and she knows what that costs. I carry forward the sense that the archive is about to close, and that the town is already deciding what it thinks of her.
