# R01 — Book 2 Ch 4: The Wet Hemline (lines 402–557)

**Running impression:** 5/5 · **DNF risk:** low

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)

This is the chapter where the cipher breaks open, and it's the best kind of revelation — slow, earned, and utterly plain. The meadowsweet plate, the meaning *the stifling of a feeling*, and then the shorthand gives: "He came up the lake path after dark... He was wet to the knees" (414–418). I read that passage twice, because it's so quiet and so devastating. Marguerite saw a boy come home wet and walked in the middle of the road so the dust wouldn't show. That's all she wrote. And that's everything. The restraint of it — the careful, deliberate refusal to go one syllable past the seeing (424) — is what makes this book a cozy and not a thriller. Then Harriet Vailes comes with the archive key news, and Wells is at Doris's door with tomatoes, and Doris can't unpack the bag. My heart ached for all three of them.

## What worked (chapter + line numbers)

- The meadowsweet plate and its double meaning: "*uselessness*, and also *the stifling of a feeling*" (406) — Marguerite underlining the second. A woman naming her own silence.
- The decoded passage (414–418): "He came to the Carmody place and he told them he had been at the Harlow barn... He had been at the lake and he was wet to the knees, and he had come back alone, and Lauren had not come home." Plain, careful, irrefutable.
- "Not *he killed her.* Not *he is guilty.* Marguerite had not written those words. She had written what she had seen" (424). That is the whole moral centre of the book, right there.
- Callie testing the cipher against the counter-narrative (432): "A woman in pain, annotating a book she loved, possibly reading patterns into old memories that were not there." She keeps herself honest, and I love her for it.
- The grocery bag as "the whole knot of it — penance and cover and cruelty and care" (538). A single object carrying a whole tangle. Superb.

## What didn't (chapter + line numbers)

- Nothing, really. If I must pick, the scene where Callie goes to Doris's kitchen knowing it's the wrong thing (466) is agonising in a way that's hard to bear — but that's the point, and it's meant to be.

## Persona checklist progress (Callie likeable? cozy contract? gore/swearing/rudeness checks)

- Callie likeable: yes. She's honest with Doris — "I'm telling you a woman saw him come home wet from a lake the night Lauren didn't come home. That's what she wrote. That's all she wrote" (532). No grandstanding, no promises she can't keep.
- Cozy contract: honoured. The wet hemline is a margin note, not a body. No grue, no swearing. The tomatoes are real and the grief is real, and neither is graphic.
- Rudeness to elders: none. She's respectful to Doris even when Doris is closing the door.

## Carried into next chapter

The archive is closed, Doris's kitchen is shut, and the only ally Callie has is a dead woman who chose, thirty years too late, to write down what she saw. The cove is named: *the old dock. The deep.* I carry forward the weight of the next plate — the water lily, *the surface and what is under it* — and the sense that the map is almost complete.
