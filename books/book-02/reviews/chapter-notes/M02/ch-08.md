# M02 — Book 2 Ch 8: What the Garden Knew (lines 953–1084)
**Running impression:** 3/5 · **DNF risk:** none · **Killer guess:** Wells Ambler — already confessed, no guessing left

## Gut reaction (in Jules's voice)
The resolution chapter, and it's the quietest one in the book — which is fine, but for a guess-the-killer reader the guessing is long over and this is all aftermath. What I get: Doris (959–997), the garden being dissolved (1001–1011), Eli's apprenticeship continuing (1014–1040), Mae coming to Callie with tea (1043–1067). It's a denouement, and a good one — but it's also the book refusing to give me a final puzzle. The best line is 1009: "the careful thing and the cowardly thing could look so much alike you could choose one and call it the other." That's the book's self-awareness talking, and it's the reason I can't dismiss this as formula.

What doesn't work: Doris's ending is a shrug. She turns the photo to the wall, then back (989–995), and tells Callie to go (997). That's not a resolution for a mother who just learned her daughter was murdered — it's a gesture. The book is protecting its cozy contract by not letting Doris break down. And the garden club dissolving the beds (1001, 1053) is a nice image but it's also the book tidying up its own metaphor. The flowers "would not spell anything anymore" (1076) — that's the author telling me the mystery is over, which is a bit on the nose.

## Suspect pool (chapter:line — who entered, who exited, why)
- **Wells Ambler** — confessed in Chapter 7. Not in this chapter. Case closed.
- **Doris Coalter** (959–997) — the grief finally moves. Not a suspect, but she's the emotional endpoint. Her refusal to thank Callie (997) is the book's honest note: justice doesn't heal the mother.
- **Eli Townsend** (1014–1040) — continues his apprenticeship. "You start by looking. You come in on Saturdays" (1038). Series thread, not mystery.
- **Mae** (1043–1067) — comes to Callie without a casserole. "Earlier than Marguerite" (1063) — the book is comparing Mae's late honesty to Marguerite's long silence. That's the thematic through-line.

## Guess-the-killer log (when I figured it / what gave it away — chapter:line)
Nothing to log. I guessed Wells Ambler at Chapter 3:290 and the book confirmed him at Chapter 7:915. The guessing phase is over at line 290 of a 1084-line book. That's the core problem for this persona: I identified the killer at chapter 3, which is exactly the metric I'm supposed to test, and the answer is "yes, too early."

## Predictability check (chapter:line)
The ending is predictable in shape — tea, glass, shop, "almost right" (1074–1078) — but that's the series' signature closing beat and it's earned here. What's predictable and unsatisfying: the book never gives me a second suspect to weigh against Wells. The suspect pool was one man. That's a fairness failure. A guess-the-killer reader wants at least two plausible candidates so the guessing is a choice. Here the choice was made for me by the blurb's "most respected man" and the single W initial in the cipher.
