# M02 — Book 2 Ch 4: The Wet Hemline (lines 402–557)
**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** low · **Killer guess:** Wells Ambler — now near-certain, but the book is making it agonizing

## Gut reaction (in Jules's voice)
The shorthand breaks. Line 414: *He came up the lake path after dark. I was at the kitchen window. The light was on. He did not see me. He was wet to the knees.* This is the book's payload. Marguerite saw a boy come home soaked from the lake the night Lauren vanished, and he told the Carmodys he'd been at the Harlow barn all evening — and he hadn't. That's a lie, and the lie is about where he was, and the lake is where Lauren disappeared. I locked Wells in Chapter 3 and this is the confirmation: the boy was wet, the boy lied, the boy is Wells Ambler (the only W in town, the only man Marguerite named beside yarrow=war). I'm at near-certainty now.

But — and this is the first time the book has made me hesitate — the book then spends 150 lines showing me why I might be wrong. Wells brings Doris groceries every Thursday for thirty years (516). He sat with her when her husband died. He walked Lauren to the bus stop. He put the floor in her kitchen (530). Doris, the victim's mother, *knows* this and cannot give up the tomatoes (538). That's a genuinely good scene — the bag of tomatoes as penance and cover and cruelty and care. If Wells is guilty, he's the most patient, long-game psychopath in cozy fiction, and if he's innocent, Marguerite saw something innocent (swimming, a fall, a rescue that went wrong) and misread it. I can't tell. The book is earning its keep.

## Predictability check (chapter:line)
The "wet hemline" is the book's Chekhov's gun and it fires exactly where I expected. What's *less* predictable — and this is the chapter's real achievement — is that the book doesn't let me feel smart for guessing. Doris's defense (516–538) is so human and so specific that it destabilizes my certainty. That's the opposite of predictability; that's the book making the guess *cost* something. Also predictable: the archive key gets recalled (442) — the obvious obstacle. But it's delivered through Harriet, a scared messenger, rather than a mook, which softens it.
