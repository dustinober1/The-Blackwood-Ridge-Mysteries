# M02 — Book 2 Ch 7: Blackwater Cove (lines 818–952)
**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** none · **Killer guess:** Wells Ambler — CONFIRMED at 911–919, confession delivered

## Gut reaction (in Jules's voice)
The body is found. The ring at 846, the remains at 854. And then the confession — which is the part I was waiting for and the part that actually surprised me. Wells didn't murder her. He pushed her, she struck the piling, she drowned, and he stood there and didn't go in (915–919). That's an accident that became a cover-up, not a premeditated killing, and that distinction matters. It also explains the "wet hemline" perfectly: he walked home soaked and lied about the Harlow barn because he was panicking, not because he'd planned a murder. Marguerite saw the wet legs and correctly read the panic but couldn't have known it was an accident. That's good — the cipher was *right* and *incomplete* at the same time, which is exactly what a careful witness would record.

What I don't like: Wells asks Callie to tell him his thirty-two years of good works "counted" (923). That's the book asking the reader to forgive him, and it's manipulative. He built a good life *over* a girl he let drown. The fact that he brought Doris groceries doesn't erase the fact that he stood on a dock and watched a teenager die. Callie's answer — "You built a good life, Wells. You built it over her. Both of those things are real" (929) — is honest and it's the best line in the book, but it's also the book dodging the moral question it set up. I'm glad she didn't absolve him. I'm less glad he asked.

That's better than a simple "gotcha." But the suspect pool was absurdly small: one named male suspect across 950 lines. That's a fairness failure by the persona's own checklist.
