I'm now fairly confident the killer is Wells Ambler. Confidence: 70%. The question is whether the book will let me *prove* it before the end, or whether it'll pull a rug. I'm watching for red herrings.

## Predictability check (chapter:line)
The suspect pool is now *one person*. That's a problem for a guess-the-killer reader — I've identified the killer in chapter 3 of an 8-chapter book, and there's no one else on the list. The book is not hiding the answer; it's hiding the *evidence*. That's a legitimate structure (the puzzle is whether Callie can prove it, not who did it), but it's not what I read these books for. I dock points here. The Wells scenes are written *well* — the warmth is real, the kindness is real, the tomatoes are real — and that's the point, and the book knows it, but it also means the "guess" part of my brief is over by line 290. Predictability: high. I'm not surprised he's the killer; I'm waiting to see if anyone else even gets accused.
