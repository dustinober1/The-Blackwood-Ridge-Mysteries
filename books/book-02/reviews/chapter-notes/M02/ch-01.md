# M02 — Book 2 Ch 1: The Estate Lot (lines 50–145)
**Running impression:** 3/5 · **DNF risk:** none · **Killer guess:** none yet — no body, no suspect pool
## Gut reaction (in Jules's voice)
Cold open, and it works. I've never read Book 1, so I'm coming in with zero context, and the first chapter doesn't dump backstory at me — it just puts Callie in the square, estranged from the town, and lets the reader feel the chill without being told what it's about. That's good craft. The botanical book is introduced like a mystery object rather than a plot device, which I respect. But I'm already clocking that this is going to be a *cipher* book, and cipher books are my least favorite kind — too much decoding, too little blood. I'm skeptical but not bored. The prose is better than I expected from a small-press series: the peonies "bowing under their own heads," the "pressed-glass quality" of the light. That's real texture. I like it. I also note that the victim (Marguerite) is already dead in chapter one, which is a relief — no "who killed her?" when the answer is "cancer." The real question is what she *knew*, and that's a different game. I'm in.

## Predictability check (chapter:line)
The opening is doing a lot of work it doesn't need to: the estrangement from the town, the reclaimed shop, the mother's death, the glass that was always hers. All of it is setup for a series that's already running. As a cold-entry reader, I don't need any of the Book 1 recap, but I also don't feel lost — it's handled as texture, not exposition. The one predictable move: Callie picks up the glass at 114 and the "narrowing" begins at 116. That's the series formula kicking in, and I clocked it immediately. Not a flaw, just a flag.
