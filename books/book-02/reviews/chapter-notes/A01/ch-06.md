## Gut reaction (in Wren's voice)
The town's counter-attack is well built — the mother performing reasonableness, the letter on council letterhead, the garden club splitting in the basement with the side-door lock never changed. And two lines are the best in the book: "the word *loved* was a thing neither of them could put in a room with the word *killed* and survive" and "Mae had walked into the office of a man whose diapers she had changed, and had said a word, and the word had been enough." But the author's rhetorical pets are now verifiable: "quiet was not peace" four times (5:700, 6:730, 6:740, plus "empty was not peace"), "animal fact" twice (5:784, 6:740, 6:784), and the sentence "She could not make the two things fit, and she did not try" appears nearly verbatim at 4:546 and 6:810. That's not a motif, that's a template. Reflective-beat ending #6. Six for six.
