**Running impression:** 4/5 · **DNF risk:** low · **AI-smell:** mild (creeping)

## Gut reaction (in Wren's voice)
Two good scenes bolted to one mechanical paragraph. The Mae confession is the most human writing in the book — "I did the thing, Callie. The thing I always do" is exactly how a person confesses a decade of cowardice — and the sheriff scene is fair, procedural, and dry ("I can't get a search warrant on *pen pressure*"). But 5:566 runs a machine cadence: "The rosemary gave remembrance and the date. The violet gave faithfulness and the name. The yarrow gave war and the second name." Four beats, identical grammar, no variation. And the author's favorite constructions are now verifiable tics: "its own sentence" at its third use (2:248, 3:354, 5:600), "Not a question. A naming." at its fourth (1:90, 2:182, 3:312, 5:624), the brass-warm ritual at its third (1:116, 4:410, 5:570). Every chapter so far ends on a quiet reflective beat. Five for five.
