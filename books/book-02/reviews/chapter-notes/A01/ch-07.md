# A01 — Book 2 Ch 7: Blackwater Cove (lines 818–952)
**Running impression:** 3.5/5 · **DNF risk:** none · **AI-smell:** mild-moderate

## Gut reaction (in Wren's voice)
The book earns its finale: the soundless dive, the ring in the gloved fist, "being right was the saddest thing about it," and Wells's confession — "I walked home and the legs of my trousers were wet and I walked in the middle of the road so the dust wouldn't show" — which mirrors Marguerite's witness statement back at itself, and the groceries rhyme ("the man across the table brought groceries to the girl's mother every Thursday") is the cruelest sentence in the book. The restraint on the remains is right. But this chapter wears its machinery openly: "his face was the face of a man who" four times (7:832, 7:858, 7:905, 7:935), em-dash density at its highest (~13), the stone simile repeated from 4:502, "coffee and floor cleaner" repeated from 5:650, "the faint must of paper" repeated from 1:64, and the "its own" aphorism family at its fifth member. The emotion after the confession is explained twice over (7:921, 7:927). Reflective-beat ending #7. Seven for seven.
