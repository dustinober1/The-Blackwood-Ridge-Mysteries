# L01 — Book 2 Ch 2: Floriography (lines 146–263)
**Running impression:** 3.5/5 · **DNF risk:** none
## Gut reaction (in Imogen's voice)
This is the chapter that earns the premise. The garden as a text is a formal idea with teeth, and Blythe executes it without irony. Lines 152–153 are the set piece: a garden planted in the old language, peony for shame ringed with rosemary for remembrance — a confession no one but another reader would catch. That is not a gimmick; it is a sustained metaphor the prose is willing to be literal about. The reveal of Doris Coalter (196) is handled with exemplary restraint — "She did not say *Lauren was my daughter*. She did not have to. The garden had said it" — and the flatness of Doris's voice (226, 230) is the best piece of vocal mimicry in the book so far. I am now reading with the critic's instrument tuned, not the genre reader's.
## Prose quality (cite chapter + line numbers)
Line 176 is the best sentence in the chapter: "Ambition. Betrayal. Folly. A bed of the qualities Marguerite had wanted to name and had pulled up by the root, leaving only the stakes, only the accusation." The three-word list, then the expansion — that is a controlled risk and it lands. Line 188's Mae smell ("lavender, the sachet-and-cinnamon smell that was Mae's armor") is the kind of sensory specificity that distinguishes this from generic small-town texture; the word *armor* does the work. Line 232 — "the hands. The posture of a woman pressing herself to the earth" — is criticism-grade observation, the kind of detail a genre writer usually does not risk. Weak spots: line 156's "the kind of thin that came from a life of not quite eating enough" is a character note that edges toward shorthand, and line 224's "a mockingbird was running through every song it had ever stolen" is a purple flourish that the rest of the chapter does not need.
## Thematic ambition (documents/truth/authorship — chapter:line)
The chapter's argument is that a garden can be a document, and that a document can be an accusation aimed at a living reader. Line 178: "The book and the ground were one text, and Marguerite had written them both for the same reader." That is the authorship theme made spatial, and it is genuinely ambitious for the form. Line 208 — "the silence was older. It had been standing between these two women for decades, and the garden said it now so they did not have to" — is the document speaking in place of the people who cannot. That is the thematic spine, and it is not decorative. Line 258: "it was not a puzzle anymore. It was a girl." The cipher has been reclassified from formal object to moral object. Good.
## Formal coherence (structure, chapter:line)
The chapter is a single, unbroken walk through the garden, and the structure is the reading — Callie moves, the garden yields meaning in stages, the human revelation (Doris = Lauren's mother) arrives exactly when the reader has already inferred it. That is fair play of a high order. The three-voice dialogue scene (200–246) is perfectly modulated: Doris rationing words, Mae translating, Callie listening. The only structural complaint is that the chapter's emotional climax (Doris's flat account, 226–231) is followed by a denouement (Mae at the gate, 248–258) that is slightly overlong — three short scenes where one would do. But the restraint of the ending, with Mae not saying *be careful*, is worth the extra length.
## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
Line 150: "She had chosen this. She was paying for it." is a beat that repeats the Chapter 1 ending and feels like a reprise rather than an advance. Line 180's moment when "the garden was just a garden" before the woman looks up is a false reset — the prose tells us the garden is ordinary and then immediately contradicts itself, which is a rhetorical move that undercuts its own claim. Line 256's "the rosemary meant remembrance, and the remembrance meant thirty-two years" is a summary that the chapter has already dramatized; it is the author explaining the symbol to us after we have already read it.
## Carried into next chapter (honest working memory)
The cipher is still uncracked, but its terms are now fixed: it is Lauren Coalter's story, it belongs to Doris, and it names a *W.* (the initial beside Lauren's name on the violet plate, 120/176). The garden has given up its vocabulary. Chapter 3 will presumably begin the actual decoding, and the question I am carrying is whether the flower language will be a real system or a decorative glossary.
