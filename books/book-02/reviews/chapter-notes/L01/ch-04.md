# L01 — Book 2 Ch 4: The Wet Hemline (lines 402–557)
**Running impression:** 4.5/5 · **DNF risk:** none
## Gut reaction (in Imogen's voice)
This is the best chapter in the book, and it is the chapter that justifies the entire premise. The meadowsweet plate (404–406) is the book's thematic key — *uselessness* and *the stifling of a feeling* — and Marguerite underlining the second is the author's own statement of method. The decoded passage (414–418) is the first real evidence, and it is delivered not as a bombshell but as a sentence Callie reads twice, which is exactly right. The scene at Doris's kitchen door (468–544) is the most morally complex passage in the series: Wells Ambler bringing tomatoes to the mother of the girl he may have killed, Doris unable to unpack the bag. Line 538 — "the bag was the whole knot of it — penance and cover and cruelty and care" — is criticism-grade analysis offered as narrative, and it is devastating. I am now fully engaged.
## Prose quality (cite chapter + line numbers)
Line 418 is the passage: "*He came up the lake path after dark... Lauren had not come home.*" The compressed shorthand rendered as full prose is the book's formal trick, and it works because the voice stays Marguerite's — observational, refusing adjudication. Line 428's "The kitchen window. The light on. The boy in the middle of the road, walking to keep the dust off his shoes because his trousers were soaked below the knees" is the cipher made visible, and the specificity of "walking to keep the dust off his shoes" is what makes it real. Line 502 is the best sentence about gesture in the book: "the open palm was no longer the gesture of a man who has nothing to hide. It was the gesture of a man who has practiced having nothing to hide for so long that the practice has become the hiding." That is a distinction a genre novel rarely attempts, and it lands. Weak: line 432's "possibly reading patterns into old memories that were not there" is Callie's self-doubt voiced too explicitly — the prose is explaining the doubt rather than staging it.
## Thematic ambition (documents/truth/authorship — chapter:line)
The chapter's thesis is that a document can be true and insufficient. Line 424: "Not *he killed her.* Not *he is guilty.* Marguerite had not written those words. She had written what she had seen... the careful, deliberate, rationed refusal to go one syllable past the seeing." That is the document-as-witness idea at its strongest — the text is a testimony, not a verdict, and the distinction is the whole moral engine of the book. Line 538's grocery bag as "penance and cover and cruelty and care" is the document's opposite: the living man's text, which says all four things at once and therefore says nothing. The archive lock (442–456) is the town authoring its own erasure, and Wells's "crusade" comment is the soft power of reputation as a document. This chapter is doing real thematic work.
## Formal coherence (structure, chapter:line)
The chapter is structured as a series of closures: the cipher opens (404–430), then the archive closes (436–462), then Doris's kitchen closes (526–542), and the chapter ends with Callie alone and turning the page (548–553). Each closure is a different kind of door shutting, and the repetition is deliberate rather than lazy. The Wells-at-the-door scene is intercut with Callie's walk up the path, which is a risky structural choice — the reader knows he is there before Callie does — but it is handled so that the tension is informational, not suspenseful, which suits the book's register. The only flaw: Harriet Vailes's visit (438–462) is a plot convenience dressed as a character beat; the archive rekeying is exactly what the plot needs and the coincidence is not disguised.
## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
Line 410's "the pen pressure of a woman who had held this particular sentence inside her for thirty-two years" is the author's own commentary inserted into the cipher's description — the prose is telling us the emotional weight rather than letting the shorthand's compression do that work. Line 476: "the man who had come back wet from a lake in 1994 was standing on the step of the mother of the girl who had not come home" is a summary that the scene has already dramatized; it is the critic's voice intruding into the character's. Line 546's "She thought of Doris... She thought of Wells... She could not make the two things fit together, and she did not try" is an explicit statement of the chapter's theme that the preceding 140 lines had already made concrete.
## Carried into next chapter (honest working memory)
The cipher has yielded its first full sentence: Marguerite saw Wells come home wet from the lake, alone, and Lauren did not come home. The archive is closed. Doris has demanded proof, not a hemline. The grocery bag is the moral knot: penance, cover, cruelty, care indistinguishable. Chapter 5 is titled "The Language of Flowers" — I expect it to be either the decoding of the remaining plates or the counter-narrative, and I am skeptical that the flower language will hold up as a system through another full chapter.
