## Gut reaction (3–5 sentences, in Nigel's voice)
I came in cold, and by the end I still find Callie Thorne a perfectly serviceable heroine, if a trifle fond of narrating her own feelings. The central conceit — a dead woman's cipher built out of the language of flowers, laid out in both a book and an actual garden — is clever enough that I begrudge it my approval. But for a curmudgeon who checks everything, the book spends far too much time telling me how much it costs, how heavy everything is, how not-at-peace everyone is, and not nearly enough time letting the facts do the heavy lifting. The procedural realism — that Cross won't act on "pen pressure" — saved it from sliding into pure cozy wish-fulfillment. Still, it's rare that a mystery gives me a perfectly solid clue and then circles it three times in introspection.

## What worked (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- Garden as cipher: the planting mirroring the botanical's meanings (Ch2:152–154, 176–179) is genuinely clever; making the setting a text is the book's best idea.
- Material evidence + decoding: the botanical connected to Marguerite (Ch1:83–96), the hand-pressure change signaling urgency (Ch1:128–131), and the full assembly to "old dock. The deep" (Ch5:572–577) form a satisfying puzzle.
- Witness details pay off: the meadowsweet passage giving Helen Carmody's account of wet knees/alibi (Ch4:412–419) is the concrete evidence I'd been waiting for.
- Procedural restraint: Cross refusing a warrant on pen pressure (Ch5:680–681) and delaying until specific location justified search (Ch7:792, Ch5:694) rings true.
- Quiet aftermath: Doris turning the photo to wall then back (Ch8:989–996), Mae's late reckoning (Ch5:622–637), Eli wanting to learn (Ch6:776–784) — small gestures, effective.

## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- Introspection overdose: repeated metaphors — "the architecture of the last months" (Ch1:61), "the not-thinking was its own kind of thinking" (Ch1:110), "the narrowing had taken her" (Ch3:410–411 echoing Ch1:115–116), "the quiet was not peace" (Ch6:730, 739), "sanctuary and tomb" (Ch6:731).
- Foretelling cost: Callie repeatedly tells us it will cost, she knows the cost, cost is the point (Ch1:136, Ch2:258–259, Ch3:394,396, Ch4:522–524, Ch5:592–593,700–701) — I don't need the narrator to keep nudging me.
- Motif overplayed: "open palm" / deliberate openness tracked excessively (Ch3:298–299,322–323,342–347,350; Ch7:880–882,917,931). Trust the reader to remember.
- Symbolic overreach: gate holding profound (Ch2:248–249), book like "body on a slab" (Ch5:562), Harlan Beech looking up too neatly (Ch5:698). 
- Cozy tics: Eli collar-touching/apology rehearsals (Ch1:75–76,81,98; Ch4:430; Ch6:746,750) — the author points at them rather than letting them land.

## Public review draft (the review Nigel would actually post, 80–250 words)
**2 stars. Checks too much, still worth reading.**

A clever idea carries much of *The Botanical Confession*: Marguerite Dabney left her testimony in the old language of flowers, encoded across the margins of a nineteenth-century botanical and echoed in the beds of her garden. For once in a cozy mystery, the sleuth actually does the legwork — tracing shorthand, cross-checking newspaper archives, confronting witnesses — and the book has the decency to admit that a dead woman's marginalia won't get a search warrant. Cross's procedural caution is the novel's most honest beat.

But the story is far too fond of telling us how heavy everything is. Nearly every chapter circles Callie's feelings about cost and ostracism; the motifs (open palms, not-peace, the tea ritual) repeat until they chafe. The confession scene is effective when restrained; the aftermath, less so when the narrator keeps nudging the reader toward the "right" emotional takeaway.

Still, when the garden and the book finally lock together into a map pointing to "the old dock, the deep," it's genuinely satisfying. Tighten the introspection by a third and this would be a sharp little cold-case mystery. As it stands, I grudgingly approve of the puzzle — I just wish the prose had trusted it more.

## One change that would flip my rating
Cut the cost-foretelling and the "not-peace/quiet was not peace" loops by ~30% (especially Ch1, Ch3, Ch6). Trust the facts and silences to carry weight. That alone would flip to 3★.
