Four stars, buying Book 1 tomorrow.

## What worked (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Hook by page 1**: Peonies, town's cold shoulder, botanical book waiting — all established lines 1:52–68. Cipher structure visible by 1:112–122.
- **Cipher as map**: Each plate = coordinate (5:564–566). Water lily decode "The old dock. The deep" (5:572) → remains at Blackwater Cove (7:852–854).
- **Wells Ambler's "open palm"**: Introduced 3:298, tested 3:322, closed 3:342, revealed as practiced hiding 4:502, collapses at confession 7:931.
- **Town's soft retaliation**: Archive closed via council (4:454–456), garden club splits (6:716–718), Harlan decides not to enter (6:728) — warmth as weapon, not threats.
- **Mae's arc**: Protects Callie from truth (5:614–623), breaks habit at Cross's office (6:800–801), "Earlier than Marguerite" (8:1063) — generational break earned.
- **Doris Coalter**: Photo turned to wall then back (8:989–995) — "moving water is heavier than still water" — grief unfreezes.
- **Procedural honesty**: Cross reads full file before dismissing (5:686), "I'll think about it" (5:694), "wear boots" (6:806), "This is a body now" (7:860).
- **Callie's skill crystallized**: "The person. Not the words. The hand behind them" (8:1032). Eli asks to learn (6:776, 8:1038).
- **Ending**: Glass = tool chosen/earned (8:1072), tea almost right = reclamation holds not belonging (8:1074–1078). Quiet, series-ready.

## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Minor telly metaphors**: "The tumbler turned" (3:389, 4:412, 5:562) — repeated lockpick metaphor for decode moments.
- **One meta-reference**: "The bible had said she would" (7:828) — odd fourth-wall nod (series bible?) in otherwise immersive prose.
- **Garden club dissolution off-page**: Reported not shown (8:1001–1002) — functional but loses a beat of collective consequence.
- **Cross's lawyer line**: "He hasn't asked for a lawyer yet" (7:901) — procedurally loose but serves scene tension.

## Public review draft (the review Priya would actually post, 80–250 words)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ Cold read, zero regrets. Callie Thorne reads marginalia like faces — a dead teacher's floriography-coded accusation pulls her into a 32-year cold case the town buried. The cipher unfolds flower by flower (rosemary=remembrance, aconite=hatred, water lily=what's under the surface) and every decode lands. Wells Ambler, the beloved principal, is the "W" in the margin; his "open palm" warmth is the mask, and when it cracks in the station interview (Ch 7), it's the most human confession I've read in years. Town doesn't threaten — it closes archives, splits garden clubs, stops meeting your eyes. Doris Coalter's grief unfreezes when the photo turns back to the room. Cross the sheriff is procedural, not heroic. Callie's "saying hands" skill gives the prose physical texture. Novella length means no bloat. Ending: tea almost right, shop hers, useful not welcome. Works perfectly standalone. Buying Book 1 tonight.

## One change that would flip my rating
Cut the three "tumbler turned" metaphors (3:389, 4:412, 5:562) and the "bible" line (7:828). Replace with the same physical reading beat — Callie feeling the ink pressure shift — and it's a clean 5★.
