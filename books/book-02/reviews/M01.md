## What didn't (specific, cite chapters/lines)
- **Archive blockade slightly convenient:** Wells uses council to block records Ch4:442-456 — effective obstacle but relies on town's exhaustion; minor procedural cheat (no legal basis for "security audit" on 14-year lock).
- **Mae's intervention with Cross off-page:** Ch6:800-802, Ch7:899-901 — we're told Mae "said a word" but not what; functional but opaque. Fair-play purist would want the scene.
- **Doris learns from Mae, not Callie:** Ch8:967, 979 — Callie's promise broken; narratively honest but the "receipt of the broken word" stings. Could have had Callie arrive minutes earlier.

## Public review draft (the review Constance would actually post, 80–250 words)
Vesper Blythe's *The Botanical Confession* is a rare thing: a fair-play puzzle where the clue ladder is genuinely visible to the reader. A dead teacher's floriography-annotated botanical volume — rosemary for remembrance, aconite for hatred, water lily for "the surface and what is under it" — yields a witness statement flower by flower. Callie Thorne decodes it in real time on the page; we see every shorthand line she sees. The suspect (Wells Ambler, the town's beloved principal) is introduced early as Lauren's boyfriend and the "W" in the cipher. His "open palm" tell is observable, not hidden. The meadowsweet plate gives the wet hemline; the water lily gives the cove. When the dive team finds the graduation ring and the bones, it's because the cipher *earned* the location. Wells's confession — intimate-partner violence, 32 years of mornings — is foreshadowed by the flower meanings themselves (war, hatred, shame). No cheats, no hidden info, no deus ex marginalia. The town's social ostracism of Callie after Book 1 grounds every procedural hurdle. A rereadable 5★ — the kind where you finish and immediately flip back to Chapter 1 to watch the machinery work.

## One change that would flip my rating
Nothing would flip it down — this is a 5★ fair-play puzzle. To flip it *up* (impossible): a second read revealing even tighter seeding.
