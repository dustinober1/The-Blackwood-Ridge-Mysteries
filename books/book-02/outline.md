# Outline

## Structure
8 chapters, 3-act shape. Single POV (Callie Thorne, third-person limited).
Planned-series Book 2 — central conflict fully resolved (Lauren recovered, Wells
exposed); covert series hook only (Eli's apprenticeship seed, Callie's unchanged
pariah state). No cliffhanger. Average ~3,125 words per chapter, matching Book 1's
template for series consistency. Seasonal clock: late spring, the garden-club
show approaching — everything blooming as a thirty-two-year-old grave surfaces.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — The Estate Lot
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,000
- **Beat:** Late spring. Callie has reclaimed the shop, the tea, the glass — but
  the reclamation is fresh and quiet, and the town flinches from her on the
  square (post-Book 1 pariah state, established through small snapshot moments:
  a shopkeeper who doesn't quite meet her eye, a customer who leaves too
  quickly). A delivery from the Ridgeway Auction House: a lot from the Dabney
  estate. Eli Townsend handled it; he flags one book to her — "I thought you'd
  want to see this one." It's the botanical. Callie opens it and recognizes the
  hand instantly: the small, precise, steady blue-black hand from Book 1's
  ending — the unknown hand she'd been meaning to come back to. Now she learns
  whose hand it was: Marguerite Dabney, retired English teacher, dead of cancer
  last week. Callie begins to read the margins and feels the first pull of the
  narrowing — and under the quiet of the reclaimed shop, the small clean thing
  she'd almost forgotten: curiosity.
- **Purpose:** Establish the pariah state and the untested reclamation. Deliver
  the keystone document. Re-engage the franchise signature (the decoding pull).
  Seed Eli's noticing (apprenticeship origin). Plant Marguerite as a presence to
  be known through her hand.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Process the estate lot; assess the books.
  - *Private want:* To feel useful again — the reclaimed quiet has been empty,
    and she hasn't admitted it.
  - *Opposition:* The town's cold politeness (she is feared/resented, not just
    known). Her own reluctance to be pulled back in.
  - *Turn:* Recognition of the hand — the unknown hand has a name now, and its
    owner is dead.
  - *Cost:* The quiet she'd reclaimed is broken; she's curious again, and
    curiosity has cost her before.
  - *Aftertaste:* The pen pressure fading in the final plates — a dying woman's
    race against herself.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Eli:* Eli flags the book, careful, a little awed. **Subtext:** he
    wants to have helped; he's testing whether his noticing counts. He asks a
    tentative question about how she knew it mattered — the apprenticeship seed.
    Callie answers more fully than she would have six months ago. Power: she is
    becoming someone he learns from; neither names it.
  - *Callie & a townsperson (snapshot):* Polite, brief, eyes averted.
    **Subtext:** she is the woman who pulled the thread; they have not forgiven
    her for it.
- **Memory / callback use:** The decoding pull / the narrowing (franchise
  signature, first deployment). Bergamot tea (does it taste right yet? — almost;
  she notices the "almost"). The Foxed Page back room (triple-weighted space).
  Eleanor's magnifying glass (now Callie's chosen tool, but Eleanor intrusions
  remain — a smell, a gesture). The "small clean thing: curiosity" — direct
  echo of Book 1's final line, to bind the opening.

### Chapter 2 — Floriography
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,000
- **Beat:** Callie goes to the Dabney house to read Marguerite through what she
  grew. The garden is legendary — and it is a second cipher: flowers planted by
  meaning (a floriography bed), not by color. Rosemary at the kitchen door
  (remembrance). Callie walks it the way she walks margins and begins to
  understand the woman who built this. At the garden she meets Doris Coalter,
  who is tending the beds in limbo with the garden club, and Mae Hartwell, who
  knew Marguerite well. Through them Callie learns the shape of what the cipher
  points at: Lauren Coalter, seventeen, vanished the summer of 1994 after a
  lakeside party. Presumed runaway. Never found. Doris never believed it. The
  cipher is not about a fresh death — it's about a thirty-two-year-old absence.
- **Purpose:** Introduce Doris (emotional heart) and re-introduce Mae (strained,
  knocking now). Establish the garden-as-text motif (new signature image-logic).
  Pivot the mystery from "fresh crime" to "cold case encoded by a dying woman."
  Plant the floriographic key concept.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Understand Marguerite — whose hand she's reading.
  - *Private want:* To know the woman who trusted her, even posthumously. To
    deserve the bet Marguerite made.
  - *Opposition:* Doris's wariness (Callie is a young woman inserting herself
    into an old wound; Doris's first instinct is to protect the wound). Mae's
    strained sadness (she sees where this is going).
  - *Turn:* The mystery is three decades cold, not fresh — and it points at a
    beloved dead girl, not a victim Callie can save.
  - *Cost:* The case becomes emotionally heavier; there is no living victim to
    rescue, only a dead girl and a mother who waited.
  - *Aftertaste:* Rosemary at the kitchen door — remembrance — and the weight
    of thirty-two years.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Doris:* Doris is dry, precise, lets silences sit. **Subtext:** she
    is measuring whether Callie is serious or a vulture; she has waited too long
    to hand the wound to someone careless. Power: Doris holds the gate to
    Lauren's memory; Callie needs her permission and knows it.
  - *Callie & Mae:* Mae's warmth is dimmed, careful; she knocks now
    metaphorically. **Subtext:** she understands Marguerite (the careful silence)
    and fears where this leads; her plea to be careful is softer than Book 1's
    plea to stop, but it's there. Forbidden topic between them: the Book 1
    fracture. Neither names it.
- **Memory / callback use:** The garden-as-text (new motif). Mae's lavender
  (armor). Doris's half-finished scarf (introduce — the endless knitting since
  1994). Lauren's graduation photo on a shelf (the absent girl). Marguerite's
  smell — rosemary, and the faint medicinal sharpness of her last months (felt
  in the garden she left).

### Chapter 3 — The Open Palm
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Beat:** Callie begins serious decoding of the floriographic cipher. She works
  in the back room, the magnifying glass over the plates — each flower's
  Victorian meaning clicking toward words (rosemary/remembrance, aconite/hatred,
  etc.), the dates and names forming a skeleton, the wider "not quite English"
  passages yielding slowly to Marguerite's teaching shorthand. She needs context
  on Lauren's world and goes to Blackwood Ridge High — where she meets Wells
  Ambler, the principal, for the first time. He is warm, steady, remembered-name
  warm; he speaks in encouraging questions that are really boundaries ("How can
  I help you with this?"). He loved Lauren, he says; he was her boyfriend; he
  never got over her running. His hands are always open, palms shown — and
  Callie, who reads hands, registers that the openness is deliberate, held, never
  accidental. Cross, meanwhile, tells Callie plainly to drop it: Marguerite died
  naturally, the case is thirty-two years cold, and reopening it will only tear
  the town further. The town can't take a second wound.
- **Purpose:** Introduce the antagonist (Wells) and make the reader feel his
  genuine warmth — he must not read as a villain yet. Establish the cipher as a
  real, decodable puzzle (the decoding pleasure). Set Cross's resistant stance
  (still procedural, the handshake not yet partnership).
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Crack the cipher; gather context on Lauren.
  - *Private want:* To find the edges of Marguerite's accusation — what,
    exactly, did the careful woman suspect?
  - *Opposition:* Wells's social warmth (a cage made of charm). Cross's
    procedural refusal. The cipher's deliberate resistance — Marguerite encoded
    for a reader, but not an easy one.
  - *Turn:* Wells's open palm — Callie reads the held-too-long openness and
    files it. Not suspicion yet; a notation. The first data point against a man
    everyone loves.
  - *Cost:* She has begun to look at a beloved man sideways, and she knows what
    that cost Gus Ralston's victims — and the town — in Book 1.
  - *Aftertaste:* The open palm, held a half-second too long. And the cipher's
    first clear phrase: a date, and a place by water.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Wells:* Warm baritone, remembered name, encouraging questions.
    **Subtext:** he is measuring what she knows and whether she's a threat; his
    warmth is real and is also a perimeter. Power imbalance: he is the town's
    pillar; she is its pariah. She does not yet know she's reading the killer;
    the reader doesn't either — but the open palm seeds it.
  - *Callie & Cross:* Clipped, direct, "leave it alone." **Subtext:** the
    handshake lingers (grudging respect) but the resistance is firm and partly
    personal — he likes Wells, leans on him; he doesn't want to believe where
    this points. Forbidden topic: that Cross himself would rather not look.
- **Memory / callback use:** The cipher-decoding moment (franchise signature,
  adapted for floriography — color of a plate, name beside it, meaning clicking
  into a word, sentence snapping short). The open palm (Wells's tell — mirror of
  Gus's "hands tell" from Book 1: over-control, not tremor). Rain on windows
  (safety inverting — Callie works alone in the back room as pressure builds).
  Cross's back-of-the-neck rub (canon — surfaces when he's out of his depth).

### Chapter 4 — The Wet Hemline
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Beat:** The cipher yields its heart: Marguerite's memory of the night Lauren
  vanished. Through the decoded passages Callie "sees" what Marguerite saw —
  Wells returning alone from Cedar Lake, wet to the knees, lying about where he'd
  been. Marguerite never had proof, only a teacher's certainty and a wet
  hemline; she told no one because the Amblers were respectable and she was
  terrified she was wrong. The wet hemline becomes the book's recurring image —
  introduced obliquely here, sharpening each time it returns. Callie now
  understands the shape of the accusation: not "Wells killed Lauren" (Marguerite
  couldn't be sure) but "Wells lied about being there, and I saw why." Wells,
  sensing threat, begins to press socially — not violence, never violence, but
  the town's own desire not to know. He leans through a council member (archive
  access friction), through parents (quiet word about Callie's "crusade"), and
  most cruelly through Doris's trust (a visit to Doris that Callie witnesses,
  where he is gentle and steady and Doris leans on him). Doris, hearing Callie's
  direction of inquiry, pulls back — protective of the wound, and of the man
  she's let become family.
- **Purpose:** Deliver the book's central evidentiary problem and emotional
  crux: an accusation from the grave that is only a record of a lie, not proof
  of a killing. Make Wells's method (social pressure, the town's complicity)
  legible. Deepen Doris's bond with Wells (the cruelest cover). Introduce the
  wet hemline as load-bearing motif.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Decode the cipher's central memory; confirm what Marguerite
    knew.
  - *Private want:* To be certain — to turn suspicion into proof, the way she
    did with Gus. But Marguerite refused certainty, and Callie must sit with
    that.
  - *Opposition:* Wells's social cage tightening. Doris's withdrawal. The
    cipher's deliberate restraint (Marguerite will not say "he killed her"; she
    only records what she saw). Callie's own doubt — what if Marguerite was
    wrong?
  - *Turn:* The wet hemline — clear, physical, specific. And Wells visiting
    Doris, gentle, the groceries on the table: the penance that is also the
    cover.
  - *Cost:* Doris turns from her. The town closes a rank. And Callie carries a
    dead woman's suspicion that she cannot yet make into a case.
  - *Aftertaste:* The wet hemline, and the groceries on Doris's table.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Wells (a second meeting):* His warmth thins — sentences shorter,
    cleaner, the charm dropping out. **Subtext:** he has decided she is a real
    threat; the practiced openness becomes effort. He uses her name. Power: he
    is testing whether social pressure will make her stop. She holds.
  - *Callie & Doris:* Doris, dry and short, withdraws permission. **Subtext:**
    she is protecting Lauren's memory from a version of the story she can't
    survive — and protecting Wells, whom she needs to have loved Lauren.
    Forbidden topic: that Doris let Lauren go to that party.
- **Memory / callback use:** The wet hemline (core motif — first full
  deployment). The open palm (Wells's tell deepening). Doris's half-finished
  scarf (stops when she's shaken — the knitting halts here). The grocery bag on
  Doris's table (Wells's penance/cover). Callie reading hands (canon) — she is
  now reading Wells against himself.

### Chapter 5 — The Language of Flowers
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,200
- **Beat:** MIDPOINT. Callie cracks the floriographic key fully — the whole
  cipher opens. The book yields not an accusation of murder but Marguerite's
  complete record: what she saw (the wet hemline), what she feared (the dock,
  the lake), and — crucially — where she believed Lauren was (the old dock at
  Blackwater Cove, Cedar Lake). The moral weight lands: this is a careful
  woman's thirty-two-year-old suspicion, aimed from the grave at the town's most
  beloved man, and it is not certainty. Callie must decide whether a dead
  woman's recorded suspicion is enough to demand a lake search that will tear
  the town apart — again, by her hand. Mae's small redemptive beat lands here:
  recognizing herself in Marguerite (the careful silence), Mae gives Callie one
  piece she'd been holding — a memory of Marguerite's last weeks, a remark, a
  visit — something that confirms Marguerite meant the cipher for Callie and
  tips Mae toward, finally, speaking a little earlier than she did with the
  Hartwell secret. Callie takes the cipher to Cross.
- **Purpose:** Midpoint turn — the investigation shifts from "decoding" to "what
  do I do with it." Land the theme (the cost of speaking too late; what do we
  owe the dead when their truth burns the living?). Give Mae her load-bearing
  micro-arc. Move toward Cross with the case.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Crack the cipher completely; decide what to do with it.
  - *Private want:* To be told it's enough — that she can hand it over and be
    absolved of the decision. (She won't be.)
  - *Opposition:* The cipher is suspicion, not proof. Cross needs physical
    evidence. The town will not want the lake dragged. Her own exhaustion —
    does she have the right to wound them again?
  - *Turn:* Mae's piece — the small act of speaking earlier — tips Callie from
    doubt to decision. She takes it to Cross.
  - *Cost:* She has chosen, again, to pull the thread. The town's second wound
    is now, in some sense, her choice.
  - *Aftertaste:* The full cipher laid out — flowers speaking what a careful
    woman couldn't — and the quiet of having decided.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Mae:* Mae's warmth, carefully extended; she offers the piece
    without being asked. **Subtext:** Mae is choosing, in a small way, not to be
    Marguerite — not to wait too long. The fracture doesn't heal, but it stops
    calcifying. Power: Mae releases Callie from the plea to stop, without
    withdrawing her fear.
  - *Callie & Cross:* She lays the cipher out. He is unmoved — it's handwriting,
    not evidence. **Subtext:** he doesn't want to believe; he likes Wells; the
    procedural refusal is also personal shield. But he listens longer than he
    would have in Book 1. The door to partnership is not opened; it's shown to
    exist.
- **Memory / callback use:** The full floriographic cipher (the keystone's
  payoff — flowers encoding a name, a night, a place). Marguerite's pen pressure
  fading (the dying woman's race — felt as Callie reads the final plate). Mae's
  lavender and the Hartwell Bible on the parlor shelf (callback to Mae's own
  silence). Bergamot tea — Callie drinks it after deciding; almost right, not
  yet.

### Chapter 6 — The Closing Ranks
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,000
- **Beat:** The town closes ranks. Word that Callie is pushing to reopen the
  Coalter case has spread, and the post-Book 1 wariness curdles into active
  resistance: the council applies archive friction, parents murmur, the garden
  club is split between women who remember Lauren and women who revere Wells.
  Wells, cornered socially, fights with everything he has — not violence, but
  charm, reputation, and the social fabric: a quiet campaign to frame Callie as
  a woman who wounded the town once and can't stop. Doris, pressed from both
  sides, pulls furthest away — she cannot bear that the man who brought her
  groceries for thirty years might be what Callie says. The cost of truth is
  made visceral: Callie is isolated, useful to no one, the pariah state at its
  worst. In the back room, alone, she nearly stops. Eli finds her — a small
  scene — and asks his real question about archival work, and the ordinary
  human contact steadies her. She does not stop. Cross, meanwhile, quietly and
  without warmth, begins the paperwork for a lake search — not because Callie
  convinced him, but because the cipher's location detail is specific enough to
  justify it, and because (he would not admit this) Mae said a word to him.
- **Purpose:** The "all is lost / dark night" beat. Make the pariah cost
  viscerally felt (slot requirement: she gains no goodwill). Let Wells's method
  (social complicity) be the antagonist's real weapon. Land Eli's apprenticeship
  seed as the small human thing that keeps Callie going. Move Cross incrementally
  (he acts, but without warmth — useful, not allied).
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Survive the town closing ranks; keep the case alive.
  - *Private want:* To be allowed to stop — to have someone tell her it's okay
    to let the dead keep their secret. No one does.
  - *Opposition:* The entire social weight of the town. Doris's withdrawal.
    Wells's charm offensive. Her own exhaustion and the empty reclaimed quiet.
  - *Turn:* Eli's question — the small, ordinary act of a scared kid standing
    straighter — and the realization that she is not entirely alone. And Cross,
    offstage, filing the search paperwork.
  - *Cost:* She is more isolated than at the start. The reclamation has not
    become belonging.
  - *Aftertaste:* The quiet of having chosen, again, to go on. And Eli's
  question — how did she know the book mattered? — echoing.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Eli:* He asks his real question; she answers. **Subtext:** he is
    becoming someone who notices, and she is becoming someone who teaches, and
    neither names it. The apprenticeship seed, planted plainly. Power: a rare
    scene of low-stakes human connection in a book short on it.
  - *Callie & Doris (brief, painful):* Doris shuts the door, gently.
    **Subtext:** Doris is protecting the version of her life she can survive;
    the friendship with Wells is load-bearing for her, and she cannot lose it
    on Callie's say-so.
- **Memory / callback use:** The town-as-chorus (snapshot glimpses of
  townspeople — post-Book 1 wariness now active resentment). The open palm /
  Wells's hands (his tell under pressure — the openness held too long, now
  visibly effortful). Bergamot tea (does it taste right yet? — almost, not
  quite; the reclamation holds but does not deepen). The Foxed Page back room as
  fortress/tomb/workspace (Callie nearly stops here, alone).

### Chapter 7 — Blackwater Cove
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,400
- **Beat:** CLIMAX. The search of Blackwater Cove. Cross, procedural and grim,
  runs it properly — and Callie is allowed to observe from the perimeter, not as
  partner but as the person whose work pointed here. The old dock is gone; the
  cove is deep, dark, silty. They find Lauren. The recovery is quiet, not
  sensational — bones in the silt, a graduation ring, thirty-two years of water.
  The physical evidence moves Cross at last: this is no longer a pariah's
  obsession, it's a body. Wells is brought in. The confrontation is not a
  confession-scene cliché — it is a man, finally seen, asking (without quite
  saying it) whether thirty-two years of decency cancels one buried night. The
  book's moral question, put to him directly: he is not a monster; he is a
  frightened boy who built a good man over a grave, and the goodness was real,
  and the grave was real, and neither cancels the other. He does not confess
  cleanly. He does not deny. He breaks, precisely, along the line between the
  man he became and the boy on the dock. Cross arrests him — quietly, without
  satisfaction.
- **Purpose:** Climax — physical evidence resolves the procedural thread; the
  confrontation lands the theme. Honor the cozy register (no gore, no
  melodrama) while delivering real weight. Give Cross his moment of duty-over-
  friendship. Let Wells be human to the last.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Find Lauren; give Cross what he needs.
  - *Private want:* To be done — to hand it over and let the town decide. And,
    beneath that, to know she was right (Marguerite was not wrong; she was not
    cowardly; she was just too late).
  - *Opposition:* The lake itself (deep, cold, may yield nothing). Cross's
    reluctance to believe until he must. Wells's final, human refusal to be
    only a villain.
  - *Turn:* Lauren is there. The ring. The body makes it real. And Wells, seen,
  breaks along the seam.
  - *Cost:* A girl is dead and has been in the dark for thirty-two years. A man
    who was, in part, genuinely good is undone. The town will fracture again.
  - *Aftertaste:* The dark water closing over the recovery. And the strange,
    terrible relief on Wells's face — the relief of finally being seen, mixed
    with the terror of what being seen means.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Cross (perimeter of the search):* Few words. **Subtext:** he
    acknowledges, without warmth, that she was right; she acknowledges, without
    triumph, that it cost him. The handshake from Book 1 is not repeated; its
    absence is felt.
  - *Callie & Wells (the confrontation):* He does not confess to her; he speaks
    to the room, or to himself, or to Lauren. **Subtext:** he wants to be told
    the thirty-two years counted. Callie cannot tell him that. She can only
    listen. Forbidden topic between them: whether she, who exhumes truth
    regardless of cost, is really different from him, who buried it. The book
    lets the question stand.
- **Memory / callback use:** Blackwater Cove / the old dock (the grave — felt
  as heavy even in daylight). The wet hemline (payoff — the image that has
  accrued across the book resolves into a body in the silt). The graduation ring
  (a small specific object — Lauren made real, finally). The open palm (Wells's
  tell collapses — the practiced openness can no longer be held). Lauren's
  graduation photo (echo — the photo on Doris's mantel, the ring on the hand).
  Cross's back-of-the-neck rub (canon — he is out of his depth, and it's
  personal this time).

### Chapter 8 — What the Garden Knew
- **POV:** Callie Thorne
- **Word target:** 3,000
- **Beat:** RESOLUTION. The aftermath, muted per cozy register — legal process
  begins offstage; the town absorbs the second wound. The hardest scene is
  Doris: Callie goes to her, and Doris learns that the man who brought her
  groceries for thirty years killed her daughter. The devastation is not
  catharsis — it is the unfreezing of thirty-two years of frozen grief, which
  will hurt more, not less, now that it can move. Doris does not thank Callie.
  She turns Lauren's graduation photo to face the wall, then back again, unable
  to decide. Callie tends the Dabney garden one last time — the garden club is
  dissolving it, but she reads it as a final cipher, a woman's life spelled in
  flowers, and she understands Marguerite fully now: a careful woman who waited
  too long, and whose only courage was to encode her cowardice for a stranger.
  Callie sees her own reflection in that — and chooses, quietly, not to become
  it. Eli, in the shop, asks his last question; Callie answers, and the
  apprenticeship seed is planted though neither knows it. Mae comes to the door
  — knocks — and they share a quiet cup; the fracture has not healed, but it
  has stopped calcifying. The bergamot tea: almost right. Not quite. Callie
  stands in the shop she has not lost, holding the glass that is now her chosen
  tool, and the book closes on a small forward step, not a victory — useful, not
  welcome; the reclamation holds, the belonging does not.
- **Purpose:** Resolution that honors the pariah slot (no rehabilitation, no
  goodwill earned). Land the theme (the cost of speaking too late; what we owe
  the dead). Give Doris the book's most painful beat. Close the garden-as-text
  motif. Plant Eli's seed and leave the Callie–Mae fracture exactly where it
  should be. Covert series hook only (Eli's trajectory, Callie's standing) — no
  cliffhanger.
- **Scene pressure:**
  - *Surface goal:* Deliver the truth to Doris; close the case.
  - *Private want:* To be told she did the right thing. No one tells her. She
    must hold it without confirmation.
  - *Opposition:* Doris's devastation (the truth has cost her the man she'd let
    become family). The town's second wound. Her own question — was it worth
    it? The book refuses a clean answer.
  - *Turn:* Doris turns the photo to the wall, then back. The grief can move
    now; it will hurt more. And Callie, in the garden, chooses not to become
    Marguerite.
  - *Cost:* Doris's friendship with Wells, which was real to Doris, is
    revealed as a thirty-year lie. The town is wounded again. Callie remains a
    pariah.
  - *Aftertaste:* The bergamot tea — almost right, not yet. The garden, being
    dissolved. The glass in her hand. A small forward step into a life that is
    not yet belonging, but is no longer just survival.
- **Dialogue subtext map:**
  - *Callie & Doris:* The hardest conversation in the book. Doris does not
    thank her; she is not capable of gratitude yet, only of the first raw edge
    of unfrozen grief. **Subtext:** she will, eventually, be glad to know. She
    is not glad now. Forbidden topic: that Doris let Wells in because the house
    was quiet. The photo turned to the wall and back is the whole scene.
  - *Callie & Mae:* A quiet cup; few words. **Subtext:** Mae's small act in Ch
    5 has not healed the fracture, but it has changed its texture — from
    calcification to something that might, slowly, soften. The knock at the
    door is the gesture.
  - *Callie & Eli:* His last question; her answer. **Subtext:** the
    apprenticeship origin — one boy, one book, one question. Neither knows what
    it will become (the series does).
- **Memory / callback use:** The garden-as-text (final reading — Marguerite's
  life in flowers, closed). Bergamot tea compass (the book's answer: almost,
  not yet — belonging is not this book's gift to give). Eleanor's magnifying
  glass (Callie's chosen tool, held at the close — the glass is hers now, not
  Eleanor's ghost, but she holds it differently). Lauren's graduation photo
  (turned to the wall and back — the absent girl's presence in Doris's life).
  The Foxed Page back room (sanctuary reclaimed, but changed). Rosemary
  (remembrance — the last smell of Marguerite, in the garden being dissolved).
