# Packaging — The Challenger

## Package role

This file is the Book 3 package-guidance companion to the draft retailer listing at `../publish/listing.md`. It prepares cover/package decisions and series-continuity checks only. It does **not** publish, upload, or generate a final retail file.

## Positioning for this novella

### Subcategory and also-bought neighborhood

**Primary path:** Kindle eBooks > Mystery, Thriller & Suspense > Cozy Mysteries  
**Secondary paths:** Mystery > Amateur Sleuth, Mystery > Women Sleuths, bookish / archival mystery readers

Book 3 sits in the **atmospheric archival cozy** lane established by Books 1 and 2. Book 1 used annotated marginalia as the franchise hook. Book 2 rotated that hook into botanical floriography. Book 3 returns to documents and archives, but with a new mechanic: stylometry, handwriting attribution, missing folios, and a trusted keeper who makes the record smooth around a hole.

### Differentiators

1. **Evidence-vs.-meaning mystery device** — Callie's intuitive reading eye is challenged by Dr. Vivian Larter's statistical authorship-attribution work. The book's commercial hook is not only a murder but a challenge to the sleuth's gift.
2. **The second hand** — red/blue stylometric overlays, handwriting comparison, and missing known-hand samples give the case a distinctive visual language.
3. **The shelf-gap image** — a physical absence becomes evidence. The pale rectangle on a shelf is subtle, elegant, and unusually specific to this book.
4. **Series reputation shift** — Callie remains not fully welcome, but she becomes grudgingly useful to Cross and the town. Packaging should signal continuity without suggesting a soft reset into a lighter cozy register.

## Cover guidance

### Series-template inheritance

> **Locked from Books 1 and 2:** elegant gold serif title treatment, deep-plum / charcoal atmospheric palette, warm parchment or aged-ivory paper tones, consistent series-label placement, consistent author-name placement, and the brass magnifying glass as Callie's recurring visual anchor.

For Book 3, rotate the focal object from a botanical book to an archival evidence table / reading-room scene. The design should still read instantly as a sibling to *The Annotated Murder* and *The Botanical Confession*.

### Visual concepts

**Concept A: The Red and Blue Overlay (recommended)**  
An oak archive table under a warm reading lamp. On the table: a stack of old county folios, a printout with red and blue stylometric annotations over faded handwriting, an antique brass magnifying glass resting near the edge of the overlay, and a single pale marble bookend in the background. A slim empty space between two folios hints at the missing sample without making the cover too literal. Warm lamplight, deep-plum shadow, tarnished-gold type area, and crisp aged-ivory paper. This is the strongest sibling to Books 1 and 2: bookish, elegant, atmospheric, and rooted in the franchise's reading-as-detection engine.

**Concept B: The Reading Room Lamp**  
A County Historical Society reading room after hours: oak tables, brass card-catalog handles, a single desk lamp forming a warm pool of light, one marble bookend on the front desk, and dark windows beyond. A brass magnifying glass lies on a green access folder. This concept gives stronger setting and murder-scene atmosphere while avoiding gore, weapons-in-action, or thriller signaling.

**Concept C: The Shelf Gap**  
Close view of an archive shelf: old folios labeled forty, forty-one, forty-two, then forty-five, with a narrow pale rectangle of wood where two folios used to sit. A brass magnifying glass catches a thin line of lamplight in the foreground. Elegant, minimalist, and conceptually precise. Risk: it may be too subtle at thumbnail unless the title and series template carry the mystery signal.

### Color palette

**Aged Jewel Tones — Archival Variant (recommended; inherits series palette)**

- Deep plum / shadow: `#5B2C6F` — locked from series template
- Tarnished gold / brass / title: `#B8860B` — locked from series template
- Aged ivory / paper: `#FFFFF0` — locked from series template
- Archive green / access folder: `#4B6043`
- Annotation blue: `#3F5F8A`
- Annotation red: `#8F3A3A`
- Marble gray: `#D0D0C8`
- Bergamot brown / tea stain: `#8B4513` — optional series callback

### Typography

- **Title font:** Same elegant serif family as Books 1 and 2. Use textured gold or warm cream/gold treatment. Keep the title large and clear; *The Challenger* is short enough to read strongly at thumbnail.
- **Author name:** Same size, color, and placement as previous series covers. Use warm gold/cream rather than stark white.
- **Series label:** `The Blackwood Ridge Mysteries · Book 3` in the exact same position and approximate size as Books 1 and 2.
- **Hierarchy:** Series label (smallest) → Title (largest) → Author name (second).
- **Thumbnail rule:** Title must remain legible at 150 px width; small red/blue overlays should read as texture, not clutter.

### Composition

- **Upper third:** Title and series label in the locked series position.
- **Middle third:** Archival table, reading-room lamp, red/blue overlay, shelf gap, or marble bookend.
- **Lower third:** Author name, optional small archival accent such as a tiny file-label strip or magnifying-glass reflection.
- **Negative space:** Preserve the breathing room used by Books 1 and 2. Avoid filling every inch with documents.
- **Series motif:** The brass magnifying glass must appear somewhere on the cover.

### Avoid

- Any dead body, blood, striking motion, or threatening figure. The murder is off-page and should remain packaged as cozy / amateur-sleuth, not thriller.
- Modern software-looking UI or sci-fi data screens. Stylometry should appear as printed academic overlays, not holograms.
- Generic courthouse, generic detective badge, or police-procedural imagery. Callie's signature is reading records, not law enforcement.
- Overly cheerful pastel palettes. The book has grief, professional self-doubt, institutional betrayal, and restrained closure.
- Breaking the series template. Book 3 must look like the next volume in the same line.
