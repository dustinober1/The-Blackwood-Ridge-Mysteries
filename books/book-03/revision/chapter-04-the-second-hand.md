# Revision Notes — Chapter 4, The Second Hand

2026-06-30 (revision round 1, v0.3.1, eighteen-pass)

## Integrated pass summary

Chapter 4 now functions as the controlled mid-investigation turn where Callie proves the second hand exists by using Larter's instrumental method and her own glass together. The revision preserves Callie's competence while making her blind spot emotionally precise: the Book 1 fraud verdict holds, but her attribution of the whole Wren corpus was incomplete.

The draft's largest issue was structural overreach. It moved too far into Chapter 5 territory by treating the shelf-list gap, receipt question, and Beech-shaped suspicion as nearly proven. The revised chapter confirms the second hand, frames folios 43–44 as the missing comparative-sample problem, and ends on the receipt search as the next action. The actual removal verdict remains reserved for Chapter 5.

## Conflicts reconciled

- **Second-hand confirmation vs. Ch 5 midpoint:** Ch 4 keeps the confirmation of the second hand but defers deliberate-removal proof to the receipts and shelf evidence in Ch 5.
- **Eli usefulness vs. over-competence:** Eli notices and asks; Callie interprets and directs. This keeps him useful without making him implausibly brilliant or surfacing later-series manipulation.
- **Stylometry vs. Callie's eye:** Neither wins. Larter's method points to what the eye missed; Callie's glass gives meaning and texture to the same pattern.
- **Beech suspicion vs. mystery pacing:** Ch 4 allows Beech to be institutionally relevant because he controls the records, but does not make him visibly villainous or solve the case.

- Ch 5's new value is the paper-trail test: if folios 43–44 never appear in receipts, the absence becomes a removal.

- Maintain Beech's mild helpfulness until the evidence forces sharper focus.
