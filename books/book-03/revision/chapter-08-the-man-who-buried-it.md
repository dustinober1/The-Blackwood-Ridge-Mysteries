# Revision Notes — Chapter 8, The Man Who Buried It

2026-06-30 (revision round 1, v0.3.1, eighteen-pass)

## Integrated pass summary

Chapter 8 now functions as the Book 3 climax, confession, arrest, and emotional resolution. The revised chapter stages the final proof as a controlled live reading in the County Historical Society reading room, where Cross can witness the shelf evidence, access/control records, Beech family clue, bookend absence, Larter's red/blue method, and Harlan Beech's hands converging in one room.

The draft had the right broad skeleton, but over-sweetened the closure, left Eli too close to the answer, restored the murder weapon awkwardly to the desk, and answered the forbidden question too directly. The revision cools the confrontation, keeps Cross present as witness rather than partner, keeps Eli outside the core proof, makes Document X actionable through Beech's confession and consent, plants the future absence invisibly, and closes on changed/grudging utility rather than welcome.

## Conflicts reconciled

- **Confession vs. info dump:** Beech's confession is broken into direct answers under pressure and tied to objects already on-page: 1994 recataloging, family letter, folios, bookend, Larter's refusal.
- **Cross action vs. no formal partnership:** Cross acts after witnessing enough in a form he can stand behind; his final language remains "useful" and file-centered, not partner language.
- **Callie vindication vs. Larter's correctness:** Callie is incomplete but not discredited; Larter was right about the second hand; the founding fraud finding stands.
- **Thematic answer vs. slogan:** Callie's difference from Beech is shown through the choice to bring the box into light, not stated as an easy moral victory.
- **Series seed vs. cliffhanger:** Document Y is only a quiet rhythm-stutter Callie attributes to ordinary archival damage/loss. It creates no on-page cliffhanger and is not connected to Harlan or Eli.
