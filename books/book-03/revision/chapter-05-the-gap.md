# Revision Notes — Chapter 5, The Gap

2026-06-30 (revision round 1, v0.3.1, eighteen-pass)

## Integrated pass summary

Chapter 5 now functions as the midpoint inversion: the case turns from *was Callie's reading incomplete?* to *the archive was deliberately narrowed before she ever read it*. The revised chapter opens on Eli retrieving the movement records, uses the receipt box to test whether folios 43–44 were officially moved/lost/transferred/disposed, and then lets the physical shelf-gap become the proof that absence has become removal.

The draft's largest issue was over-solving. It made Callie name Harlan Beech as killer too early, made him melodramatically dangerous before the still-hands chapter had earned it, and treated several later-climax inferences (kept folios, family hand, murder conclusion) as already certain. The revision preserves the midpoint proof but limits what it proves: folios 43–44 existed, sat on the shelf long enough to leave a trace, and left the collection by no official paper trail. Harlan comes into sharper focus as the keeper/access point through his labels, transfer signature, and curatorial control, but Callie is ready to read him rather than accuse him.

## Conflicts reconciled

- **Midpoint proof vs. over-solving:** Receipts + shelf now prove unofficial removal, not the whole murder solution.
- **Callie vindication vs. Larter's correctness:** "I was edited" preserves both truths: Larter was right about the second hand; Callie was reading a deliberately narrowed archive.
- **Beech focus vs. clue pacing:** Harlan's labels, signature, and access sharpen suspicion while his still-hands tell and full motive remain for later chapters.
- **Cross involvement vs. Book 3 slot:** Cross clears a temporary transfer and sends Deputy Bell, but does not deputize Callie or make the partnership formal.
- **Eli usefulness vs. manipulation risk:** Eli retrieves, carries, tallies, and asks grounded questions. He does not steer Callie toward hidden future-series knowledge.
