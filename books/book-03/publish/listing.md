## Book description

### Primary description

An academic challenge should have stayed academic. In Blackwood Ridge, it becomes murder.

After exposing two of the town's buried truths, Callie Thorne has earned a reputation no one quite knows what to do with. She is useful now. Not welcome. Then Dr. Vivian Larter arrives at The Foxed Page with a leather portfolio, a statistical model, and a problem Callie cannot dismiss: the Wren margin notes that made Callie's name may contain a second hand she failed to see.

Larter's evidence is fair, careful, and devastating. If she is right, Callie's most famous reading was incomplete—and somewhere in the County Historical Society archive, a missing comparative sample may prove who altered the record.

By morning, Larter is dead in the reading room.

There is no forced entry. No easy suspect. Only a polished marble bookend, a blank sign-out line, and an archive that has become too orderly around a hole.

To find the killer, Callie has to do the one thing more frightening than proving herself right: admit where she was wrong. With Sheriff Cross beginning to consult her despite himself and Eli learning to read the record beside her, Callie follows red-and-blue handwriting overlays, missing folios, and the careful hands of a keeper trusted by everyone in town.

Because some records are not destroyed. They are smoothed until the absence looks natural.

And Callie Thorne has never trusted a silence that neat.

**The Blackwood Ridge Mysteries, Book 3.** Each novella resolves its own case; the series arc deepens with every volume.
