# H01 — Book 3 Ch 2: After the Lecture (lines 149–258)
**Running impression:** 3/5 · **DNF risk:** low
## Gut reaction (in Nigel's voice)
The lecture scene works — Larter presents, the room turns on Callie, the man in the second row voices the town's mean relief (181–185). Cross's hat-on/hat-off signaling (161, 187, 229) is a clean behavioral tell. But the chapter ends with Larter dead on the reading-room floor (217–220) and the room "almost right" (225–227) — a phrase the prose loves. Rain starts/stops on cue (151, 205, 255). Mae's lavender-scented oracle turn begins (165–169).
## What worked (cite chapter + line numbers — begrudgingly, if at all)
- The town's mean relief (185–186): "They had not wanted her to be a liar. They had only wanted her to be fallible. It was a smaller thing, and a crueller one." One of the book's truer observations.
- Cross's hat language (161, 187, 229): consistent, readable, not explained.
- Beech's stillness during lecture (189): hands stop, then resume — the first tell.
- Larter's "I'd rather be wrong" (197): costs her something, accent climbs through — human moment.
- The room reading (227–228): Callie catalogs the near-rightness — cloth folded, bookend gone, overhead light wrong, slide left on. Procedural eye at work.
## What didn't (cite chapter + line numbers)
- Rain as mood punctuation: 151 (wipers), 205 (had not noticed it stop), 255 (had not noticed it stop again). Three beats in one chapter.
- Mae's lavender/thin sachet (165–166): "The asking-permission knock. Callie had not known, until this autumn, how much a knock could wound." Overwritten.
- Larter's death scene (217–220): "Someone had tried to make the scene look like a working room... The copper smell was under the lemon oil and the old paper, and it was the only honest thing in the room." The "honest thing" line is a thumb on the scale.
- Callie's dream/haunting (205): "Once, in the gray hour before dawn, she woke certain she had heard her mother turn a page..." Dead mother in the hall — cheap atmosphere.
- "Almost right" room description (225–226): the phrase appears again as structural description.
