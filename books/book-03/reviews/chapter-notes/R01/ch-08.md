# R01 — Book 3 Ch 8: The Man Who Buried It (lines 1389–1679)
**Running impression:** 5/5 · **DNF risk:** none

## Gut reaction (in Dottie's voice)
Oh, what a satisfying ending! Beech's confession — so honest, so tragic in its quiet way. "I thought of my name first. Then the town. Then the record. In that order" (8:1525). And that arrest scene, Cross placing his hand on Beech's shoulder, choosing duty over thirty years of friendship (8:1565–1567). Then THAT final image — Linda Parrish not looking away, holding the wave "one second longer than manners required" (8:1665). My, my. Justice served, hope kindled, and Callie back at her page. Tea and truth, together again.

## What worked
* **Beech's confession:** "I made the notes say what they needed to say" (8:1531) — tragic, honest, earned.
* **The reading-room confrontation:** Everything happens in the curated space where it belongs (8:1445–1533).
* **Cross's thaw:** "For the file, your reading was useful" (8:1617) — "useful" is progress from "useful, not welcome."
* **Linda Parrish's thaw:** "She lifted two fingers, held them there one second longer" (8:1665) — the town's smallest crack.
* **Callie's final reflection:** "When the box was asked for, she brought it into the light" (8:1639) — growth, not perfection.

## What didn't
* Beech's confession comes fairly quickly once cornered (8:1509–1531) — Dottie likes tidy justice but this is almost too neat.
* The "not yet" of the town's welcome (8:1667–1669) means the warmth is still coming — bittersweet.
