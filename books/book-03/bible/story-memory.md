# Book Story Memory

> Seeded from `series-bible/story-memory.md` with only the motifs, wounds,
> promises, and callbacks relevant to Book 3's slot ("The Challenger"). The
> per-book `/novella-bible` skill extends this with book-specific detail;
> series canon must not be contradicted.

## Book 3 obligations (from series-outline.md)

- **Callie's Book 1 findings are tested.** A visiting academic arrives to study
  Blackwood Ridge's archives, bringing a document that contradicts Callie's
  Book 1 findings. The central question: were Callie's conclusions right?
- **The magnifying glass is used on someone else's terms for the first time.**
  The academic is a peer who respects Callie's work enough to challenge it —
  external validation of her methods, not just her results.
- **The academic's challenge reveals a gap in Callie's Book 1 work** — a
  document she missed. (First puppet-master seed: she does not yet know the
  document was removed by someone who did not want her to find it.)
- **Reputation shift:** pariah → grudging utility. For the first time, someone
  *sought Callie out* — the academic, who respected her Book 1 work. By book's
  end, Cross asks for her input for the first time (not formally, but the door
  is open).

## Motifs and callbacks (series canon — preserve)

- **Eleanor's brass magnifying glass:** Now Callie's chosen tool (reclaimed end
  of Book 2: "Not Eleanor's glass. Hers"). Book 3 tests the glass on someone
  else's terms — a peer who brings their own evidentiary standards.
- **Margin notes as voices:** Handwriting carries the living presence of the
  writer. Book 3 introduces the academic's hand — a new, scholarly hand — and
  the document that contradicts Callie's findings. Callie reads hands the way
  she reads faces (canon from Book 1 Ch1).
- **The decoding moment (signature move):** Slow to a crawl over letter shapes,
  pen pressure, the ghost of the hand; then meaning clicks and the sentence
  snaps short. Book 3 must develop, not merely repeat.
- **Bergamot tea compass:** Book 2 resolved to "almost right. Not quite... the
  almost-right was its own answer." Book 3 tests whether the reclamation deepens
  under the new pressure of being *challenged* rather than merely resented.
- **Rain on windows:** Safety / inside-while-world-stays-outside. Inverts under
  threat.
- **The Foxed Page back room:** Eleanor's workspace, where Eleanor died,
  Callie's fortress. Triple weight (sanctuary/tomb/workspace).
- **The two-register pivot:** A warm scene undercut by a dread detail; the prose
  changes temperature without announcing it.
- **Eleanor intrusions:** Unprompted sensory memories of Eleanor revealing how
  close to the surface Callie's grief still is.
- **The town as chorus:** Brief, specific snapshots of townspeople establishing
  the community Callie is risking. Post-Book 2, the pariah edge is "the
  almost-relief that doesn't arrive."

## Emotional scars entering Book 3

- **Callie:** Pariah state confirmed as resolved (Book 2) — "useful, not
  welcome." Glass fully reclaimed. Made an interior vow against Marguerite's
  silence (*not like this*). New wound/seed standing unanswered: the forbidden
  question — "*Are you so different, Callie Thorne, from the man who buried
  it?*" Book 3 will pressure this: being challenged on her *methods'* soundness
  tests whether the exhuming-truth vocation is itself the thing she can't
  defend.
- **Callie:** Unprocessed grief for Eleanor — loosened, not resolved. Trigger:
  finding someone alone in a room (flashes to Eleanor).
- **Mae:** Decades of complicit silence, broken in Book 1; micro-redemptive arc
  in Book 2 (spoke "earlier than Marguerite"). Fracture with Callie "might
  soften"; has not healed. Track whether the calcification continues to soften
  or sets again.

## Book 3 new canon (extend, do not contradict series canon)

- **Dr. Vivian Larter's hand (new hand — establish distinctly):** Forensic
  linguist's hand — small, precise, even, blue-black ink; the hand of someone
  trained to be legible. Callie reads it as *disciplined*, the hand of a woman
  who taught herself not to be misread. Distinct from Wren (cramped cipher),
  Pike (eager downhill slant), Eleanor (looping familiar voice), Marguerite
  (small precise steady blue-black). Larter's precision could be mistaken for
  Marguerite's at a glance — note the family resemblance and the difference
  (Larter's is trained-flat hill-country; Marguerite's was natural economy).
- **The stylometry-vs-intuition tension (the book's method axis):** Larter's
  instrument measures *how* a person writes (function-word frequency,
  syntactic habit); Callie reads *why* they wrote it (pressure, rhythm,
  intention). The book tests whether attribution alone is justice or whether
  motive is the real evidence. By the end Callie needs *both* — the
  instrument names the gap, the intuition names the keeper. Do not let one
  method "win"; let them be incomplete alone.
- **The County Historical Society reading room (new location, new weight):**
  Callie's *opponent's* archive — the room curated by the man who tampered
  with the record. The marble bookends (polished weekly) become the murder
  weapon. The signature log (canon mechanic) is evidence. The room smells of
  lemon oil, old paper, and cold air — distinct from the bergamot-and-leather
  of The Foxed Page and the cold-stone of the town archives.

### Document X and Document Y (the engine + the seed)

- **Document X (the engine — solved in-book):** A known handwriting sample of
  the Beech clerk (Harlan's ancestor), removed from the County archive by
  Harlan ~1994 to hide the 1850s amendment to the Wren notes. Its *absence*
  is the gap Larter's stylometry detects. Callie identifies the gap, infers
  X's existence, and — reading Harlan's hands (the stillness tell) and the
  record (the empty shelf-space where X was) — names Harlan as the keeper who
  buried. X is recovered or its removal proven; Harlan confesses. **Book-
  contained.**
- **Chapter 5 midpoint lock:** Movement receipts show no official trail for
  folios 43–44 (no transfer, conservation, disposal, loss, or consultation);
  the shelf itself shows that two folios once sat there and were removed. This
  proves unofficial removal and turns the investigation from self-doubt to
  tampered-record hunt. It does **not** yet recover X, reveal Document Y, prove
  motive, formalize Cross/Callie, or make Beech a melodramatic villain; Callie
  is ready to read the keeper, not formally accuse him.
- **Document Y (the seed — invisible, unresolved):** A second, related record
  Callie infers while reconstructing the gap — a record that, if it existed,
  would change her attribution *further* than X alone explains. It is not in
  the archive, and Harlan did not take it. Callie attributes its absence to
  loss/time. She does **not** know it was deliberately removed. (Future Eli
  thread — do not name Eli, do not surface the steering in Book 3.) Plant
  Y's absence as a quiet detail the reader might later remember; do not
  underline it.

### Book 3 character memory additions

- **Callie:** The cerebral dread of being proven wrong — a new interior
  register (see voice-dna.md). The moment she reads Larter's output and
  cannot refute it. The moment she realizes Harlan is the answer to her
  forbidden question (*yes, she is different — because she exhumes*). The
  glass used on someone else's evidentiary terms for the first time.
- **Larter (before death):** The ambushed smell of woodsmoke and lard (her
  grandmother's kitchen) hitting her somewhere in Blackwood Ridge — a sensory
  memory that humanizes her before she is a body. Let Callie like her.
- **Harlan:** The peppermint he takes for the stomach that has troubled him
  since the night he removed X. The brass bookend he polishes every Tuesday —
  the murder weapon in plain sight. His Book 2 seed ("slowed at her door,
  then swept past"; "looked up for the first time in weeks") reads in
  retrospect as guilt. Do not retcon; let it pay off.
- **Cross:** The hat-withholding tell is the warming signal to track. If he
  removes his hat indoors in Callie's presence, the door is opening. His
  blind spot this book: he trusts Harlan — the institutional figure who
  hands him coffee.
