# Premise

## Logline

When a visiting forensic linguist arrives in Blackwood Ridge with a
stylometric study that attributes the Wren margin notes to a different hand
than Callie Thorne named in Book 1 — and is found bludgeoned in the County
Historical Society the morning after presenting her challenge — Callie must
defend the interpretation that made her a pariah, catch a killer, and reckon
with the possibility that the one thing giving her exile any meaning might be
wrong.

## Theme

The cost of being right. Book 1 made Callie a pariah for being right; Book 2
confirmed the town would rather she had looked away. Now someone respected
Callie's work enough to engage it seriously — and died for it. The book tests
whether being proven has a body count, and whether conviction in one's gift can
survive a credible, respectful expert saying *you got the most important thing
wrong*. Underneath: evidence vs. meaning — a forensic linguist can prove *who*
wrote a document; Callie reads *why* they wrote it. Is attribution alone
justice, or is motive the real evidence?

## Central conflict

Are Callie's Book 1 methods sound — and can she prove it before the challenge
discredits the one finding that gives her pariah-state any meaning, and before
Larter's killer escapes into the gap the challenge exposed?

## Protagonist (sketch)

**Callie Thorne** — antiquarian bookshop owner (The Foxed Page), archivist,
reader of marginalia. Desire: to know her Book 1 reading of the Wren notes
wasn't a self-serving mistake. Flaw/wound: the forbidden question from Book 2
— *are you so different, Callie Thorne, from the man who buried it?* — sharpened
rather than softened by being challenged on her methods. (Full character work in
`/novella-bible`.)

## Opposing force

Two-layered. **Primary:** the killer — Harlan Beech, County Historical Society
head, a keeper of records who buried one to protect a family secret and will
kill to keep the attribution question closed. **Secondary/abstract:** the
challenge itself — a credible modern method (stylometry) that Callie's intuitive
historical eye has no instrumental answer for. She must defend a reading she
*feels* in her hands against a model that *measures*.

## Stakes

- **Public:** If Larter's challenge stands unchallenged, Callie's Book 1
  attribution — the interpretive core of the founding-fraud case — is publicly
  wrong, and the exile she's endured for it loses its meaning.
- **Personal:** Larter is murdered *because* she took Callie's work seriously.
  If Callie can't find the killer, the lesson hardens: anyone who engages her
  gift gets hurt. The cost of being right becomes the cost of being *read*.
- **Relational:** Cross's door is open for the first time. If Callie's methods
  can't hold under scrutiny here, the door closes for good.

## Setting & tone

Blackwood Ridge, Virginia. Late autumn, weeks after Book 2's close — the town
wounded a second time, the garden beds dissolved, bergamot tea still reading
"almost right. Not quite." Primary new location: the **County Historical
Society** archive and reading room (distinct from the town archives destroyed
in Book 4) — wood-paneled, brass-handled, the smell of old paper and lemon
oil, marble bookends on every table. Returning: The Foxed Page (Callie's shop
and apartment), Hartwell House, the Sheriff's Office.

Tone: literary, atmospheric, gothic-tinged chill under a cozy sensory blanket
(old leather, rain, hot tea, shop stacks). This book adds a new register — the
**cerebral dread of being proven wrong** — layered under the usual comfort. The
magnifying glass is turned, for the first time, on someone else's terms.

## Comps

Series comps hold:
1. **The Secret, Book & Scone Society** (Ellery Adams) — tonal cousin
2. **The Thursday Murder Club** (Richard Osman) — moral-weight cousin
3. **The Cracked Spine** (Paige Shelton) — archival-sleuthing blueprint

Per-book accent: the "credible expert challenges the sleuth's foundational
case" dynamic echoes **The Eighth Detective** (McEwan) and the academic-murder
texture of **The Woman in the Library** (Goodall).

## The two-document mechanic (the engine + the seed)

- **Document X (solved in-book):** a comparative handwriting sample that should
  have been in the archive and would have let Callie test her attribution.
  Removed years ago by Harlan Beech to protect a family secret. Larter's
  stylometric challenge threatens to make its absence visible; Beech kills her.
  Callie recovers the absence, identifies Beech, closes the case.
- **Document Y (invisible seed):** a second, related record whose existence
  Callie infers while reconstructing the gap — but it isn't in the archive, and
  Beech didn't take it. Callie assumes it was lost to time. (Future Eli thread.)
  She does not yet know it was deliberately removed. Seed planted clean.
