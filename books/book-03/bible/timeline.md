# Series Timeline

## Deep backstory

- **1780s:** Blackwood Ridge founded. Original land grants issued to settling families, including the Hartwells. The Ralstons arrive later and establish themselves through trade and milling.
- **1840s:** Silas Ralston orchestrates a land fraud. Jacob Wren, the town surveyor, is coerced into falsified survey records but secretly encodes the true measurements in margin notes. Wren flees town.
- **1843:** Reverend Thomas Hartwell discovers the fraud and is killed — staged riding accident through Ralston influence over the magistrate.
- **1850s (Book 3 layer — new canon):** The County clerk (Harlan Beech's ancestor) amends the Wren margin notes — inserts a secondary hand imitating Wren's cipher, adding material that muddies attribution and indirectly shields the Ralstons. A small, plausible tampering that bends the record. The clerk's known handwriting sample (Document X) would later prove it.
- **~1994 (Book 3 layer):** Harlan Beech, then a young curator, finds Document X in the County Historical Society stacks, recognizes his ancestor's amendment, and removes X before anyone (especially the Thorne women) can compare it. He tells himself it is preservation. This is the founding act of his adult life.
- **1840s–present:** The fraud calcifies into accepted history. The Ralstons become the town's benefactors. Oral fragments survive in whispers.
- **~1985:** Eleanor Thorne establishes The Foxed Page antiquarian bookshop.
- **~1994:** Eleanor gives Callie the brass magnifying glass and teaches her to read margin notes.
- **~2024:** Eleanor Thorne dies. Callie inherits the shop and the grief.

## Per-book events

### Book 1 — The Annotated Murder

- **~6 months before:** Wendell Pike begins decoding Jacob Wren's margin notes, approaches Callie for collaboration.
- **~3 months before:** Pike discovers the keystone document and begins a manuscript.
- **~2 weeks before:** Gus Ralston (Silas's descendant) learns of the manuscript and decides to kill Pike.
- **Night of the auction:** Pike poisoned at the Ridgeway Auction House. Body found in back office. Eli Townsend saw Gus leaving the back corridor.
- **Days 2–5:** Callie begins decoding Wren's margin notes in the archives. Cross focuses on Eli. Mae asks Callie to stop. Gus pressures Callie through the town council (archive access revoked).
- **Days 6–8:** Callie finds the murder accusation in the 1843 ledger (S.R. iussit). Historical scope revealed: stolen land, murdered minister, bought magistrate.
- **Days 9–11:** Callie finds the keystone at the Historical Society. Discovers Gus and Pike in the visitor register. Eli reveals he saw Gus in the back corridor. Gus confronts Callie at the shop at night — veiled death threat.
- **Day 12:** Callie takes the full evidence to Cross. Eli's alibi (paperback), the register entries, the witness statement, and the exit log. Cross calls it in.
- **Day 13:** Gus arrested. Scandal breaks. Town fractures. Eli cleared. Cross shakes Callie's hand. Mae comes to the door with soup.
- **After:** Callie reclaims the shop, the tea, the glass. Finds a botanical book with an unknown hand's marginalia — hook into Book 2.

### Book 2 — The Botanical Confession

**1994 (deep backstory, surfaced this book):**
- **June 1994:** Lauren Coalter (17) vanishes the night of the Blackwood Ridge High graduation party at Cedar Lake. Her boyfriend Wells Ambler (19) shoves her on the old dock at Blackwater Cove during an argument; she strikes a piling and does not surface. He does not go in. He walks home wet to the knees, walks in the middle of the road to avoid dust, tells the Carmody family he was at the Harlow barn. The town writes it "runaway"; the lake drag finds nothing; the case is closed. Marguerite Dabney (English teacher) sees him come home wet from her kitchen window and never speaks it aloud.
- **1994–2026:** Wells becomes a teacher, returns to Blackwood Ridge, becomes principal, and brings groceries to Doris Coalter every Thursday for thirty years — penance and cover in the same gesture. Marguerite annotates a 19th-century botanical ("Flora's Lexicon of the Virginias," 1873) with a floriographic cipher, page by page, over decades, and plants her garden by the same meaning. She waits.

**Late May–June 2026 (book present):**
- **Late May:** Marguerite Dabney dies of cancer. The Dabney estate lot arrives at The Foxed Page (Eli Townsend flags the provenance — the hand in the botanical is Marguerite's). Callie picks up Eleanor's glass for the first time since December and reads.
- **Days 1–2:** Callie walks the Dabney garden, meets Doris Coalter (Lauren's mother), learns the 1994 disappearance. Doris commands: find the truth in the book and bring it to her first.
- **Days 3–5:** Callie decodes the floriographic key (rosemary/remembrance, rue/regret, aconite/hatred, violet/faithfulness, yarrow/war, meadowsweet/stifling-of-a-feeling). Recognizes "Ambler" beside yarrow. Visits Wells Ambler at the school; files the open-palm tell under *deliberate*. Cross warns her off.
- **Days 6–8:** The meadowsweet plate yields the first witness statement — "He was wet to the knees... he had come back alone, and Lauren had not come home." Harriet Vailes recalls the archive key (Wells's social-pressure method via the town council). Callie witnesses Wells delivering groceries to Doris; Doris withdraws her kitchen ("find the rest of it before you come to my kitchen again").
- **Days 9–11 (midpoint):** The cipher reveals itself as a coordinate map. Water-lily plate → the old dock at Blackwater Cove, the deep. Damask-rose plate → "Say it." Mae confesses Marguerite's October visit (the dying woman's bet on Callie) and confirms the book was aimed at her. Callie takes the decoded file to Cross. He reads the whole thing, then: "I'll think about it." Harlan Beech looks up for the first time in weeks.
- **Days 12–14 (all-is-lost):** The town closes ranks — Linda Parrish confrontation, formal archive-closure letter, garden club split, Doris's final withdrawal via Mae, Harlan slowing at the door then sweeping past. Callie nearly stops. Eli arrives with the last delivery and asks "Could you teach me? To see that?" — the apprenticeship seed. Callie says "I can try" and does not stop.
- **Day 15 (climax):** Cross calls at 7 p.m.: dive team, 6 a.m., "wear boots." Mae had walked into his office and said one word. At Blackwater Cove: the graduation ring surfaces at 08:14, Lauren's remains at 09:00. Wells is called in, sees Callie, confesses to her ("She was leaving... I pushed... I didn't go in"), asks "Tell me it counted." Callie: "You built a good life, Wells. You built it over her. Both of those things are real." Cross arrests him. Callie signs the provenance statement. "She didn't run. She never ran."
- **After (resolution):** Doris turns Lauren's photo to the wall, then back — the grief unfreezing ("moving water is heavier than still water"); "You can go now, Callie." The garden club votes to dissolve the Dabney floriography beds; cuttings dispersed into scattered yards. Callie's interior vow: *not like this* — she refuses Marguerite's silence. Eli's Saturday apprenticeship formalized. Mae's three-knock visit ("earlier than Marguerite"). Bergamot compass: "almost right. Not quite... the almost-right was its own answer." Glass: "Not Eleanor's glass. Hers." "that was enough. For now, that was enough."

### Book 3 — The Challenger

**1994 (deep backstory, surfaced this book):**
- **~1994:** Harlan Beech, young County Historical Society curator, finds Document X (his ancestor's known hand) beside the Wren margin notes, recognizes the 1850s amendment, and removes X from the County archive. Founding act of his concealment. (Contemporaneous with Eleanor giving Callie the glass — the same year the keepers diverged: one buried, one taught.)

**Pre-book (recent backstory):**
- **Months before:** Dr. Vivian Larter (forensic linguist, UVA) runs a stylometric re-attribution of the Wren margin notes corpus. Her model attributes portions of the notes to a different hand than Callie named in Book 1 — flagging the Beech amendment without naming it. She requests access to the County Historical Society archive and contacts Callie, whose Book 1 work sent her into the subfield.
- **Weeks before:** Larter's access is approved through Harlan Beech (who is helpful, courteous, and watches her every move). She is unaware of the threat. A small lecture is scheduled to present her preliminary findings.

**Story-present (chronological skeleton — beats to be detailed in outline):**
- **Opening:** Larter arrives in Blackwood Ridge — the first person to seek Callie out. The challenge is presented: the Wren notes may not be a single hand. Callie cannot instrumentally answer it.
- **The lecture (inciting catalyst):** Larter presents her stylometric re-attribution publicly. The gap in Callie's Book 1 attribution becomes visible to the town. Harlan Beech is in the room, polishing bookends.
- **The murder:** Larter found bludgeoned in the County Historical Society reading room the morning after the lecture. Marble bookend. No forced entry — she knew her killer, or her killer had a key.
- **Investigation:** Callie must defend her Book 1 reading (does the attribution hold?) while hunting the killer. Cross consults her — the door opens (not formally). Eli assists with research. The missing Document X is the key: its absence is the gap Larter's stylometry detected.
- **Midpoint:** The realization that X was *removed* — not lost. The record was tampered with. The keeper is the destroyer.
- **All-is-lost:** Callie's methods can identify the gap but not, instrumentally, the keeper. Cross's trust in Beech is the obstacle. The forbidden question sharpens: *is she different from the man who buried it?*
- **Climax:** Callie reads Beech's hands (the stillness tell) and the record (the empty space where X was) and names him. Confession — not rage, relief. The thirty-year weight set down. Cross arrests the man who handed him coffee.
- **Resolution:** The Beech amendment becomes public; the Book 1 fraud verdict holds (the amendment muddied attribution, it did not invent the fraud). Callie's reputation shifts — pariah to "grudging utility." Cross removes his hat in her presence (the door opens). Document Y — a second missing record Callie infers but cannot find — is noted and attributed to loss. (Puppet-master seed, invisible.)
- **After:** Bergamot compass reading to be set in outline. The forbidden question receives its answer: *yes, she is different — because she chooses to exhume.* The cost-of-being-right theme lands: the woman who respected her work died for it.

## Ending state after Book 1

- **Callie:** Reclaimed her life from grief's hold. Still owns The Foxed Page. Relationships strained but intact. Reputation shifted from quiet shopkeeper to the woman who pulled the thread. Holding a new mystery (the botanical book).
- **Mae:** Confessed her knowledge of the family secret. Relationship with Callie strained but not broken. Brought soup. The door is open but different now.
- **Cross:** Shook Callie's hand. Grudging respect established. Will be less dismissive of her in future — but still procedural.
- **Eli:** Cleared. Standing taller. Callie earned his trust. Potential ally.
- **Gus Ralston:** Imprisoned. The Ralston name disgraced. Historical Society closed. The town's power structure permanently altered.
- **The town:** Wounded. Knows the truth about its foundations. Some grateful, some resentful. The old-money hierarchy cracked.

## Ending state after Book 2

- **Callie:** Pariah state confirmed as resolved Book-2 condition — "useful, not welcome," neither rehabilitated nor crushed. Reclamation held under pressure but did not deepen into belonging ("almost right. Not yet" → "almost-right was its own answer"). Glass fully reclaimed ("Not Eleanor's glass. Hers"). Began teaching Eli on Saturdays (apprenticeship seed sprouted). Made an interior vow against Marguerite's silence: *not like this* — she will speak even when the speaking only burns. New wound/seed: the forbidden question ("Are you so different from the man who buried it?") — left standing, unanswered, a recurring Callie-vow/wound to track.
- **Mae:** Chose to speak — eight months late but "earlier than Marguerite." Confessed Marguerite's October visit and the bet-on-Callie. Walked into Cross's office and said one word that moved him to act. Now knocks three times (asking permission) where she used to walk in. The fracture with Callie might soften; it has not healed. "Earlier than Marguerite" is her Book-2 gift and absolution-without-absolution.
- **Cross:** Read the whole file before dismissing it (first time — new since Book 1). Withheld his hat indoors (still resistant; the handshake's warmth still not extended). Arrested his friend Wells Ambler — "the mechanical procedure of a man doing his duty over the ruins of a friendship." Did not shake Callie's hand at the close. Jaw-set is his tell when a friend's name enters the room (not the neck-rub). Still a procedural guardrail; the door between him and Callie exists but is not opened.
- **Eli:** Apprenticeship formalized — Saturdays at The Foxed Page, "I'll show you what I can." Standing straighter; steady hand (new); collar-touching habit persists but is becoming a badge, not an apology. His father's "call when you've figured things out" remains unpaid. Series arc toward "de facto apprentice" now seeded and committed.
- **Wells Ambler:** Arrested; legal process begun off-page ("somewhere past the county line"). Confession on record. Thirty-two years of penance-and-cover ended. Not a series-returning character.
- **Doris Coalter:** Grief unfrozen — "moving water is heavier than still water." Turned Lauren's graduation photo to the wall, then back. "You can go now, Callie" — severance as the only gift she can give. Scarf unfinished; knitting stopped since the news. Not a series-returning character but the book's hardest emotional debt.
- **Marguerite Dabney:** Dead; vindicated. "She had been right — not wrong, not a coward, just too late." Garden dissolved; cuttings dispersed into a hundred yards where rosemary will grow without spelling anything anymore. The cipher's death is a mercy.
- **The botanical book ("Flora's Lexicon of the Virginias," 1873):** Now at the sheriff's office as evidence; provenance chain starts with Callie. Established series keystone document — its floriographic mappings are canon.
- **The town:** Wounded a second time. A second pillar (after Gus Ralston) fallen to Callie's hand in a year. The garden club fractured then voted to dissolve the Dabney beds. Harlan Beech looked up once, then swept past. The pariah-state's hardest edge is the almost-relief that doesn't arrive.
