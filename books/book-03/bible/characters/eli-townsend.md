# Eli Townsend

## Desire (external)
Survive. Keep his head down, keep his job at the auction house, and earn enough
respect that people stop looking at him like a transient.

## Need (internal)
To believe he belongs somewhere. He's been running from failure his whole short
life — dropped out of college, disappointed his parents, landed in Blackwood
Ridge because it was the last bus stop before he ran out of money.

## Wound / flaw
He's a young man with no roots and no safety net. In a town built on family
names and generational ties, he's invisible by default — which makes him the
perfect scapegoat. His anxiety and social awkwardness read as guilt to people
who don't know him.

## Contradictions
- He's innocent and he acts guilty — not because he did anything, but because
  he's spent his whole life expecting to be blamed for things.
- He wants to be seen, and he makes himself as small as possible.

## Private shame / fear
That everyone is right about him. That he's a screwup, a drifter, a
disappointment. That he'll never belong anywhere.

## Social mask
Polite, nervous, eager to please. He over-explains everything because silence
feels dangerous. He smiles too much and agrees too quickly — the behavior of
someone who learned early that compliance is survival.

## Voice & mannerisms
Fast, slightly breathless speech. He apologizes reflexively — "sorry, sorry,
I just—" starts sentences and abandons them. He fidgets: hoodie strings, keys,
anything in his pockets. Under the anxiety, there's a sharp mind that surfaces
when he forgets to be scared.

## Body under stress
Shuts down. Goes quiet, goes still, pulls into himself. When Sheriff Cross
interrogates him, he looks exactly like a guilty man — sweating, trembling,
unable to make eye contact — because that's what systemic anxiety looks like
to someone who doesn't recognize it.

## Misreadings
He thinks his argument with Pike makes him look guilty, so he lies about it —
which makes him look guiltier. He doesn't understand that Callie is trying to
help him because no one in authority has ever been on his side.

## Appearance
Early twenties. Lean, restless, dresses to disappear — hoodies, plain tees,
worn sneakers. He looks younger than he is, which makes people underestimate
or pity him. Perpetual dark circles from bad sleep.

## Arc
Functions as the ticking clock: if Callie doesn't solve the case, Eli goes down
for a murder he didn't commit. His arc is small but essential — Callie sees in
him the kind of overlooked person she's spent her career trying to protect.
Saving him is what makes the cost of truth bearable, at least partially.

## Relationships
- **Callie Thorne:** Near-stranger at the start. She comes to the auction
  house; he helps her find things. He's wary; she's distant. But when she
  realizes he's the scapegoat, protecting him becomes personal — he's an
  ordinary person about to be erased by history, which is exactly what she's
  dedicated her life to preventing.
- **Sheriff Cross:** Cross's prime suspect. Eli's anxiety in interrogation
  rooms cements Cross's certainty. This is the near-miss that drives the
  stakes.
- **Dr. Wendell Pike:** Eli had a public argument with him days before the
  murder — Pike accused him of misfiling a rare document. It was minor, but it
  becomes circumstantial evidence.

## Recurring memories / images
- The bus ride into Blackwood Ridge — the last stop, the first place that
  felt like it might stick.
- His father's voice on the phone: "Call when you've figured things out."
  He never calls.

## Things they refuse to say
- Why he really dropped out of college.
- That he was in the building the night of the murder — not to kill anyone, but
  to return a book he'd borrowed without asking, which looks devastating on
  paper.

## Notes
- Eli is the human cost of the sheriff's tunnel vision. His function is to make
  the stakes concrete: a real, vulnerable person goes to prison if Callie fails.
- Keep his page time modest — he doesn't need a full subplot, just enough
  presence that the reader fears for him.

## Appearances

### Book 2 — The Botanical Confession
- **State entering:** Cleared; standing taller; introducing himself to customers instead of hiding. Trust with Callie earned. Apprenticeship seed planted in bible but not yet dramatized.
- **Arc:** Flagged the Dabney estate lot's provenance to Callie (he identified Marguerite's hand in three more volumes — his noticing is now an active investigative asset, not just background). Over the book he practices "not apologizing for taking up space" — cut his hair, wears shirts with buttons (touches the collar as a badge of the new habit). Asked Callie the book's key human question: "How do you know? When you look at a page — at someone's handwriting — how do you know if it means something?" Received her real answer (not the professional one). Then: "Could you teach me? To see that?" — the apprenticeship seed, formally asked. Callie: "I can try." At the close, formalized: "You come in on Saturdays. I'll show you what I can."
- **State exiting:** Apprenticeship **committed** — Saturdays at The Foxed Page. Standing straighter; his hand is now steady (new — "the steadiness was new," the anxiety-to-confidence evolution's next step). Collar-touching persists but reads as a badge, not an apology. "He was past the *thank you*, into the place where a person just nods because the words have been said and the rest is the work."
- **Father's debt:** Still unpaid. "Call when you've figured things out" — he never calls. Book 2 does not resolve this; track for later books.
- **Series arc seed promoted:** Origin of Eli's archival apprenticeship is now canon-delivered. Track for Book 5's "de facto apprentice" payoff (per series outline).
